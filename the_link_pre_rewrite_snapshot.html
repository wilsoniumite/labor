The Link: Wages, Machines, and What Remains

A task model of automation, job rents, and the fiscal system, with U.S. measurements, 1962–2025
Stella Wilson
KTH Royal Institute of Technology · Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken (SEB) · thmwi@kth.se
Working draft — August 2026. Views are the author's own and do not represent those of KTH or SEB. Written in collaboration with Claude (Anthropic); see the AI-use note on the final page.
Abstract. Wages are prices on a link: the non-uniformity of human capability relative to machines across tasks. A worker's pay decomposes into three strata — an exit floor, a premium set by the level and slope of the capability schedule at the marginal task, and job-specific rents (union premia, licenses, efficiency wages) on top. Automation demolishes the strata from the top down: profit-directed adoption targets the best-protected jobs first (following Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2026), then flattens the schedule, then crosses the participation margin. When machines can make machines, the value released does not vanish but migrates to rents on non-produced factors — land, sites, energy. The real wage in machine-made goods is bounded below by absolute human productivity; the collapse operates through the land-priced components of consumption — immiseration through rent, not through the wage. Because roughly seven-tenths of U.S. consumption and revenue are wage-linked, the fiscal system is a tributary of the collapsing base; conditional transfers fail in regime-specific ways, while an unconditional transfer funded by rents on non-produced factors is the unique instrument — up to the corner's own equivalence of consumption and rent bases — whose base cannot contract and whose transfer moves no margin. The coverage ratio of that remedy — site rents against a universal subsistence floor — is measured here at one-third and rising. At the corner the pair restores the second welfare theorem with instruments that exist: the corner's character is institutional, not technological. The paper states the model with proofs, reports U.S. measurements for 1962–2025, and lists thirteen falsifiable predictions.

1. The claim

Machines and humans can each do most tasks; what differs is by how much. Where the difference is large and uneven across tasks, human labor is complementary to machines and commands a premium far above subsistence. Where machine capability becomes uniform relative to ours, that premium evaporates — not because humans got worse at anything, but because the gap closed. This paper takes one sentence seriously and follows it to the end: your wage is the rental price of a machine, times your edge over it, at the task where your edge is smallest among the tasks humans still hold. Not need, not effort, not merit: replacement cost at the margin.

Three consequences follow, each developed below with proofs and then measured. First, a demolition schedule: automation removes the layers of a paycheck in a fixed order — job rents first (and the better protected the job, the earlier the robot comes for it), the capability premium second, participation last — with distinct observable signatures at each stage, one of which has already been found in U.S. data. Second, a conservation law: when machine-making itself automates, machine prices collapse to their land-and-energy content, so the value released by the demolition reattaches to non-produced factors; "rent dissipation" is really rent migration — with a corollary that answers the classic objection to immiseration stories: machine-made goods do get cheaper, so the squeeze arrives through the land-priced part of the budget, not the goods part. Third, a fiscal theorem: the tax-and-transfer state is plumbed almost entirely into the wage loop, so it thins as the loop does; in the limit, redistribution funded by taxes on wages becomes an exact shell game, while a uniform transfer funded by a tax on site rents is the one instrument that survives — not as a preference among options, but as the only column of the ledger with anything left in it. Two further results close the arc: a feasibility theorem prices the remedy — the U.S. coverage ratio of a fully rent-funded floor measures one-third today and climbs with automation itself — and the strongest objection, Baumol concentration on human-essential tasks, is granted its mechanism and priced: it forks on substitutability, binds only through a proof apparatus, and where it rescues the aggregate labor share it leaves the median wage to collapse anyway. A closing welfare theorem draws the moral: at the corner the land-tax-and-dividend pair restores the second welfare theorem with instruments that exist, so the corner's character — dystopia or inheritance — is a choice of tax base, not of technology.

Related work

The machinery is Acemoglu and Restrepo's task framework (2018, 2022), run with the new-task margin closed — Section 9 defends the closure — and their rent-dissipation mechanism (2026) transplanted onto the capability schedule. Korinek and Stiglitz (2019) posed the below-subsistence scenario this model resolves through exit; Korinek and Suh (2024) is the closest formal predecessor on wage collapse under transformative AI, differing here in where the collapse ends — at non-produced factors rather than capital accumulation — and in the conditionality contrast of Propositions 6 and 7. Susskind (2017) and Sachs and Kotlikoff (2012) model technological unemployment and immiseration through other channels; Moll, Rachel, and Restrepo (2022) route automation's gains to wealth-holders, where this model names the terminal claimant: land. Caselli and Manning (2019) prove real wages cannot fall in terms of machine-made goods — the objection Proposition 4 concedes and then answers through the land content of subsistence. Grossman and Rossi-Hansberg (2008) supply the trading-tasks lens; here the foreign wage enters as a second machine rental, so offshoring and automation are one demolition read at two heights. Zeira (1998) is the replacement ancestor; Autor (2015) the reinstatement-side counterpoint; Hémous and Olsen (2022) endogenize automation's direction — the engine the fortification remark leans on; Karabarbounis and Neiman (2014) document the decline's global reach. Aghion, Jones, and Jones (2019) supply the strongest stabilizer — Baumol concentration of expenditure on human-essential tasks — engaged in Section 9. George (1879) supplies the terminal instrument; Ricardo (1817), the margin logic the whole model runs on.

2. The model

Tasks. Producing the final good requires a continuum of tasks x ∈ [0,1], each in equal amount — a checklist where every box is ticked once per unit of output (Leontief aggregation over tasks). The good's unit cost is the sum of its tasks' unit costs.

Two ways to tick a box. One human-hour completes γL(x) units of task x; one machine-hour completes γM(x) units. The human edge is ρ(x) = γL(x)/γM(x). A machine-hour rents for c — taken as given until Section 5, where it becomes the second protagonist.

Wedges. Some jobs pay more than the market requires: union premia, licensing rents, efficiency wages paid because monitoring is hard. Following Acemoglu and Restrepo (2026), model these as a wedge µ(x) ≥ 1 that firms must pay on task x, with µ = 1 on a positive mass of tasks. Wedge jobs are rationed: firms hire into them only up to the point where the inflated wage equals marginal product, and workers queue for them — a convention that returns in Section 3: because the rationed jobs are full, the marginal participant is evaluated at the base wage.

People. N identical workers each supply one hour or exit to an outside option worth s — the woods, the family, the commons: the value of the best life available without selling labor. Note that s quietly presupposes access to some land — a debt Section 6 prices.

Land. Fixed in total, heterogeneous in quality, worst parcel's reservation rent zero. Idle until Section 5, decisive after.

Lemma 1 (Deflation). Labor holds task x iff µ(x)·w/γL(x) ≤ c/γM(x), i.e. iff w ≤ c·ρ̃(x), where ρ̃(x) = ρ(x)/µ(x). All allocation in the model runs on the wedge-deflated schedule ρ̃, not the raw one.

Proof. Divide both sides of the cost comparison by µ(x). ∎

Geometrically: a wedge is negative altitude. It drags a task down the effective schedule toward the waterline without changing how good humans actually are at it. For the threshold argument, relabel tasks so ρ̃ increases from left to right, and assume the relabeled schedule is continuous and strictly increasing where labor is employed; Figure 1 keeps the raw ordering so the pockets stay visible. Competitive firms assign each task to the cheaper input, so there is a threshold x*: machines take [0, x*), labor takes [x*, 1], and in equilibrium firms are exactly indifferent at the margin while everyone willing to work at the going wage is working or has exited.

Lemma 2 (Existence and uniqueness). With the relabeled ρ̃ continuous and strictly increasing where labor is employed, an equilibrium pair (w, x*) exists and is unique for any number of participants; on a flat stretch the wage is pinned and only the assignment of identical-cost tasks is indeterminate, with nothing riding on it.

Proof. Absorbing more participants requires labor to hold more tasks, so the market-clearing wage c·ρ̃(x*(n)) is continuous and strictly decreasing in the participant count; participation supply is a step at s. A strictly decreasing continuous curve crosses a step exactly once, with the exit resolution of Proposition 1(iii) handling the below-s branch; flat stretches pin the wage at c·ρ̄ and leave free only a measure-preserving relabeling of identical tasks. ∎

3. The wage is a price on the link

Proposition 1. (i) In any equilibrium using both inputs, the base wage is w = c·ρ̃(x*). (ii) The wage's sensitivity to human scarcity is governed by the slope of ρ̃ at x*: steep slope, responsive and insulated wage; flat schedule ρ̃ ≡ ρ̄, labor demand perfectly elastic at c·ρ̄ and the wage pinned there regardless of how many or few humans exist. (iii) No equilibrium wage sits below s while anyone works; if the clearing wage would fall below s, workers exit until the wage rises to s (possible only when the schedule has slope) or all have left.

Proof. (i) If w > c·ρ̃(x*), the marginal task and, by continuity, tasks just above it are cheaper by machine; firms drop labor there; the unemployed underbid; w falls. If w < c·ρ̃(x*), tasks just below x* strictly favor labor; firms demand more hours than exist; bidding raises w. Only equality is stable. (ii) To absorb more hours, labor must win more tasks, and a firm hands over a task only when the wage falls relative to that task's machine cost; so more participants push x* left and w down, in proportion to the slope. Flat schedule: no wage above c·ρ̄ employs anyone, every wage at c·ρ̄ employs everyone willing. (iii) is the participation condition applied to (i) and (ii). ∎

Definitions. The link is the pair (level, slope) = (ρ̃(x*), ρ̃′(x*)). The level sets the wage; the slope insulates it from scarcity. Call the schedule-has-slope case the linked regime and the flat case the corner regime — labor demand perfectly elastic at machine parity — with two sub-cases: corner-above (c·ρ̄ ≥ s — everyone works at a pinned wage) and corner-below (c·ρ̄ < s — rational exit). The corner-below case resolves the "wages below subsistence" scenario of Korinek and Stiglitz (2019) the only way it can be resolved: by leaving, not by dying — in practice, by becoming a dependent, because a mother can see value in her child that the economy cannot.

Running example. c = $10, s = $25. Task A: ρ = 1, µ = 1 (machines hold it). Task C: ρ = 4.5, µ = 1. Task B: ρ = 5, µ = 1.25 — objectively humans' best task, but wedged. Effective edges: ρ̃(B) = 4, ρ̃(C) = 4.5, so B is the effective marginal task and w = $40. The C-worker earns $40; the B-worker earns µ·w = $50. Now let AI improve machines uniformly by 25%: raw edges fall to ρ(B) = 4 and ρ(C) = 3.6. If nothing else changed, the new wage at the B-margin would be $32 — a 20% pay cut delivered entirely by someone else's machine getting better at your task, with no human getting worse at anything. One caveat carries its own proposition: this arithmetic is nominal — it holds the machine rental c fixed while machines improve, and machine progress also cheapens machine-made goods. Proposition 4 does the general-equilibrium bookkeeping and locates exactly which part of the cut is real.

History in one figure. Before the industrial era, machines scarcely existed; wages sat near s, pinned by land scarcity — Ricardo's version of the same margin logic, run through soil instead of silicon. The industrial revolution split capabilities violently: machines absurdly better at the physical, useless at the cognitive. The schedule became enormously dispersed and steep, the link tight, wages high and insulated — the historical anomaly we mistake for a law. Computing began flattening the simple-cognitive end. AI makes the flattening general, and Figure 2's annotation is the whole argument: the level falls and the slope falls, and they are separate injuries. Technology giveth, and technology taketh away.

Heterogeneity. Real people carry personal schedules and personal outside options. Every result below holds worker-type by worker-type; "heterogeneity lifts the mean above the floor" is the cross-sectional shadow of Proposition 1, and floor statements refer to the bottom of the support, not the average.

4. Demolition from the top

A paycheck now has three geological layers, and the model's central narrative is that automation removes them in order.

wage at task x = s + [c·ρ̃(x*) − s] + [µ(x) − 1]·c·ρ̃(x*)
(exit floor) + (link premium) + (wedge rent)

Proposition 2 (Targeting; Acemoglu–Restrepo 2026, transplanted). When an automation advance makes a set of tasks feasible for machines, the tasks actually flipped are those with the lowest ρ̃ among the feasible ones. Provided the advance is not biased toward low-wedge tasks, the flipped set is first-order stochastically the high-µ set: automation is targeted at the best-paid versions of each job. The proviso is load-bearing: a wedge whose institution can reach the feasible set itself — blocking adoption by law or contract rather than pricing labor — exits the theorem's domain; the fortification remark below tracks where it goes.

Proof. Adoption occurs where labor cost exceeds machine cost, µ(x)·w/γL ≥ c/γM, i.e. where ρ̃(x) ≤ w/c: the adoption region is a lower set in the effective schedule, and wedges are what push otherwise-high tasks into it. ∎

In the running example: after the 25% machine improvement, ρ̃(B) = 3.2 and ρ̃(C) = 3.6, so B floods before C — automation takes the task where humans are objectively better (4 > 3.6), because the wedge made it the cheapest conquest. The displaced worker loses twice, and differently: the $10 wedge rent vanishes with full incidence — a composition effect that moves no margin, so nobody shares it — while the base-wage decline is spread economy-wide through the margin. Acemoglu and Restrepo find exactly this asymmetry empirically: displacement effects propagate across groups; rent losses land undampened on the displaced.

Three corollaries, each a prediction:

Self-liquidation of labor-side protection. A wedge that operates through price raises its own task's automation priority. Union premia, licensing rents, and any legally mandated "human-made" price premium are self-undermining under sustained automation; the only protection that survives Proposition 2 is quantity exclusion — law removing a task from the automatable set entirely. The better protected the job, the earlier the robot comes for it.

The within-group signature. Because wedge jobs sit in the upper-middle of each group's internal wage distribution, stage one of the demolition compresses wages within exposed groups: losses concentrate in roughly the 70th–95th within-group percentiles, with the bottom (no rents to lose) and the very top (unautomatable rent jobs) moving only with the base wage (Figure 4). Acemoglu and Restrepo (2026) document precisely this U-shape for U.S. groups, 1980–2016 — a finding that contradicts the folk assumption that technology always widens inequality at every level of aggregation, and one this model predicts rather than accommodates.

The subsidy mirror. An in-work benefit is algebraically a wedge µ < 1 — labor accepting less at a task — producing over-employment where private rents produce under-employment. Private and public distortions are the two half-lines of a single µ-axis, with the competitive model at µ = 1; Section 6 returns to the public half.

The transition can be negative-sum. Wedges break the comforting theorem that automation, however brutal distributionally, always raises total output. A firm automating a $50 wedge job saves $50 minus the machine; society only frees what the worker produces elsewhere — $40 minus the machine. The $10 gap is a pure allocative loss, incurred every time Proposition 2's targeting fires. Acemoglu and Restrepo estimate that for the U.S., 1980–2016, this loss offset 60–90% of automation's productivity gains — possibly all of them. Privately profitable automation ran close to socially break-even, and perhaps negative-sum, before any of this model's later concerns arrive. The loss has a finite fuel tank — it is capped by the wedge stock, and once the wedge stratum is demolished the competitive envelope logic resumes — but "finite" here has already meant four decades.

Remark (fortification: what the demolition leaves standing). Proposition 2 governs wedges that price labor, and the distinction that matters is not who holds power but where the lever rests. A strike threat is priced leverage: its value is the cost of replacing the strikers, which is exactly what automation feasibility destroys — so bargained premia erode on the theorem's schedule, and history mostly shows the veto being sold rather than held (dockside mechanization agreements, print-room lifetime guarantees: buyouts that convert blocking power into ordinary transition rents). A statute is different. Scope-of-practice rules extended to software, an automation ban written into contract, a mandated human in the loop — quantity exclusions survive parity untouched, and under pressure protection actively converts from the first form into the second. The demolition is therefore a filter: it removes price wedges in ρ̃-order — the U-shape data — and leaves quantity wedges standing, so the surviving wedge stock distills toward legal form. Three consequences. Adoption at fortified tasks is punctuated: nothing moves until the accumulated gap clears the cost of confrontation, then the stratum goes at once. The finite fuel tank caps only the contestable stratum: a blocked task's forgone saving grows with every machine improvement, and the fortified sectors — sheltered, non-tradable, Baumol-side — grow their expenditure share as everything else cheapens, so the drag compounds twice. And the terminal wage column is blank only of competitive wages: a fortified wage is institutional rent in wage clothes — infra-marginal, taxable as rent, revocable as law — a column Proposition 3's corollary already holds open. (The manager deciding whether to automate management is the limiting case, wedge-holder and adoption-decider in one chair, disciplined only by entry and takeover — slowest where product markets are most sheltered.)

5. Where value goes when machines make machines

Proposition 3 (Value collapse). Let producing one unit of machine services require a units of machine services (a < 1: machines net-reproduce) and ℓ units of non-produced services — sites, energy, ore — renting at r. Free entry forces c = a·c + ℓ·r, hence

c = ℓr / (1 − a).

With time preference δ (building takes a period), c = ℓr(1+δ)/(1 − a(1+δ)), finite iff a(1+δ) < 1; the wedge δ is interest — the wage of waiting — competitive, real, and thin. Durability generalizes it: with wear at rate d the user-cost recursion gives c = ℓr(δ+d)/(1 − a(δ+d)) — the display is the one-period case d = 1 — so wear thickens the sliver as waiting does, and the destination is unchanged. An idea's reproduction requires approximately nothing of anything, so its competitive price is approximately zero; any positive price for an idea is institutional scarcity, chosen rather than natural — chosen, as Romer made canonical, because at marginal-cost pricing ideas with fixed creation costs are not created at all: the institutional scarcity is constitutive of the idea sector, not a gratuitous distortion. Which ideas earn rents, and for how long, remains a policy dial; and once an income floor exists, it is itself a second margin for funding creation.

Proof. Above recipe cost, entrants build machines with machines and undercut; below it, builders exit. The displayed equation is the zero-profit condition; solving it is one step. (With many machine types this is a matrix equation c = Ac + Lr solved through (I−A)−1; the scalar carries the full logic.) ∎

Corollary (the surviving-income taxonomy). In the corner regime, gross income decomposes exhaustively into rents on three kinds of scarcity — physical (land, energy, sites, water: permanent), institutional (whatever law elects to protect: IP, licenses — and by Section 4's self-liquidation result, only quantity-exclusions endure), and dynamic (transition rents while capacity builds: real, temporary, and "temporary" can mean decades for a nuclear plant) — plus thin interest. Wages have left the list.

Worked instance. Model weights are just numbers — an idea. Distillation pierces even trade secrecy, because the behavior leaks out through the product's own outputs; no patent regime fully contains a function you can query. The ownable things are, surprise, the datacenters, the land under them, the power contracts and the water rights. Proposition 3 says the market value of the AI sector migrates from the former to the latter — whatever the current market believes.

Proposition 4 (Real wages and the land content of subsistence). In the corner regime, with the recursion of Proposition 3: (i) the real wage in machine-produced goods is w/pg = 1/L̄, where L̄ = ∫dx/γL(x) is the labor a human would need to complete the whole checklist alone — absolute solo productivity, invariant to machine quality; the machine rental cancels. (ii) The price of non-produced services relative to machine-made goods is r/pg = (1−a)/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄), and it diverges as the task-side edge closes (ρ̄ → 0) or the recipe's land content vanishes (ℓ → 0). The self-replication margin moves it boundedly, and in the opposite direction to a naive reading: improvement is a ↓, which raises r/pg toward its bound 1/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄), while a → 1 is regress — it sends machine costs, not rents, to infinity (c = ℓr/(1−a)), and r/pg → 0. Even substituting machines for land inside the recipe, ℓ = ℓ0(1−a), leaves r/pg = 1/(ℓ0·ρ̄·L̄): bounded. (iii) Against a consumption bundle with expenditure share σ on non-produced services — housing, siting, energy, food-acreage — the real wage collapses if and only if σ > 0; at σ = 0 it is bounded at 1/L̄. The participation constraint w ≥ s binds through the land-priced component of subsistence.

Proof. (i) With ρ flat at ρ̄, γM(x) = γL(x)/ρ̄, so pg = c·∫dx/γM = c·ρ̄·L̄ = w·L̄. (ii) Substitute c = ℓr/(1−a) into r/pg = r/(c·ρ̄·L̄). (iii) A geometric price index P = pg1−σ·rσ gives w/P = (1/L̄)·(pg/r)σ, which by (ii) tends to zero along the divergent margins (ρ̄ → 0, ℓ → 0), for any σ > 0. ∎

This concedes and then answers the strongest objection to every immiseration story (Caselli and Manning, 2019): cheaper machines cannot starve anyone in terms of the things machines make — correct, and beside the point, because subsistence is not made of machine-made goods alone. Space, shelter, energy, and the acreage under food carry irreducible non-produced content, and that is precisely where Proposition 3 sends the value. Immiseration, when it comes, arrives through rent, not through the wage: the pay packet buys as many gadgets as ever and ever less ground. The exit option s was land-backed from Section 2; the theory closes on itself.

Closing the loop: where the spending goes

Proposition 3 prices machines from the cost side; nothing so far says where household spending comes to rest. This subsection closes the corner-regime economy and states "conservation of rents" as what it is at the corner: an accounting identity.

Setup. Households jointly hold the land (the distribution of claims is arbitrary here, and becomes the subject of Proposition 8). Preferences are Cobb–Douglas over the machine-made final good g and direct land services h — housing, siting, space — with land share σ ∈ (0,1): U = g1−σ·hσ. Land is homogeneous with total stock T, renting at r, split between production use TP (entering machine-making through ℓ) and housing TH. Machines perform all tasks (labor has exited, or is of negligible measure); δ = 0, so the interest sliver is suppressed — with δ > 0 a thin interest flow reappears on the income side, exactly the wage of waiting of Proposition 3, and nothing below changes beyond carrying the term. Free entry in machine-making gives c = ℓr/(1−a) and hence pg = c·ρ̄·L̄.

Proposition 5 (Closure and conservation of rents). In the corner regime: (i) equilibrium exists and the real allocation is unique,

TH = σ·T, TP = (1−σ)·T, Y = (1−a)(1−σ)·T / (ℓ·ρ̄·L̄);

(ii) the entire goods bill is production-land rent, and national income is aggregate site rent identically,

pg·Y = r·TP,  pg·Y + r·TH = rT;

(iii) market clearing reproduces the cost-side relative price of Proposition 4(ii), r/pg = (1−a)/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄): the model is overidentified, and consistently so.

Proof. Gross machine services X solve X = ρ̄·L̄·Y + a·X — services for final tasks plus services consumed making services — so X = ρ̄·L̄·Y/(1−a), and production land demand is ℓ·X. Substituting pg = ℓr·ρ̄·L̄/(1−a) gives pg·Y = r·ℓ·ρ̄·L̄·Y/(1−a) = r·ℓ·X = r·TP: goods spending passes through the zero-profit machine sector and lands, all of it, on production land. Land is the only primary factor employed, so household income is rT. Cobb–Douglas shares give r·TH = σ·rT and pg·Y = (1−σ)·rT, hence TH = σ·T; land-market clearing TP = T − TH then pins Y as displayed; and dividing pg·Y = (1−σ)·rT by the expression for Y returns r/pg = (1−a)/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄), which is (iii). Uniqueness is immediate from the shares. ∎

Dissipation is migration, by construction: every unit of final spending resolves through the machine recursion into a claim on a site. The obvious escape route — that automation's released value might dissipate into consumer surplus at collapsing prices rather than reattach to land — is closed: prices do collapse and the identity holds, because what collapses is pg relative to r, never the rent's claim on the flow of spending. The wealth data already lean this way — Rognlie (2015) shows the entire postwar rise in the net capital share is essentially housing, which is to say land.

Remark (substitution: the CES dial). Replace Cobb–Douglas with CES elasticity η between goods and land services, and write q ≡ r/pg. The land share of expenditure becomes

sh(q) = σ·q1−η / (σ·q1−η + 1 − σ),

and as machine improvement sends q → ∞ (Proposition 4(ii)): sh → 1 for η < 1 — complements; the squeeze sharpens and housing eats the budget — while sh → 0 for η > 1 and substitution bounds it. Prediction 8 is thereby promoted from a deflator observation to an estimate of η; the climbing shelter share of low-income budgets is the η < 1 signature.

Two robustness remarks — heterogeneous parcels with an idle margin, and the corner-above case with labor still present — are deferred to the appendix; neither changes where anything goes.

Remark (distribution, made stark). The identity prices the stakes of Proposition 7: in the corner, a household without land claims has zero market income — not low; zero. Whether rents can fund the floor is therefore not one policy question among several; it is the question of whether anyone without a site eats. Proposition 8 takes it up.

6. The fiscal system is a tributary of the labor loop

For each fiscal instrument i, let λi be the wage-linked share of its base — for transfers, of its funding or its eligibility, as the question requires. Four accounting facts follow from Propositions 1 and 3. (i) In the corner regime the current-wage base shrinks toward participation × parity — toward zero in corner-below — so λ-weighted revenue contracts in proportion. (ii) The eligibility scissors: work-conditioned claims contract with participation while need-based claims expand; revenue and obligations cross like blades. (iii) Instruments taxing embodied past labor — the structures share of property tax, consumption financed from past-wage savings — keep yielding through the collapse and decay only at depreciation and drawdown rates: the fiscal cliff has a time constant, measured in years to decades. Cushion, not comfort. (iv) VAT is the exception that matters: its base survives, and its incidence migrates by itself to whatever finances consumption — which in the corner regime, by Proposition 3, is rents. The one instrument that follows the value without being redesigned.

Proposition 6 (Conditionality). (i) An unconditional transfer u enters both sides of the participation condition — work iff w + u ≥ s + u — and cancels: no margin moves, while the disposable floor rises by exactly u. The cancellation decomposes: u leaves the work–exit price w − s untouched, so its compensated (substitution) response is zero as an identity; what remains is income effect. Once u ≥ s, exit is feasible without woods or family: the transfer is a purchased commons, re-providing the outside option that enclosure priced away. (ii) An in-work benefit b lowers the reservation wage to s − b. In the linked regime, the induced participation pushes the margin left and part of b leaks into lower wages — the Speenhamland result rebuilt without employer power, the leak flowing through the market's own margin and growing as the schedule flattens. In the corner regime the wage is pinned, so there is no leak — but if s > c·ρ̄ ≥ s − b, the benefit summons work whose replacement value is c·ρ̄ from people whose time is worth s outside: society burns s − c·ρ̄ per hour to preserve the appearance of employment. The failure mode is regime-dependent: leak when linked, make-work in the corner. (iii) An out-of-work benefit b′ raises the reservation to s + b′ and taxes the first hour of work at b′; as parity compresses the wage distribution toward the floor (Section 4, stage one), the mass of workers inside any fixed withdrawal band rises mechanically. The same rulebook generates a worsening trap; the dysfunction belongs to the regime, not the rules.

Proof. The displayed inequalities plus Proposition 1's comparative statics. ∎

Remark (the decomposition, and the price of a moved margin). Write an instrument as the pair (mw, me) — its payment in and out of work — and Δ = mw − me for its work-contingent differential. The reservation wage is R = s − Δ, or R = s(y) − Δ once the exit option's value rises with the unearned cash y that accompanies it (leisure normal, in reduced form; s′ ≥ 0). Participation then responds through two channels only: ∂R/∂Δ = −1 — the substitution effect, full strength always — and ∂R/∂y = s′, the income effect, which the reviewed evidence puts near zero (Hoynes and Rothstein, 2019). The proposition's three instruments are three values of Δ. The in-work benefit (Δ = b) and the out-of-work benefit (Δ = −b′, its income term pushing the same way) are substitution instruments: moving the margin is their operating principle, and 6(ii)–(iii) price what that costs by regime. The unconditional transfer is the unique Δ = 0 instrument — zero compensated response as an identity, s′-sized response in fact. In the corner the accounting closes, symmetrically: every misplaced hour of participation burns |c·ρ̄ − s| — make-work summoned below the outside option burns s − c·ρ̄ (6ii); income-effect exit above it burns c·ρ̄ − s — a price that vanishes at the regime boundary. Deadweight is margin-moving times this price: full-strength for b and b′, s′-sized for u. And in the linked regime u's residual is not even a loss to labor: what little exit it induces pushes the threshold back and the base wage up — the Speenhamland leak run in reverse, accruing to workers rather than employers.

Read against the demolition schedule, the conditioning is the coverage. A work-conditioned claim pays only in the state the collapse removes — insurance that lapses exactly when the insured event arrives. An exit-conditioned claim pays in the loss state but manufactures entries into it, taxing the first hour back (6iii). The unconditional transfer is written on no state at all, so its guarantee survives the regime change that voids the others; in the corner it is pure insurance at zero deadweight — no substitution margin by construction, no leak to employers (the wage is pinned), nothing summoned. Proposition 7(ii)'s funding invariance is the same property read from the base side: the pairing cannot be taxed away at one end and moves no margin at the other.

Proposition 7 (Funding). (i) In the corner regime, labor demand is perfectly elastic at gross wage c·ρ̄, so a payroll or income tax t cannot pass to firms; workers' net wage is (1−t)·c·ρ̄. With full participation the transfer is u = t·c·ρ̄, and disposable income is

(1−t)·c·ρ̄ + t·c·ρ̄ = c·ρ̄ — identically, for every t, at full participation.

A wage-funded universal transfer in the corner regime is a closed loop through the same pockets. With partial participation it is worse than futile: the transfer spreads over N people while the tax falls on fewer, each worker's disposable income drops below c·ρ̄, and when it crosses s, marginal workers exit, the base shrinks, and u falls — a self-undermining spiral. (ii) Land's supply is fixed and its rent is a residual, so a tax on site rent capitalizes into the land price while leaving the rent flow and land use unchanged: the base cannot contract in response to being taxed. By (6i), the transfer it funds moves no margin either. That conjunction — untaxable-away base, distortion-free transfer — is the theorem. Two practical caveats, acknowledged: assessment must separate site value from improvements — a century-old valuation literature, hard but workable — and a near-complete tax capitalizes into land prices, compressing the collateral channel during transition. Neither touches the invariance logic. (iii) By Proposition 3's corollary, the non-empty funding bases in the corner regime are physical rents, institutional rents, transition rents, and thin interest; the wage column is blank. Funding transfers from the wage base is therefore impossible; and among the non-empty columns the site-rent base is distinguished rather than merely available — institutional rents are policy-elastic by construction and self-liquidating where labor-attached (Section 4), transition rents expire, interest is thin, while the land column, by (ii), is the only one that cannot empty itself. Base-class necessity plus within-class dominance; not, strictly, instrument necessity.

Proof. (i) Incidence under perfectly elastic demand, plus arithmetic. (ii) Fixed supply plus residual rent, plus (6i). (iii) Exhaustiveness of the taxonomy. ∎

Remark (the regime dial). Away from the corner, everything is continuous. With labor-demand elasticity εD — which Proposition 1(ii) maps to the inverse slope of ρ̃ at the margin — and participation elasticity εS, workers bear εD/(εD+εS) of a payroll tax, and exactly that fraction of a wage-funded transfer is shell game; the in-work leak of Proposition 6(ii) is the same ratio mirrored. The corner is the εD → ∞ endpoint, not a knife-edge: the theorems degrade smoothly in observable incidence. Which makes the regime measurable — every payroll-incidence estimate is, in this model, a reading of the slope of the link.

The converse deserves equal billing: in the linked regime, standard redistribution works roughly as advertised — labor demand is inelastic enough that taxes stick where aimed. The theory does not say the textbook was wrong; it says which regime the textbook was written in, and identifies the switch. Redistribution purely among labor, in a fully wage-linked economy at machine parity, pins its floor to subsistence no matter how the rules shuffle; the George program — tax the site rent, pay the uniform dividend — was suspended by the industrial era's steep schedule and is resumed by its flattening. George is not merely elegant; in the corner, he is necessary.

Feasibility: the coverage ratio

Necessity is not sufficiency: Proposition 7(iii) says the rent column is the only one standing, not that it is tall enough. The stark remark of Section 5 asks whether anyone without a site eats; this subsection prices the question.

Setup. Per person, subsistence is a bundle (gs, hs) — gs units of the machine-made good, hs of direct land services — costing Ps = pg·gs + r·hs (the bare P of Proposition 4's proof is the aggregate index; Ps is the floor's price tag). Full capture of site rent funds a universal transfer of rT/N per head; partial capture funds the proportional fraction, so the clean object is coverage at full capture, κ ≡ rT/(N·Ps), with q ≡ r/pg = (1−a)/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄) as in Proposition 4(ii).

Proposition 8 (Feasibility of the rent-funded floor). In the corner regime: (i) the coverage ratio is

κ = q·T / (N·(gs + q·hs)),

strictly increasing in q from 0 toward the physical land ratio T/(N·hs), which it never attains: machine improvement raises coverage on every margin — along a ↓ only as far as the bounded q = 1/(ℓ·ρ̄·L̄) allows (the 4(ii) echo); along ρ̄ → 0 and ℓ → 0 all the way toward the ratio itself. (ii) The floor is fundable, κ ≥ 1, iff q ≥ q* = N·gs/(T − N·hs), a finite threshold that exists iff T > N·hs; if land per head falls short of subsistence's direct land content, no machine progress funds the floor. (iii) The fiscal constraint is the land constraint in disguise: κ ≥ 1 holds exactly when N·(gs/q + hs) ≤ T — the direct plus embodied land content of N subsistence bundles fits in the land there is. Since national income is rT identically (5ii) and the base cannot contract under taxation (7ii), the rent-funded floor is feasible whenever any allocation of the economy could deliver N subsistence bundles; the wage-funded route stays infeasible at every q (7i).

Proof. (i) Substitute Ps and divide through by pg: κ = q·T/(N·(gs + q·hs)); dκ/dq = T·gs/(N·(gs + q·hs)²) > 0, with limits 0 and T/(N·hs), and the margins move q by Proposition 4(ii). (ii) Set κ = 1 and solve for q. (iii) One unit of the good carries ℓ·ρ̄·L̄/(1−a) = 1/q units of land through the recursion, so N bundles demand N·(gs/q + hs) of the T available; rearranging κ ≥ 1 gives exactly this inequality. ∎

The demolition funds its own remedy: the same q that empties the wage column (4ii–iii) runs the coverage ratio up its curve, so George's program grows more fundable precisely as it grows more necessary. The failure modes are exhaustive and physical, not fiscal: too little land per person (T ≤ N·hs), or progress not yet far enough (q < q*). A worked instance (a = 0.6, ℓ = 0.2, ρ̄ = 1.8, L̄ = 1, so q = 10/9; T = 100; unit bundle gs = hs = 1): fifty people are covered with a nineteenth to spare (κ = 20/19); eighty must wait for q to reach 4; one hundred twenty — land per head below the bundle's land content — are never covered. The ratio says which world you are in.

κ is measurable — Section 7 measures it (Figure 6). The floor's own spending also feeds the base back through the closure; favorable in direction, a tendency in magnitude, deferred to the appendix.

The commons, priced

Every floor so far has rested on s — the woods, the family, the value of the best life available without selling labor — and Section 2 admits that s quietly presupposes access to some land. This subsection makes the debt explicit, in the goods numeraire of Proposition 4: let the exit life yield an autarkic keep s0 — own production on a plot, machine-independent by construction — require he of land, and fall back on a dependency floor sd when even the plot is out of reach.

Proposition 9 (The enclosure margin). (i) While land demand falls short of the stock, the marginal parcel rents at its reservation value, zero: the commons is the idle margin, the exit plot is free, and s = s0 — the outside option of Propositions 1–8 is exogenous because land is not yet scarce at the margin. (ii) The corner closes the margin: TP + TH = T (5i), every parcel is employed, and the exit plot pays the ruling rent: s(q) = max(s0 − q·he, sd), falling one-for-one with the land price of subsistence living and reaching the dependency floor at qenc = (s0 − sd)/he — finite machine progress completes the enclosure. The margins that demolish the wage (4ii–iii) demolish the escape from it. (iii) The race: coverage κ(q) rises (8i) while s(q) falls; the natural floor dies at qenc and the artificial one becomes fundable at q* (8ii). Whether a gap opens between them — commons gone, floor not yet fundable — is a crowding question: qenc ≥ q* iff N ≤ qenc·T/(gs + qenc·hs). In the gap, the instruments of the next subsection are not optimization but rescue.

Proof. (i) Reservation rent zero plus excess supply. (ii) Land clearing is 5(i); the threshold solves s(q) = sd. (iii) Monotonicity of κ (8i) and of s(q); equate qenc to 8(ii)'s q* and solve for N. ∎

Remark (desperate supply, and the floor that walks back in). In the linked regime a falling s moves the participation margin inward: enclosure manufactures labor supply, pushing employment up and the wage down through Proposition 1's comparative statics — the enclosure-era reading of the model, run forward. The cancellation of 6(i) survives throughout — u enters both sides of work-versus-exit whatever s(q) is — but its meaning sharpens: past qenc the guaranteed floor is sd + u, so the transfer is not a supplement to the commons but its replacement, and the bill it must cover is exactly the he·q the enclosure took. The circle closes on the funding side too: the exit plots' rents are inside rT, and the subsistence bundle of Proposition 8 already carries the plot (hs) — the same flow that priced the commons away is the flow that buys it back. (s0 constant takes the exit life as autarkic; a machine-tooled exit adds a falling goods term, and the conclusion stands wherever the exit bundle is land-heavy — the σ-logic of 4(iii), applied to the woods.)

The mix on the way down

Propositions 8 and 9 price the destination and the danger; this subsection prices the crossing. Until κ reaches one, the floor needs revenue beyond current site rents, and the only other standing bases are the transition's own: dynamic rents, institutional rents, and the still-alive wage flow. Two instruments claim them. A land-value tax reaches every site rent, consumed or saved, moves no margin (7ii), and needs a cadastre; it cannot touch a bottleneck profit or a royalty, which attach to no site. A uniform VAT reaches whatever finances consumption — including exactly those dynamic and institutional rents — and needs no valuation; but the slice of consumption financed by wages makes it, to that extent, a payroll tax in disguise, carrying the regime dial's deadweight on the participation margin. Each instrument is blind where the other sees.

Proposition 10 (The mix frontier). Fund the floor, total cost N·Ps, with an LVT at rate tL ≤ 1 on the rent flow rT and a uniform VAT at rate tV on aggregate consumption spending E, of which a share λC is wage-financed; let the VAT's deadweight be the standard half-square on its wage slice. (i) For κ ≥ 1 the optimum is the LVT alone: tV = 0, zero deadweight; no mix question arises. (ii) For κ < 1 — the measured present — the optimum fills the rent base first and bridges the gap with the VAT: tL = 1, tV = N·Ps·(1−κ)/E, and the minimized deadweight is proportional to λC·(1−κ)² at a given floor-to-consumption ratio: the transition cost of the program is the product of the two measured series — the wage-linkage of consumption times the squared shortfall of coverage — and both are moving the right way. (iii) As the corner approaches, λC → 0 and the bases converge by Proposition 5's identity — all consumption spending resolves into rents — so the two instruments become one and the mix question retires itself.

Proof. The LVT's marginal deadweight is zero (7ii) and the VAT's is positive and rising in its rate, so revenue fills the rent base first; the remainder pins tV, and substituting N·Ps − rT = N·Ps·(1−κ) into the half-square gives the display. (iii) is Proposition 5(ii) read as a tax base: with spending and rent the same flow, a tax on either is a tax on both. ∎

Remark (what the VAT is for). On the way down, the VAT is not a consumption-steering device but the only practical claim on scarcities that pay no ground rent: the buildout's bottleneck profits, the licensed and patented margins — dynamic and institutional rents that finance consumption without crossing a cadastre. That reach decays by construction: transition rents expire, labor-attached institutional rents self-liquidate (Section 4), and what remains fortified is a policy choice, not a base that endures on its own. So the weights travel with the regime — VAT-heavy while the transition's rents are fat, LVT-heavy as they decay and λC falls, indifferent at the corner — and the frontier stays interior for the whole crossing: neither instrument dominates until the point where they merge. Today's measured pair (λC = 0.72, κ = 0.33) prices the crossing at index 0.32; at, say, (0.50, 0.60) it is 0.08 — the program gets cheaper to run precisely as it becomes more necessary.

Two structural findings sit underneath the table. First, the corporate veil: the labor share of GDP is in the mid-fifties, yet the labor share of income households actually receive is 73% — because of the $9.8T gross operating surplus (2023), $3.8T is consumed by depreciation, $0.85T retained by business, and $0.6T intercepted by corporate tax before any of it becomes spendable income. Depreciation, the largest term, is Proposition 3 wearing accounting clothes: most of gross capital income is the machines maintaining themselves — the a·c term of the recursion, visible in the national accounts. Second, the scissors in time: the economy's wage-linkage (λC) has drifted down for sixty years while the fiscal system's wage-exposure (λR) rose and locked — the state deepened its dependence on exactly the base the technology erodes.

The remedy, measured. The coverage ratio of Proposition 8 is a measurable object, and Figure 6 measures it: the numerator is the aggregate site-rent flow the national accounts carry in pieces — Z.1 land residuals (market-value real estate minus replacement-cost structures, household and business) under capitalization-rate variants, with the housing-services flow as a lower-bound member — and the denominator is population times a costed subsistence bundle, priced by the Census thresholds' own CPI-updated logic. The ratio has climbed for seventy years, as Proposition 8 says it should — the demolition funding its own remedy — and the κ = 1 line is not yet in reach. Every land measure used is a lower bound, so the truth likely sits above the band's middle.

The fork, drawn. Prediction 8's mechanism is visible in the rawest series available: the same average paycheck, indexed at 1964, buys nearly four times the durables and a fifth less shelter — a near-fivefold fork between the machine-made and the land-priced denominators, opening exactly as machine capability spread (Figure 7). The divergence is Proposition 4's bookkeeping in public data: gadgets follow γM, ground follows r.

8. Predictions

  1. Within-group compression precedes between-group collapse. Groups exposed to automation lose most at the 70th–95th within-group percentiles; dispersion inside exposed groups falls even as gaps between groups widen. [By construction for 1980–2016 — the mechanism was transplanted from this finding (Acemoglu–Restrepo 2026); the predictive content is its persistence, and its inversion where protection holds law (Prediction 2).]
  2. Protection accelerates replacement — where it prices labor. At equal capability, the unionized, licensed, or premium-priced version of a task is automated earlier than the unprotected version; where protection instead holds the adoption decision — law, contract, scope rules — the ordering inverts, adjustment turns punctuated, and protection converts toward legal form under pressure, with a rising share of collective agreements carrying automation clauses as the direct trace. Price-based human-provenance premia get automated away or normalized away; only legal task reservations endure. [Cross-regime contrast: testable with matched task/occupation data crossed with contestability.]
  3. The transition can be negative-sum. Periods of intense wedge demolition show automation raising output while yielding little or no net TFP gain. [Estimated for 1980–2016: rent dissipation offset 60–90% of automation's productivity gains.]
  4. Wages pile up toward the floor, so traps worsen without rule changes. The share of workers inside benefit-withdrawal bands — facing high effective marginal tax rates — rises mechanically as compression proceeds. [Testable in METR microdata.]
  5. The labor share's decline is not mean-reverting. It resumes after each cyclical recovery rather than reverting to the postwar mean, deepening with each wave of capability uniformity. [Ongoing; the decline is global — Karabarbounis–Neiman 2014.]
  6. The rising capital share is land in disguise. Measured capital income increasingly resolves into housing and site value rather than returns on reproducible equipment. [Consistent: Rognlie 2015, Knoll–Schularick–Steger 2017; continued concentration predicted.]
  7. AI value attaches to sites, power, and water — not weights. Model-only firms' margins compress through distillation and copying; the durable balance-sheet items are datacenters, land, energy contracts. [Unfolding.]
  8. Real wages fork by deflator. Wage series deflated by machine-producible goods hold or rise while the same series deflated by shelter-heavy, land-content bundles stagnate or fall, and the gap widens with automation; the land-content share of low-income budgets climbs. The divergence is the mechanism of Proposition 4, not a measurement nuisance. [Drawn in Figure 7: a near-fivefold fork since 1964; sharpens as parity spreads.]
  9. The fiscal scissors widen. Wage-linked revenue exposure stays high while the wage-linkage of the economy falls; crises ratchet transfer growth financed by borrowing, and the borrowed channel's ultimate incidence stays undecided until the regime decides it. Operationally: each recession lifts the borrowed share of transfer financing by several points, with less than full retracement. [Visible in Figure 5.]
  10. Payroll incidence migrates onto workers. As schedules flatten, the estimated pass-through of payroll taxes to net wages rises toward one, and wage-funded transfer expansions show progressively fuller worker incidence — the regime dial of Section 6, read from the incidence literature. [Testable against payroll-incidence estimates over time.]
  11. Conditional programs' failure mode migrates. In-work subsidies shift from leaking into lower wages (linked regime) toward financing negative-value make-work (corner regime) as machine parity crosses outside options occupation by occupation. [Testable as parity thresholds cross.]
  12. The rescue is hollow where it operates. Where human-essential expenditure shares rise (Baumol concentration), aggregate labor share and median wage diverge; within the human-essential sectors, income concentrates with reach (the top share rising with the broadcastable fraction); co-present and legally reserved premia hold while certified-at-a-distance premia erode toward their fraud bound. [Testable: sectoral labor shares against within-sector top-income concentration; provenance-premium panels as verification technology shifts.]
  13. The exit option is priced by rents. Where the land content of subsistence rises, exit substitutes toward dependency: young-adult coresidence, multigenerational households, and household-formation delays track rent-to-wage ratios, not unemployment alone — and track them less where commons or cheap marginal land remain. [Testable in household-formation panels against local rent indices.]

9. Assumptions, honestly

Where a skeptic should push, in descending order of importance. Fixed task space — the real dichotomy: Acemoglu and Restrepo (2018) derive reinstatement: falling wages redirect invention toward new labor tasks, and for two centuries that counterweight operated. This paper closes that margin deliberately, and the closure should be read as the pessimistic branch of a stated fork. Branch one: reinstatement continues — which requires a standing reservoir of latent tasks where humans retain comparative advantage after each advance; that reservoir is the link, so the stabilizer presupposes what is at issue. Branch two: the reservoir thins — the branch supported by the displacement-versus-reinstatement accounting since 1987 and by the generality of AI, which targets the task-creation process along with the tasks. On branch one this paper's corner is postponed and its propositions describe a direction of travel; on branch two they describe a destination. The paper takes branch two and says so. Exogenous wedges: wedge locations and enforcement mode are taken as given; if institutions regenerate wedges or convert them to quantity form, stage one slows and the fortified stratum's drag compounds — the tank refills at one end while it drains at the other (the fortification remark tracks this; its bindingness is political economy, outside this model's machinery). Rationed wedge jobs: the participation margin is evaluated at the base wage (queue logic); a lottery convention softens the floor by the mean wedge and flips nothing downstream. Checklist (Leontief-over-tasks) production, one good, homogeneous workers: CES aggregation softens thresholds into bands; heterogeneity makes every statement type-by-type; regimes and propositions survive. Human-premium tasks: a theorem rather than a judgment — Aghion, Jones, and Jones (2019) supply the mechanism and Proposition 11 prices it: the rescue requires a K both non-automatable and non-substitutable; preference-membership binds only through the proof apparatus (co-presence or provenance law); and where the rescue operates it is distributionally hollow. What stays a judgment is the premise — how much capability residue persists: the first entry's fork, unchanged. Transition dynamics unmodeled (and the border, one page): Section 10 opens the economy — trade as the early waterline, rents to the producing jurisdiction's land, the border-split bases, migration flagged. The buildout era's very real bottleneck rents are classified (dynamic scarcity) but not tracked.

The strongest objection, as a theorem

The ledger above flags the human-essential reservoir as the model's largest judgment; this subsection prices it. Let K ⊂ [0,1], of measure k > 0, be the tasks machines cannot hold — for any reason: capability residue, enforced preference, law — earning wage wK, and let per-task machine costs m collapse along Section 5's margins.

Proposition 11 (The Baumol fork). (i) Every good whose checklist meets K has unit cost C = k·wK/γL + (1−k)·m → k·wK/γL as m → 0, and labor's share of that good's cost → 1: Baumol's disease, derived rather than assumed. (ii) Against K-light substitutes with elasticity η, the expenditure share of K-content tends to 1 for η < 1, to the taste weight θ at η = 1, and to 0 for η > 1: the rescue forks on substitutability, not on sentiment. (iii) Terminal expenditure concentrates on the non-collapsing categories — direct land services and K-labor — with the machine-made share at zero; labor's aggregate share is bounded below by the K-share on the η < 1 branch, and the corner regime of Propositions 3–8 is exactly the K = ∅ boundary of this theorem.

Proof. (i) Read the share off C(m) and let m → 0. (ii)–(iii) CES shares with one collapsing price: the collapsing term vanishes from the denominator for η < 1 and dominates it for η > 1; with three categories the machine term vanishes and land and K split the limit by weights and prices, θT·r1−η against θK·wK1−η; setting the K-weight to zero returns the corner. ∎

Remark (what K is made of, and what it shrinks to). Membership has three sources: capability residue (branch one of this section's first entry — thinning, by that fork's own argument), preference, and law. A preference-based membership must be verifiable to bind: at the android limit human provenance is a credence good, and the sustainable premium obeys πH ≤ min(φH, v·f/(1−v)) — latent taste φH, verification power v, legal penalty f for provenance fraud. Enforced as price alone (f → 0), the premium collapses for every v < 1: certified-human unravels exactly as Proposition 2's self-liquidation predicts, through fakes rather than robots. The two survivable anchors are f > 0 — provenance law, which is quantity exclusion in Section 4's sense, the fortified set again — and v → 1: co-presence, humanity verified by being in the room. So K's terminal core is what law reserves plus what bodies witness; the floor under human premia is a property of the proof apparatus over tasks, not of the tasks.

Remark (superstars: the rescue is distributionally hollow). Within K, reach is machine-side: recording, broadcast, replication serve the β-fraction of K-demand that does not require the room, and concentrate it on the best few, while the co-present remainder is rationed by human capacity. With n K-workers, the top performer's share of K-income is β + (1−β)/n — rising with reach at rate 1 − 1/n — and the median K-income falls to (1−β) of the mean. Combine with the fork: on the η < 1 branch the aggregate labor share can persist, even approach one, while the median wage still collapses to the floor — the persisting share belongs to K, and within K to its stars. Aggregate rescue, median demolition: the objection, even granted its premise, does not save the paycheck it was raised to defend.

One consequence travels back to Section 6: on the K branch the subsistence bundle itself carries a K-service component — care, teaching, the human hour the floor must buy — so Ps gains a non-collapsing term and the coverage ratio κ sits lower at every q. The reservation economy taxes its own remedy.

10. The open economy, in one page

The model has run closed; one page opens it. Let a tradable subset of tasks be performable abroad at foreign wage wf, with effective foreign schedules built exactly as at home (Lemma 1 with foreign subscripts), and let machines rent at c everywhere: Proposition 3's recursion respects no border.

Proposition 12 (Trade as the early waterline). (i) A tradable task is held at home iff w ≤ min(c·ρ̃(x), wf·ρ̃f(x)), where ρ̃f deflates the home-over-foreign human edge by the same wedge: the foreign wage enters as a second machine rental — a rental on human capability — so on the tradable set the demolition of Section 4 runs with a weakly higher waterline: same targeting (wedges are negative altitude in both waters), same strata, earlier timing. (ii) Offshoring is a way-station, not a destination: the parity that empties the home tradable set empties the foreign one — when machine cost undercuts wf, the offshore task flips onward to machines wherever they sit — and the terminal allocation is the closed model's corner, with production landing where sites, energy, and water are cheapest. (iii) Rents accrue to the land of the producing jurisdiction: a consuming country's spending resolves, through the recursion, partly into foreign site rents — the embodied land content of its imports — beyond its LVT's reach; a destination VAT reaches exactly that slice, while the LVT reaches domestic sites whoever owns them. The border splits the bases, each instrument covering the other's cross-border blind spot: the mix frontier of Proposition 10 is interior in the open economy for a second reason.

Proof. (i) Three suppliers per task; divide the cost comparison by µ(x) as in Lemma 1. (ii) Lemma 1 with foreign subscripts. (iii) Proposition 5's pass-through runs where the machines run; the destination principle and in-rem incidence are definitional. ∎

Remark (borders, rooms, and feet). Three notes. The wedge story travels: on the tradable set the best-protected jobs flood first whichever water rises — which is why the empirical literature struggles to separate automation from offshoring; in this model they are one waterline read at two heights. The refuge doubles: the non-tradable set is largely the co-present set, so the room that verifies humanity (the remark to Proposition 11) is also the room the container ship cannot enter — co-presence shields twice, and the terminal K of Section 9 is unchanged by opening the border. And feet move: a jurisdiction's dividend is its own rT/N, so dividend differentials invite migration and the crowding threshold of Proposition 9(iii) is per-jurisdiction — flagged, not modeled, alongside the buildout dynamics it belongs with. For a consuming country in deficit, part of the floor's price tag is foreign land — beyond the domestic LVT, within the destination VAT: on open borders, the bridge of Proposition 10 is not optional but constitutive.

11. What the planner would do

The preceding sections price the demolition, the migration, the floor, the crossing, and the objection; this one asks what a planner holding the model's own instruments should do, and finds the answer has been assembling itself all along.

Proposition 13 (The corner welfare theorem). In the corner regime with land fully enclosed (Proposition 9) and the George pair available — an LVT at rate tL ≤ 1 funding a uniform transfer u = tL·rT/N: (i) Efficiency. Every member of the pair's family is production- and participation-efficient: the automation margin is undistorted, and under u the private participation rule — work iff c·ρ̄ ≥ s — coincides with the social one, because at parity the wage is the machine's replacement cost, which is the marginal task's social value. (ii) Implementation. The pair delivers, at zero deadweight, the entire one-parameter family of distributions from the inherited division of rT (tL = 0) to the equal division (tL = 1): the base cannot contract (7ii), the transfer moves no margin (6i), and nothing else in the corner is left to distort. Land rent is the economy's one real lump sum, and the second welfare theorem — a fiction under wage taxation — holds at the corner with instruments that exist. (iii) Converse. Within the model's instrument set the pair is unique up to the corner equivalence of consumption and rent bases (10iii): work-conditioned floors violate (i) by the burn |c·ρ̄ − s|, and wage-funded floors violate (ii) by the shell game (7i).

Proof. (i) The adoption margin compares costs the planner also faces; under u the displayed participation rule is 6(i)'s cancellation read at the social wage c·ρ̄. (ii) Person i with land share ωi receives (1−tL)·ωi·rT + tL·rT/N; the family sums to rT for every tL, and no term moves a margin by 7(ii) and 6(i). (iii) is the conjunction of 6(ii)–(iii), 7(i), and 10(iii). ∎

Remark (the moral, stated plainly). Hold the technology fixed at the corner — same q, same rT — and vary only the fiscal architecture. Under the inherited, wage-linked state the base is the collapsing column: revenue follows the wage to zero, work-conditioned floors lapse with the work, and the terminal income of the claimless is sd. Under the George pair the same economy pays everyone rT/N above the dependency floor, participation is efficient, and the machines work for whoever holds the dividend. The corner is not a forecast of misery; it is a fork between two bookkeeping systems. The difference between the corner as dystopia and as inheritance is a tax base — which is to say, a choice. Externality pricing composes freely on top: the pair is lump-sum-like, so a Pigouvian floor on land abuse or a carbon levy interacts with nothing here, and is priced separately, as it should be.

Remark (second best, on the way). Away from the corner the planner is not free: the wage still carries λC of consumption; the wedge stock makes some privately profitable adoption socially negative — the social margin compares machine cost to the base wage, not the wedge-inflated one, so the band between them, of width (µ−1)·w/γL per task, is negative-sum adoption, the fuel of Section 4's transition loss — and the fortified stratum blocks even efficient adoption. Production efficiency survives the second best (Diamond and Mirrlees, 1971): tax rents and consumption, never the adoption margin itself — the fortified equilibrium is precisely the violation. The second-best schedule is then the measured schedule: fill the rent base first, bridge with the VAT at deadweight λC·(1−κ)² (10ii), let the weights travel as both series move, and treat the gap of Proposition 9(iii) — commons dead, floor not yet fundable — as the emergency it is. The planner's timetable is not a doctrine; it is two time series the national accounts already publish.

Appendix: deferred remarks

Remark (heterogeneous land and the idle margin). With heterogeneous parcels the aggregates of Proposition 5 are unchanged: rT reads ∫r(τ)·dτ over parcels in use, parcels at the idle margin earn their reservation rent of zero, and r in the identities is the rent schedule, not a single number. Nothing in the proposition requires every acre to be employed — only that spending, wherever it goes, crosses a site.

Remark (corner-above). With labor still present at machine parity the identity weakens to: all non-wage income is site rent, GDP = N·c·ρ̄ + rT, with the wage bill pinned at parity and vanishing along the ρ̄ → 0 margin, recovering Proposition 5's identity in the limit. The full mixed-case accounting adds notation without changing where anything goes.

Remark (the base feeds back — tendency, not theorem). The floor of Proposition 8 is spent partly on land services; in the corner that spending resolves into rents (Proposition 5), lifting r and with it the base rT. The direction is favorable — the loop closes the right way — but the magnitude rides on distribution and substitution; claimed as tendency only.

Data and computation. All U.S. figures are gross annual flows computed from BEA national accounts (annual NIPA series retrieved via FRED, 2026 vintage), with Federal Reserve Z.1 balance-sheet lines, BEA fixed-assets stocks, and the CPI added for the feasibility coverage ratio. Revenue is decomposed into wage- and ownership-linked parts (payroll to wages; personal income tax split by the labor/capital mix of household income with a progressivity tilt; corporate and sales taxes bracketed between standard incidence readings; property taxes to ownership); transfers draw on the pool pro rata (accounting attribution, not incidence estimation), with the borrowed share equal to the government current-account gap times transfers' share of current expenditure, attributed under three labeled rules. Benefit conditionality follows program rules: conditioned on work history, or withdrawn as earnings rise, counts as wage-linked; the six largest named programs alone give the 0.84 floor. Every arguable classification was computed under all defensible variants; reported values are medians with min–max bands across the grid. Flows are gross: a retiree's pension counts as a flow to a past worker, not netted against their own contributions. Notation. ρ(x) here is a capability schedule; the scalar ρ̂ ≈ 0.5 in Acemoglu–Restrepo (2026) is a wedge-markup estimate, and their λ is a task-substitution elasticity unrelated to the wage-linkage shares λi used here.
