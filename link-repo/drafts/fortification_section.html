The proviso is load-bearing: a wedge whose institution can reach the feasible set itself — blocking adoption by law or contract rather than pricing labor — exits the theorem’s domain; the fortification remark below tracks where it goes.

Remark (fortification: what the demolition leaves standing). Proposition 2 governs wedges that price labor, and the distinction that matters is not who holds power but where the lever rests. A strike threat is priced leverage: its value is the cost of replacing the strikers, which is exactly what automation feasibility destroys — so bargained premia erode on the theorem’s schedule, and history mostly shows the veto being sold rather than held (dockside mechanization agreements, print-room lifetime guarantees: buyouts that convert blocking power into ordinary transition rents). A statute is different. Scope-of-practice rules extended to software, an automation ban written into contract, a mandated human in the loop — quantity exclusions survive parity untouched, and under pressure protection actively converts from the first form into the second. The demolition is therefore a filter: it removes price wedges in ρ̃-order — the U-shape data — and leaves quantity wedges standing, so the surviving wedge stock distills toward legal form. Three consequences. Adoption at fortified tasks is punctuated: nothing moves until the accumulated gap clears the cost of confrontation, then the stratum goes at once. The finite fuel tank caps only the contestable stratum: a blocked task’s forgone saving grows with every machine improvement, and the fortified sectors — sheltered, non-tradable, Baumol-side — grow their expenditure share as everything else cheapens, so the drag compounds twice. And the terminal wage column is blank only of competitive wages: a fortified wage is institutional rent in wage clothes — infra-marginal, taxable as rent, revocable as law — a column Proposition 3’s corollary already holds open. (The manager deciding whether to automate management is the limiting case, wedge-holder and adoption-decider in one chair, disciplined only by entry and takeover — slowest where product markets are most sheltered.)

  • Protection accelerates replacement — where it prices labor. At equal capability, the unionized, licensed, or premium-priced version of a task is automated earlier than the unprotected version; where protection instead holds the adoption decision — law, contract, scope rules — the ordering inverts, adjustment turns punctuated, and protection converts toward legal form under pressure, with a rising share of collective agreements carrying automation clauses as the direct trace. Price-based human-provenance premia get automated away or normalized away; only legal task reservations endure.
