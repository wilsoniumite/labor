# East Asia (Japan, South Korea, Taiwan): land taxation and official land valuation, from Meiji Japan's 1873 land tax reform to 4 November 2026

Reading key for confidence labels used throughout:
- **[P]** = confirmed in a primary source I actually opened (statute text or official law-history page, government ministry/tax-authority publication or statistics, official museum/archive publication of a tax authority, court decision text). Contemporaneous newspaper reports of court rulings and official announcements are marked **[P-news]** (contemporaneous journalism, not the official document itself).
- **[S]** = secondary source only (encyclopedia/scholarly reference work, trade press, later journalism), or a primary source known to me only through a secondary summary or a search-engine snippet.
- **[U]** = unresolved/unverified (my background knowledge, conflicting sources, or found only in Wikipedia/advocacy leads). Not to be stated as fact without further checking.
- "Computed" = my own arithmetic on a cited table.

Research note: the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through this assignment. After that point I verified items only by fetching official URLs directly (law databases, ministry statistics, newspaper archives). Several Korean and Taiwanese official sites were unreachable from this environment (see Access log at the end). Japanese National Tax Agency (NTA) history pages are in Shift-JIS and render as garbage in some readers; I converted them myself, so quotes below are from the converted text.

Romanisation: Japanese in modified Hepburn, Korean in Revised Romanisation, Chinese in Hanyu Pinyin (Taiwanese official names keep their usual spellings).

## Dated timeline (for merging into the global chronology)

### Japan

- 1870 — Kanda Takahira's memorial 『田租改革建議』 (Denso kaikaku kengi) proposes replacing rice tribute with a cash tax on land values recorded on saleable land deeds (沽券 koken); reissued 1872 as 『田税新法』 — [NTA Tax Museum special exhibit 2002, "年貢と税金"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02.htm); [same exhibit, "地券制度"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]
- 1872 (Meiji 5) — Tokyo urban land (previously tax-exempt townsmen's and samurai land) taxed at 1% of land value; "Jinshin" land certificates (壬申地券 jinshin chiken) issued — [NTA, 租税史料叢書 vol. 1 introduction (Ushigome Tsutomu)](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA exhibit "地券制度"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]. Kotobank's Heibonsha entry dates the Tokyo certificates and tax to Dec 1871 and the lifting of the ban on permanent land sales to Feb 1872, with Jinshin certificates extended to all private land in Jul 1872 — [Kotobank, 地租改正](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- 1873-04 — Finance Ministry convenes prefectural officials to debate the draft reform (地租改正方法草案) — [NTA exhibit "地券制度"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]
- 1873-07-28 — Land Tax Reform Act (地租改正法) promulgated as Dajōkan Proclamation No. 272, comprising the Land Tax Reform Regulations, Enforcement Rules and Instructions to Local Officials; tax = 3% of assessed land value, in cash, on the owner — [NTA exhibit](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]; exact day 28 July and the local-surcharge cap (≤1/3 of land tax) — [Kotobank (Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- 1874-05 — Chapter 8 added: land values to be held unchanged for five years even if sales show different prices (the promise of a five-yearly revaluation) — [NTA vol. 1 intro](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1875 (Meiji 8) — urban land rate raised from 1% to 3%, matching rural land — [NTA vol. 1 intro](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1875-07-14 — eight villages near Kashiwazaki (Niigata) sign a pledge to finish their parcel registers, maps and marker stakes for inspection — [NTA "実地検査の誓約書"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/184.htm) [P]
- 1876-12 — Ise riot (伊勢暴動), the largest anti-reform uprising (Mie, spreading to Aichi, Gifu, Sakai); more than 50,000 people punished — [Kotobank, 伊勢暴動](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%8A%E5%8B%A2%E6%9A%B4%E5%8B%95-432657) [S]
- 1877-01 — Imperial rescript on tax reduction (減租の詔): rate cut to a uniform 2.5%; half of the paddy tax temporarily payable in rice — [NTA vol. 1 intro](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA exhibit](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]
- 1879 / 1881 — reform of arable and residential land complete 1879; forests and wasteland 1881 — [Kotobank (Yamakawa)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]; project ended Meiji 14 (1881) — [NTA vol. 1 intro](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1880 — five-year value freeze extended a further five years; only "special land value revisions" (特別地価修正) allowed; Kanagawa and 17 other prefectures cut values by ¥16.9 million — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA vol. 2 intro (Suzuki Yoshiyuki)](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- FY1881 — land tax ¥43,274,000 = 70.2% of government revenue — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1884 — Land Tax Ordinance (地租条例 chiso jōrei) replaces the 1873 regulations; deletes the five-year revaluation chapter and the promise to cut the rate to 1%; legally fixes the reform's values — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1885–1888 — nationwide re-inspection of parcels (地押調査 jiōshi chōsa) to build new land ledgers (土地台帳) — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA exhibit 2015](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h27shiryoukan/02.htm) [P]
- 1887 and 1889 — special value revisions: Kyoto + 11 prefectures (−¥12.9 million of land value), then Tokyo + 42 prefectures (−¥129.5 million) — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- FY1887 / FY1897 / FY1907 — land tax = 63.6% / 37.6% / 24.9% of tax and stamp revenue; largest tax every year 1873–1898 — [NTA essay, Nov 2025](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm) [P]
- 1898 (13th Diet) — first rate rise: fields to 3.3% (time-limited 1899–1903) with urban residential land at 5%; accompanied by revision of field values; 1899 special revision Tokyo + 41 prefectures (−¥148.6 million) — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P; note internal inconsistency on the field rate, see Q1]
- 1904–1905 — Russo-Japanese War emergency taxes raise urban residential land rate from 5% to 8% and then 20% — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1910-03 — Residential Land Value Revision Act (宅地地価修正法): residential land value reset to 10 × annual rental value; first revision of residential values since the 1870s; results (Dec 1911) average 4.44 × old values; total residential land tax ≈ unchanged (¥16.12m → ¥16.04m) — [NTA essay](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm); [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- FY1912 — land tax falls below 20% of revenue — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1918-08 to 1921-03 — nationwide survey of paddy/field values on a rental-value basis (339 temporary staff) — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1923–24 — after the Great Kantō Earthquake the Reconstruction Bureau adopts a route-value (路線価 rosenka) land appraisal method modelled on Cleveland, Ohio — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1926-03 — Land Rental Value Survey Act (土地賃貸価格調査法); every parcel in Japan given a rental value by Dec 1927 — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1931 — Land Tax Act (地租法): tax base becomes registered rental value (賃貸価格 chintai kakaku); rate 3.8% (4% in 1931 only); revaluation every 10 years — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1938 — first (and only) ten-yearly revision of rental values (base date 1936-04-01) — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1946 — inheritance tax values residential land at a multiple of rental value — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1947 — land tax transferred from national to prefectural tax — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1949-05 — Temporary Residential Land Rental Value Revision Act (war damage); survey Apr–Oct 1949, run by the new National Tax Agency (created 1949-06-01) — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1949-09 — Shoup Mission recommendation: abolish land and house taxes, create a municipal fixed asset tax — [MIC chronology of local tax reforms since Shoup](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- 1950 — Local Tax Act reform: land tax and house tax abolished; fixed asset tax (固定資産税 kotei shisan zei) created as a municipal tax on land, houses and depreciable business assets; rental values abolished as a legal concept — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf); [MIC fixed asset tax overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html); [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1955 — NTA introduces route prices for inheritance/gift tax valuation in Tokyo, Osaka, Yokohama, Nagoya, Kobe, Kyoto — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]; via NTA Commissioner's circular 「宅地の評価について」 — [Kotobank, 路線価](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B7%AF%E7%B7%9A%E4%BE%A1) [S]
- Early 1960s — valuation under national Fixed Asset Evaluation Standards (固定資産評価基準) introduced — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P for the reform; exact first application year (commonly FY1964) U]
- 1969 — Land Price Publication Act (地価公示法 Chika kōji hō), Act No. 49 of 1969 — [e-Gov law text](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/344AC0000000049) [P]
- 1970 — first published land prices (公示価格 kōji kakaku, "kōji chika"): 970 standard sites in the Tokyo, Osaka and Nagoya areas — [MLIT table of sites, 1970–](https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001333701.pdf) [P]
- 1973 — special land holding tax (特別土地保有税) created following the Jan 1973 Tax Commission report on land taxation — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- 1990-10 — Tax Commission's basic report on land taxation: make fixed-asset land valuations balanced and "appropriate", strengthen special land holding tax — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- 1991 — Land Value Tax Act (地価税法 Chikazei hō), Act No. 69 of 1991 — [e-Gov](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000069) [P]
- 1992-01-01 — national land value tax in force (0.3%; 0.2% in 1992) — [Cabinet Office Tax Commission paper, 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf) [P]; [Kotobank, 地価税](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E4%BE%A1%E7%A8%8E-167331) [S]
- 1992 — NTA route prices set at about 80% of published-price-based values — the 80% rule [P: NTA](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/kokuzeicho/2026/rosenka/index.htm); the start year 1992 [U]
- FY1994 — fixed-asset values for residential land set at about 70% of published prices ("7割評価"), with transitional burden adjustment — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html); [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- 1996–1997 — land value tax rate halved to 0.15% — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf) [P]
- FY1997 — new fixed asset "burden adjustment" keyed to the burden level (負担水準) — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- 1998 — land value tax suspended "for the time being" (当分の間) — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf); [MLIT land tax overview](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tochimizushigen/land/zei_gaiyou_hoyuu.htm) (seen via search snippet) [P/S]
- FY2003 — special land holding tax suspended "for the time being" — [MLIT FY2003 tax reform summary](https://www.mlit.go.jp/yosan/yosan03/zeisei03g/zeisei03g_.html); [Tokyo Metropolitan Tax Bureau](https://www.tax.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kazei/real_estate/special) [P]
- FY2021 — COVID measure: FY2021 taxable values held at FY2020 level for land whose burden would have risen — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- 2026-03-17 — 2026 published land prices: national all-use average +2.8%, fifth straight rise; 26,000 standard sites — [MLIT press release](https://www.mlit.go.jp/report/press/tochi_fudousan_kensetsugyo04_hh_000001_00074.html); [MLIT summary PDF](https://www.mlit.go.jp/totikensangyo/content/001985434.pdf) [P]
- 2026-07-01 — 2026 route prices published (target ≈80% of published-price-based value) — [NTA](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/kokuzeicho/2026/rosenka/index.htm) [P]; average standard-site change +2.9%, largest since 2010 — [Zeiken Press](https://www.zeiken.co.jp/zeikenpress/column/0020zp20260730/) [S, seen via search snippet]
- FY2027 — next triennial fixed asset revaluation (inference from the 3-year cycle and the FY2021 base year) [U/inference]

### Taiwan (Republic of China)

## Q1. Japan, 1873 to 1950: the Meiji land tax reform (chiso kaisei), how land was valued, rates, revenue, protest, and the long decline of the land tax

### Takeaway
The 1873 reform turned a harvest-share tribute paid in rice by villages into a fixed cash tax of 3% (2.5% from 1877) on an official land value (地価 chika) assessed once, parcel by parcel, by capitalising an estimated yield. The values were meant to be revised every five years but were frozen by law in 1884; partial "special revisions" mostly cut values, residential land was not revalued until 1910 (when values rose 4.4-fold on average), and from 1931 the tax base became rental value. The land tax supplied about 70% of revenue in 1881 but under 20% by 1912, and it was handed to the prefectures in 1947 and to municipal fixed asset tax in 1950.

### Cited Findings

**The reform package and the survey**
- The reform was promulgated in July 1873 as Dajōkan Proclamation No. 272, made up of three instruments: the Land Tax Reform Regulations (地租改正条例), Enforcement Rules (施行規則) and Instructions to Local Officials (地方官心得書); it fixed owners, calculated land value "on the basis of the land's yield", taxed 3% of that value (cut to 2.5% in 1877), payable in money — [NTA exhibit "地券制度"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]
- Before the reform, Edo-period tribute (年貢 nengu) was paid in rice, assessed with harvest inspections and village collective responsibility (村請制 murauke-sei); revenue moved with harvests and rice prices. After the reform the owner, not the village, was liable, and a fixed annual amount made budgeting possible — [NTA exhibit "年貢と税金"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02.htm) [P]
- Survey method: owners themselves surveyed their parcels; village representatives compiled parcel registers (地引帳 jibikichō) and maps; prefectures inspected and corrected; values were also in principle declared by owners but in practice fixed through village-level deliberation using several years of yields, grades and grain prices; prefectures then compiled certificate ledgers (地券台帳) and issued certificates — [NTA, "実地検査の誓約書"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/184.htm); [NTA exhibit 2015, "地租改正"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h27shiryoukan/01.htm) [P]
- Inspection: two-person prefectural teams did a parcel check (地押 jiōshi, one reading the register, one the map, while the owner read out the stake on each plot) and a measurement check — [NTA, network/184](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/184.htm) [P]
- Land certificates: the 1872 Jinshin certificates had been meant to record prices formed by free sale; because a market-price basis for all land was judged impossible to achieve quickly, the 1873 law instead used a "statutory land value" (法定の地価) printed on new certificates (地券 chiken) — [NTA exhibit "地券制度"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h14shiryoukan/02a.htm) [P]. The Jinshin survey results were not used for the reform except in a few prefectures such as Yamaguchi — [Kotobank (Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]

**How the land value (chika) was calculated**
- Principle: land value = one year's yield capitalised at the locally customary interest rate; but seed and fertiliser costs were fixed uniformly at 15% of the harvest and the capitalisation rate capped at 7% for owner-cultivated and 5% for tenanted land, "measures that raised land values" (地価を高める措置); local village charges capped at one-third of land tax — [Kotobank (Shogakukan Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- In the worked example in the reform rules, tax took 34% of the harvest, the landlord 34% and the tenant 32%, i.e. a rent of 68% of the crop (still paid in kind) — [Kotobank (Ōbunsha Japanese history dictionary)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- A worked plot example (as reproduced in a Journal of Economic History article I could not open): gross yield valued at ¥4.80; after seed/fertiliser, land tax and local tax, net profit ¥2.448, which capitalised at 6% gives a land value of ¥40.80 — [Cambridge Core, "Taxation and Landownership in the Westernization of Japan"](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/taxation-and-landownership-in-the-westernization-of-japan/CD607E502FAEC1644F03A50287BAB4C4) [S, via search snippet only]. Computed check: with seed/fertiliser 15% (¥0.72), tax 3% and village charge 1% of value, V = (4.80 − 0.72) ÷ (0.06 + 0.03 + 0.01) = ¥40.80; land tax ¥1.224 + village ¥0.408 = ¥1.632 = 34% of the gross crop, matching the 34% in the Ōbunsha entry.
- Implementation drifted from "set values from local conditions" to a method in which the government fixed a target average value and prefectures distributed it across village and plot grades (地位・村位等級組立法) — [Kotobank (Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- Urban residential land was valued from rents and sale prices under 1876 rules; rural house plots were simply valued as top-grade fields ("上畑"), which officials later said violated "the principle of taxation" — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA exhibit 2015, "都市の拡大と賃貸価格"](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h27shiryoukan/03.htm) [P]
- Even the Land Tax Reform Bureau conceded the results lacked "sufficient equalisation" (「充分ノ平準」) because of "the remnants of the feudal system and differences in local custom" — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P, quoting the Bureau]

**Rates and the 1877 cut**
- 3% of land value from 1873; urban land first at 1% (1872), raised to 3% in 1875; the 1877 rescript cut all land to 2.5% — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- The 1873 regulations also promised (Ch. 6) to cut the rate step by step to 1% once commodity taxes exceeded ¥2 million; the 1884 Land Tax Ordinance deleted this — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Encyclopedias tie the 1877 cut to the 1876 riots: "the reformed land tax remained heavy, so peasant disturbances arose everywhere; in 1877 the rate was lowered to 2.5%" — [Kotobank (Mypaedia)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]; the Ise riot "drew out the tax-reduction rescript of January the following year" — [Kotobank, 伊勢暴動 (Heibonsha)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%8A%E5%8B%A2%E6%9A%B4%E5%8B%95-432657) [S]
- The 1877 measure also allowed half the paddy tax to be paid in rice because falling rice prices had made the cash tax heavier; inflation from the 1877 Satsuma Rebellion later made the issue moot — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Later rates: 1898 rise to 3.3% for fields (time-limited 1899–1903), with urban residential land 5% and rural residential 3% — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]. **Source conflict:** the same NTA text says "3.3%" in its overview but "3.7%" for fields in its account of the House of Representatives' amendment; 3.3% is the commonly cited figure [flag for checking].
- 1904–05 war taxes: urban residential land 5% → 8% → 20% — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1910: residential land 2.5% (on the new rental-based values), fields 4.7% (Diet amendment), other land 5.5% — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1931 Land Tax Act: 3.8% of rental value (4% for 1931); the draft had 4.5% to keep revenue constant, lowered after naval-arms savings from the London Naval Conference were applied to tax cuts — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]

**Revenue and share of government revenue**
- FY1881 ¥43,274,000 = 70.2% of revenue; in the 60% range in FY1882–86 (Meiji 15–19); 50% range in FY1887–96 (Meiji 20s); falling through the 30% range to the 20% range from FY1897 (Meiji 30s); below 20% in FY1912; the land tax remained the largest single revenue source through the Meiji era (briefly overtaken by the liquor tax at the end of Meiji) and was definitively overtaken by income tax after the First World War — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Land tax as a share of "taxes and stamp revenue": FY1887 63.6%, FY1897 37.6%, FY1907 24.9%; it was the top tax every year from 1873 to 1898 — [NTA essay, Nov 2025](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm) [P]. **Minor conflict:** NTA vol. 1 says land tax stayed the top revenue item through Meiji except briefly; the 2025 essay says top "from 1873 to 1898". The difference may reflect revenue vs tax-only bases; unresolved.
- Why the share fell: the land tax was a fixed tax on a fixed base, so it changed only when values or rates changed, while liquor tax, income tax and business tax grew with the economy — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- One secondary source (citing Ono Takeo) puts the land tax at 82% of ordinary revenue in 1868–1875 — [Cambridge Core (JEH)](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/taxation-and-landownership-in-the-westernization-of-japan/CD607E502FAEC1644F03A50287BAB4C4) [S, search snippet only]

**Protest**
- Ise riot (Mie, December 1876): began with demands to lower the official rice-conversion price; at year end rice sold for about ¥3.50 per koku at Yamada while the prefecture's conversion price was ¥5.19; spread to Aichi, Gifu and Sakai prefectures with attacks on government buildings and district officials — [Kotobank, 伊勢暴動 (Heibonsha; Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%8A%E5%8B%A2%E6%9A%B4%E5%8B%95-432657) [S]
- Scale: "those punished exceeded 50,000" (処罰された者は5万人以上); Yamakawa's dictionary: "処罰者5万人" — [Kotobank, 伊勢暴動 (dictionary entries incl. Yamakawa)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%8A%E5%8B%A2%E6%9A%B4%E5%8B%95-432657) [S]. Note: a search-engine summary mis-rendered this as "50,000 executed"; the dictionaries say punished.
- Makabe disturbance (Ibaraki, 1876) and the popular verse 「竹槍でドンと突き出す二分五厘」 ("with bamboo spears we thrust out two-bu-five-rin", i.e. 2.5%) appear in school materials and summaries — [U, leads only]
- After the Diet opened in 1890, land-tax relief dominated budget fights. Low-valued regions (e.g. Aizu in Fukushima, Aomori, Iwate) petitioned for a rate cut, opposing revaluation; Mie petitioned for revaluation to correct inequalities; Tsushima islanders (Yoshida Hachisuke and 3,511 others) petitioned the House of Peers for lower values and were rejected — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]

**What happened to the valuations: freezing, partial revision, and the shift to rental value**
- 1874 rule: values unchanged for five years; some local officials publicly promised a five-year revaluation to calm protest, "in effect a government pledge" — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1880: the Land Tax Reform Bureau opposed revaluation (cost, revenue instability, and the fact that rising rice prices had lightened the real burden); freeze extended five more years with only local special revisions; 18 prefectures did them by autumn 1883 and 17 more were applying — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1884: Land Tax Ordinance abolished the revaluation chapter — "legally, the results of the reform were fixed" — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Special revisions (all reductions): 1880 (−¥16.9m of land value), 1887 (−¥12.9m), 1889 (−¥129.5m), 1899 (−¥148.6m); limited to particular areas, "no nationwide uniform review" — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- By 1898 the government's own case for a higher rate said sale prices had become "two to ten times or more" the ledger values — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1906 Tax Law Review Committee: debated market value vs assessed value, annual vs periodic revision; candidate bases sale price, rent, net income; chose net income, and for house plots rental value as its proxy; it declined to set any revaluation deadline because the five-year rule of 1873 had become a "dead letter" (空文) — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- The multiplier 10 × rent was derived by capitalisation: land tax 2.5% of value + other charges 1% + repairs 5% of rent + owner's 6% return ⇒ value ≈ 10 × rent; to avoid it being a tax rise, new values were capped at 20 × the old value — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1910 valuation machinery: tax offices drew up rental values; 464 elected committees (4,178 members, chosen by two-stage election of residential land taxpayers) reviewed them in 60-day sessions; government decided disputes; values were displayed for 20 days and could be appealed within 30 days — [NTA essay](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm); [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- 1910 results (announced Dec 1911): 9,413,767 parcels, 1.14855 billion tsubo; revised values averaged 4.44 × old; total residential land tax ~¥16.12m → ¥16.04m — [NTA essay](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm) [P]. (The essay prints "明治43（1919）年" for the 1910 Act; 1919 is a typo.)
- Valuation bases switched entirely to rental value: 1918–21 field survey; 1926 Land Rental Value Survey Act; values decided by elected local committees and confirmed by government; all parcels done by Dec 1927; the 1931 Land Tax Act made rental value the base for all land, revisable every 10 years — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm); [NTA exhibit 2015](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h27shiryoukan/03.htm) [P]
- Why rental value: officials said it "best expresses the land's use value", suits ability to pay, and is "comparatively easy to survey" — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- The 1926 survey moved from parcel-by-parcel valuation (一筆主義) to valuing zones of similar land together (区域主義) — [NTA exhibit 2015](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h27shiryoukan/03.htm) [P]
- The second ten-yearly revision (due 1946–48) was delayed by inflation; a war-damage revision of residential rental values only was rushed through in six months in 1949; the general revision never happened because the 1950 reforms abolished rental values — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Route-value methods: the 1923 Reconstruction Bureau method expressed each street's value as an index for a standard lot (5 ken deep × 1 ken frontage) multiplied by a district base price; the Tokyo tax supervision bureau used the same "street value" (街路価 gairoka) for its 1926 rental-value survey; the NTA adopted route prices for inheritance tax in 1955 — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]

**Scenes (Japan before 1950)**
- *A pledge in the snow country, 1875.* Eight villages near Kashiwazaki, Niigata, behind with their parcel declarations and "directly reprimanded" by prefectural officers, signed a pledge on 14 July 1875 to renumber their registers, draw maps and plant marker stakes (畝杭 seguī) "and promptly receive inspection". One village promised 20 September; the inspectors arrived on 10 October — [NTA, network/184](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/184.htm) [P]
- *The pear orchard that was worth eight fields.* In Akai village (Miyagi), a pear orchard was valued on its actual pear crop rather than on barley, as the early rules then required, making it eight times as valuable as neighbouring fields; a special revision was refused in 1884 and approved only in 1887 — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *Buying votes with phantom co-owners, 1910.* In Fukui, competition to elect the residential land valuation committee was so fierce that organisers registered single parcels as co-owned by a hundred or several dozen people to create voters; the ringleaders were arrested and the Tax Bureau warned other districts — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *Tomioka's committee, autumn 1910.* The committee opened on 10 October, examined the Tax Bureau's grade table, inspected sites, lowered the top grade in Tomioka and Shimonita towns by one step, cut the district total by over ¥5,500 and closed on 5 November — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *An American method in burnt-out Tokyo.* After the 1923 earthquake, the Reconstruction Bureau needed to value huge numbers of plots quickly for land readjustment and chose a method based on Cleveland, Ohio's route-value system — the ancestor of today's rosenka — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]

### Inferences
- The Meiji land value was a capitalised-yield value set once, with standardised costs and capped interest rates chosen to produce enough revenue to replace the old tribute; it was never a market value, and the legal freeze of 1884 turned it into a historic cadastral number. That made the tax predictable for the state but steadily lighter in real terms as prices rose, and uneven across places whose economies changed (cities, railway towns).
- The pattern after 1884 is the classic one for ageing valuations: the state preferred rate changes and targeted downward "special revisions" to a politically risky general revaluation; when residential values were finally reset in 1910 they had to rise more than fourfold, and the reform was designed to keep total revenue constant so that it redistributed burden rather than raised it.
- The move to rental value (1910 for house plots, 1931 for all land) was justified on administrative grounds (rent is observable) as much as on principle; the 10-year revision rule for rental values was itself overtaken by war and inflation and never operated more than once.

## Q2. Japan after 1945: the Shoup Mission and the fixed asset tax; the published land price system (1969–70); route prices; the 1994 alignment to 70%; the land value tax (1992–98); the special land holding tax (1973–2003)

### Takeaway
Post-war Japan has several official land values for one plot: the Land Appraisal Committee's annual "published price" (kōji kakaku, intended as the full "normal price" at 1 January), prefectures' 1 July survey, the National Tax Agency's annual route prices (≈80% of published-price-based value) for inheritance and gift tax, and municipalities' triennial fixed-asset values (≈70% of published prices for house land since FY1994). The bubble years produced two land-holding taxes on top of the fixed asset tax: a national land value tax from 1992 (suspended from 1998) and the local special land holding tax from 1973 (suspended from FY2003). Bringing fixed-asset values up to 70% of market in 1994 forced long "burden adjustment" phase-ins.

### Cited Findings

**Shoup and the fixed asset tax (固定資産税)**
- The 1949 Shoup recommendation called for reviewing the land tax and house tax and creating a municipal fixed asset tax; the 1950 reform abolished both and created the fixed asset tax (municipal only), adding a tax on depreciable business assets — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- MIC describes the tax as a property tax levied "on the benefit principle" for the link between owning fixed assets and municipal services — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- With the 1950 reform the land ledger law was amended, rental values abolished and the ledger moved from the Finance Ministry to the Legal Affairs Bureau; the fixed asset tax got its own base, "the price of land" (initially still computed from rental values) — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Today: taxable value = "price (appropriate market value)" (価格（適正な時価）); standard rate 1.4%; revaluation every three years with values otherwise held; mayors must determine values according to the Fixed Asset Evaluation Standards set by the minister; residential land valued at "about 70% of published land prices" (introduced with the FY1994 revaluation); houses at replacement cost less depreciation — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- Reliefs: residential land taxable base reduced to 1/6 (≤200 m² per dwelling) or 1/3 (excess) — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- Burden adjustment: since FY1997 the tax base moves gradually toward the assessed value according to the "burden level" (負担水準 futan suijun = last year's taxable base ÷ this year's assessed value); for commercial land the taxable base is capped at 70% of assessed value and frozen when the burden level is 60–70%; rises for other land are 5% of the (adjusted) value per year, with a floor of 20% — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- Revaluations have always come with phase-in rules: "transitional burden adjustment" with the first revaluations in the mid-1960s; burden adjustment again with the FY1994 revaluation — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- FY2021 COVID measure: land whose burden would rise kept its FY2020 taxable base; FY2022 commercial land rises limited to 2.5% of value — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- Revenue: FY2024 fixed asset tax ¥10.0795 trillion = 42.1% of municipal taxes (¥23.95 trillion); city planning tax a further ¥1.4426 trillion — [MIC Local Finance White Paper 2026, Fig. 26 data](https://www.soumu.go.jp/menu_seisaku/hakusyo/chihou/r08data/2026data/csv/z-026.csv); [white paper text](https://www.soumu.go.jp/menu_seisaku/hakusyo/chihou/r08data/2026data/r08czb01-03.html) [P]. Computed: ≈21.8% of all local taxes (¥46.2691 trillion). The fixed-asset share of municipal taxes has been 40–42% each year FY2014–FY2024 — [MIC Fig. 27 data](https://www.soumu.go.jp/menu_seisaku/hakusyo/chihou/r08data/2026data/csv/z-027.csv) [P]. Land's share within the fixed asset tax is not separated in these tables.
- The Fixed Asset Evaluation Standards are a binding delegated regulation; courts have held that uniform national standards serve fair, balanced valuation, and that a registered price above the value produced by the standards is unlawful even if below market — [Wakimoto Toshinori, Nihon University economics journal](https://www.eco.nihon-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/92_03_05.pdf) [S; case dates garbled in the PDF text]

**The published land price system (地価公示 chika kōji)**
- Land Price Publication Act, Act No. 49 of 1969: the Land Appraisal Committee (土地鑑定委員会) of the ministry, "once every year", obtains appraisals by "two or more real estate appraisers" for each standard site, reviews and adjusts them, determines "the normal price per unit area" at a base date and publishes it; the purpose is to give an indicator for ordinary transactions and a basis for compensation in public land acquisition — [e-Gov law text](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/344AC0000000049) [P]
- First publication (1970): Tokyo, Osaka and Nagoya urbanisation areas only — 113 municipalities, 3,681 km², 970 sites; 1971: 1,350 sites; 1972: 2,800; 1973: 5,490; 1975: 15,010 sites covering urban planning areas nationwide — [MLIT table](https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001333701.pdf) [P]
- 2026: 26,000 sites (25,565 actually surveyed: 430 alternate-year sites, 4 Fukushima and 1 Noto sites suspended); base date 1 January; national all-use average +2.8%, residential +2.1%, commercial +4.3%; Tokyo area commercial +9.3%; fifth consecutive rise — [MLIT 2026 summary](https://www.mlit.go.jp/totikensangyo/content/001985434.pdf); [MLIT press release, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.mlit.go.jp/report/press/tochi_fudousan_kensetsugyo04_hh_000001_00074.html) [P]
- Site density (2020 standard): ~1 site per 0.7 km² in urbanisation-promotion areas, ~1 per 27 km² in urbanisation-control areas — [MLIT site allocation](https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001333700.pdf) [P]
- Prefectural land price survey: values as at 1 July, published around September each year — [NTA notice, Oct 2020](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/pdf/0020010-042.pdf) [P]

**Route prices (路線価 rosenka) for inheritance and gift tax**
- NTA regional bureaus fix route prices and multipliers every year for all private land "for the convenience of filing and the fairness of taxation", valuing at 1 January "at about 80% of the price based on published land prices"; 2026 values published on 1 July 2026 — [NTA 2026 route price page](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/kokuzeicho/2026/rosenka/index.htm) [P]
- Reason for the 80% margin: to avoid over-taxation if land prices fall after publication — [Kotobank (Kyodo glossary)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B7%AF%E7%B7%9A%E4%BE%A1) [S]; the NTA can "correct" route prices if large falls are confirmed (it considered this in 2020 and decided no correction was needed for January–June 2020) — [NTA notice, Oct 2020](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/pdf/0020010-042.pdf) [P]
- Taxpayers may instead use an appraiser's valuation of market value — [NTA notice, Oct 2020](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/pdf/0020010-042.pdf) [P]
- Fixed-asset route prices (set by municipalities; in Tokyo's 23 wards by the metropolitan government) are about 70% of published prices; inheritance-tax route prices about 80% — [Kotobank, 路線価 (dictionary entries)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B7%AF%E7%B7%9A%E4%BE%A1) [S]
- 2026 route prices: average change of standard sites +2.9% (2025: +2.7%), fifth straight rise; Tokyo +9.4%; top value Ginza 5-chōme (Kyūkyodō) ¥53.36 million/m², top for 41 years — [Zeiken Press](https://www.zeiken.co.jp/zeikenpress/column/0020zp20260730/) [S, via search snippet]

**Land value tax (地価税 chikazei), 1992–1998 (suspended)**
- Base: land held on 1 January each year, valued at market value as measured by inheritance-tax valuation (i.e. route prices); a large basic deduction (fixed amounts for companies and individuals, or ¥30,000 per m², whichever larger) meant only large holders paid; rate 0.3% (Act Art. 22: 3/1000 on the value above the deduction) — [Cabinet Office Tax Commission paper, 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf); [e-Gov, Land Value Tax Act](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000069) [P]
- Phase-in and cuts: 0.2% in 1992 (transitional), 0.15% in 1996–97 — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf) [P]; [Kotobank](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E4%BE%A1%E7%A8%8E-167331) [S]
- Revenue (settled, ¥ billion): FY1992 520.1; FY1993 605.3; FY1994 487.0; FY1995 406.3; FY1996 177.2; FY1997 160.1; revenue per 0.1% of rate fell from 260.1 (1992) to 106.7 (1997) as land values fell — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf) [P]
- Suspended from 1998 "for the time being" in view of falling land prices and the weak economy — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf) [P]; [Kotobank](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E4%BE%A1%E7%A8%8E) [S]. The Act remains on the statute book — [e-Gov](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/403AC0000000069) [P]

**Special land holding tax (特別土地保有税 tokubetsu tochi hoyū zei), 1973–2003 (suspended)**
- Created in 1973 after the January 1973 Tax Commission report calling for a local "land holding tax" — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- Aim: promote effective land use and curb speculation; levied on acquisitions and holdings above a size threshold (2,000 m² in Tokyo's 23 wards; 5,000 m² in municipalities with city-planning areas; 10,000 m² elsewhere); land held over 10 years excluded from the holding charge — [Tokyo Metropolitan Tax Bureau](https://www.tax.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kazei/real_estate/special) [P]
- Valuation base was **acquisition cost**, not an assessed value: holding charge = acquisition price × 1.4% − fixed asset tax equivalent; acquisition charge = acquisition price × 3% − real estate acquisition tax equivalent; after land prices fell, the lower of acquisition price or "revised acquisition price" was used — [Tokyo Metropolitan Tax Bureau](https://www.tax.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kazei/real_estate/special) [P]
- 1990–91: reviewed and strengthened (idle-land charges, special rules for big-city municipalities) — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- Revenue FY2000 ¥42.5 billion; from FY2003 "for the time being" no new taxation, to promote land liquidity — [MLIT FY2003 tax reform summary](https://www.mlit.go.jp/yosan/yosan03/zeisei03g/zeisei03g_.html) [P]

**The early-1990s push to align official values with market prices**
- Oct 1990 Tax Commission basic report on land taxation: "promote balance and appropriateness of fixed-asset land valuation" and strengthen the special land holding tax; Nov 1993 report: burden adjustment for the revaluation (implemented FY1994) — [MIC chronology](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000998640.pdf) [P]
- From FY1994 residential land valued at ~70% of published prices nationwide — [MIC overview](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]; Nishinomiya City explains the 70% target drew on the different nature of the two systems and the observed ratio in the stable-price late 1970s/early 1980s — [Nishinomiya City](https://www.nishi.or.jp/kurashi/shizei/tochi/tochi/koteishisanhyoka.html) [S, via search snippet]
- Because assessed values jumped, municipalities introduced expanded residential reliefs, temporary special measures and gradual burden adjustment; Kawasaki notes the burden-level system from FY1997 — [Kawasaki City FAQ](https://www.city.kawasaki.jp/templates/faq/230/0000027203.html) [S, via search snippet]

**Scenes (post-war Japan)**
- *The pavement in front of a stationer's.* The single most valuable route-price point in Japan has, for 41 straight years to 2026, been the stretch of Ginza Chūō-dōri in front of the Kyūkyodō stationery shop, at ¥53.36 million per m² in 2026 — [Zeiken Press](https://www.zeiken.co.jp/zeikenpress/column/0020zp20260730/) [S]
- *A pandemic check of the 80% margin.* In October 2020 the NTA announced it had commissioned outside experts to see whether route prices exceeded market values after COVID; finding no large falls for January–June, it made no correction — a rare glimpse of the 80% safety margin being tested — [NTA notice, Oct 2020](https://www.nta.go.jp/information/release/pdf/0020010-042.pdf) [P]

### Inferences
- Japan's post-war design separates *who measures market value* (the Land Appraisal Committee using private appraisers) from *who taxes* (NTA, municipalities), and then applies deliberate discounts (80%, 70%) so that official tax values sit safely below market. The published price thus acts as the anchor, and tax values are pegged to it rather than independently appraised. The 1994 alignment is the clearest case of a government moving tax values toward market prices; it was done by raising assessed values abruptly and then spreading the tax increase over many years through burden adjustment rather than by changing rates.
- The two bubble-era holding taxes show the opposite cycle: introduced or strengthened when land prices were rising (1973, 1991–92), and suspended rather than repealed when prices fell (1998, 2003). Both measured land in ways that avoided a new valuation machine: the land value tax piggy-backed on inheritance route prices; the special land holding tax used acquisition cost.

- Initial fixed asset tax rate in 1950 (often given as 1.6%, later 1.5% and 1.4%) and the year the 1.4% standard rate began: not verified.
- Exact year when route prices were first set at ~80% of published prices (commonly 1992) and earlier ratios: not verified in a primary source.
- Before-and-after numbers for the FY1994 jump (e.g. fixed-asset values as a share of published prices in the late 1980s, often said to be 10–20% in big cities): not found in a source I could open.

## Q3. South Korea: official land prices from 1989–90; the 1989 "public concept of land" laws and the Constitutional Court; the Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax (2005–); the 2020 realisation roadmap and its reversal; the Korea Real Estate Board

### Takeaway
Korea built a single national official land price system in 1989–90 to serve the new "public concept of land" taxes: about 300,000 (now 600,000) standard parcels appraised by licensed appraisers, from which local governments derive prices for every other parcel with comparison tables. Two of the three 1989 laws were struck down or crippled by the Constitutional Court (1994, 1999), and in 2008 the Court also struck down household aggregation in the 2005 Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax. Official values have stayed well below market (realisation rates of about 54–69%); a 2020 plan to raise them to 90% was reversed in 2022–24, and the rates have been frozen at 2020 levels for 2023–2026.

### Cited Findings

**Official land prices (공시지가 gongsi jiga): design and producers**
- Before 1989, land had several official values; the 1989 system was meant to unify them ("여러갈래로 나뉘어 있던 땅값체계를 일원화") — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002); [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]
- First round (base date 1 January 1990): 24.17 million privately owned parcels (76% of all parcels; untaxed state land excluded) to be priced by 30 August 1990; 300,000 standard parcels individually appraised; the other 23.87 million priced from standard parcels with a multiplier table (land price comparison table, 토지가격비준표) reflecting road frontage, distance to stations, slope and land use; results deliberated by local land evaluation committees at city/county/district offices and displayed for inspection at neighbourhood offices — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002) [P-news]
- The Ministry of Construction led, with the National Tax Service and local governments; standard parcels were appraised by two or more appraisers; officials expected prices to reach only "80–90% of market"; existing transfer/inheritance/gift tax bases were then 35–40% of market — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002) [P-news]
- Individual prices were finalised on 18 August 1990; NTS staff said official prices "came close to market prices" (about 90%, versus 70–80% for the old special-area standard values) — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]
- Uses from 1990–91: capital gains and gift tax (Sept 1990), inheritance tax (Jan 1991), comprehensive land tax (종합토지세, replacing the Home Affairs Ministry's 시가표준액 from 1991 with a phase-in), excess land value gains tax, development charges, residential land excess charges, compensation for public acquisition, state land disposals, land transaction permits — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]
- Today official prices are used by 67 administrative systems (taxes, health insurance contributions, basic pension, compensation) — [Kyunghyang Shinmun, 20 Mar 2024](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202403200600005); [Bizwatch, 13 Nov 2025](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/real_estate/2025/11/13/0033) [P-news]
- Current process for standard land prices: MOLIT commissions appraisal firms (감정평가법인등) selected by criteria; price-balancing consultations across appraisers, districts, provinces and between land and houses; screening; checks including an external inspection team and local governments; owners' and local governments' opinions; Central Real Estate Price Disclosure Committee (중앙부동산가격공시위원회) review; publication in the Official Gazette; objections within 30 days to the MOLIT minister, answered within 30 days — [KREB, land price disclosure page](https://www.reb.or.kr/reb/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=9771&cntntsId=1168) [P]
- Since August 2012 the Korea Appraisal Board/KREB performs the support functions (formerly done by the Korea Association of Property Appraisers); under Art. 28 of the Act on Public Announcement of Real Estate Values (in force 1 Sept 2016) MOLIT designates it for this — [KREB](https://www.reb.or.kr/reb/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=9771&cntntsId=1168) [P]
- Individual land prices (개별공시지가) and individual house prices are computed by city/county/district governments from the standard prices — [Newsis, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251217_0003444123) [P-news]
- Who values housing: KREB prices apartments (since 2005) and standard detached houses; objections on standard houses go to KREB branches, on standard land to the appraiser in charge — [Newsis, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251217_0003444123) [P-news]; KREB designated price survey body under the 2005 law — [KREB history](https://www.reb.or.kr/reb/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=9682&cntntsId=1138) [P]
- 2026 round: 600,000 standard parcels out of 35.76 million taxable parcels (about 7,700 replaced); standard land +3.35% (2025: +2.89%); Seoul +4.89%, Yongsan-gu +8.80%; standard detached houses +2.51%; realisation rates unchanged (standard land 65.5%, standard houses 53.6%) — [Newsis, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251217_0003444123) [P-news]
- Frequency: annual (base date 1 January) — [KREB](https://www.reb.or.kr/reb/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=9771&cntntsId=1168) [P]. A Korean Wikipedia lead says that from 2000 surveys need not be redone every year where prices barely change — [Korean Wikipedia, 공시지가](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EA%B3%B5%EC%8B%9C%EC%A7%80%EA%B0%80) [U]

**The 1989 "public concept of land" (토지공개념) laws and the Constitutional Court**
- The four instruments launched together in 1990: excess land value gains tax (토지초과이득세, "tochoseh"), residential land ownership ceiling (택지소유상한제), development gains restitution (개발이익환수제, development charges 개발부담금) and the comprehensive land tax (종합토지세) — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002) [P-news]
- **Excess land value gains tax:** taxed the rise in official land prices on idle land (with designated "hot" areas assessed annually) — e.g. in areas where prices rose 50%+ the gain = difference between official prices at 1 January of successive years, less expenses — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]. By December 1993 NTS had assessed 94,177 taxpayers for ₩947.7 billion; about ₩670 billion had been collected; ~200 cases were pending at the Supreme Court — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730003001) [P-news]
- **1994-07-29 ruling:** the full bench (rapporteur, 주심, Justice Kim Yang-kyun) held the Act incompatible with the Constitution (헌법불합치): taxing "unrealised, notional income" was improper, and treating all rented land as idle was unconstitutional; rather than void it immediately (which would create "legal and fiscal confusion"), the Court barred further application until the legislature revised or repealed it; it urged reform of the land-price laws behind it — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730001002) [P-news]. The government said the law would survive in amended form; ruling-party voices wanted abolition — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730001001) [P-news]
- **Residential land ceiling:** owners in the six largest cities including Seoul (1990 report; the 1999 report says "Seoul and the six metropolitan cities") holding more than 200 pyeong (≈661 m²) had two years from 2 March 1990 to use or sell the excess before annual charges, computed with official prices, began on 2 March 1992 — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]
- **1999-04-29 ruling:** the Constitutional Court full bench (rapporteur Justice Shin Chang-eon), on petitions by Mr Jeong and 66 others, held that a ceiling was a legitimate public aim on Korea's scarce land, but 200 pyeong was "too small" and a blanket prohibition "infringes the property right guaranteed by the Constitution"; the law had already been repealed in August 1998; people with pending suits could win, but those who had paid without challenge had no remedy — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Apr 1999](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1999/04/30/19990430023002) [P-news]
- **Development charges:** computed from official prices at completion of a development project — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]; they continue today as a levy on development projects (housing sites, industrial complexes, etc.) — [Korean Wikipedia, 개발부담금](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EA%B0%9C%EB%B0%9C%EB%B6%80%EB%8B%B4%EA%B8%88) [U]

**Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax (종합부동산세)**
- Announced 11 Nov 2004 as part of a holding-tax overhaul: a national surtax on about 60,000 owners whose combined national holdings exceeded thresholds (housing: NTS standard price above ₩900 million); top rate 3% on housing, 1.0–4.0% on land; local property tax base 50% of standard price; annual tax increases capped at 50% — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Nov 2004](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/2004/11/12/20041112001004); [same day, worked examples](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/2004/11/12/20041112003003) [P-news]
- By 2007–08 the housing threshold was ₩600 million and aggregation was by household (from 2006); about 379,000 households paid the housing portion for 2007 — [Seoul Shinmun 14 Nov 2008](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003006); [Seoul Shinmun 14 Nov 2008 (b)](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003004) [P-news]
- **2008-11-13 ruling:** household aggregation unconstitutional (위헌), effective immediately; NTS said it would refund unconstitutional portions — estimated ~₩500 billion to ~160,000 taxpayers for 2006–07; couples could avoid the tax by splitting title (a ₩1.2 billion home held 50/50 fell below the ₩600 million per-person threshold) — [Seoul Shinmun 14 Nov 2008](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003006); [(b)](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003004) [P-news]
- Later changes (not verified in primary sources): Dec 2008 cut (single-home threshold ₩900m; rates 0.5–2%); 2018-09-13 measures raised rates (top 2.7%, 3.2% for multi-home owners; burden cap 150% → 300%); 2020-07-10 measures set 1.2–6% for owners of 3+ homes or 2 in regulated areas, and companies at 6%; 2022 tax reform raised basic deductions to ₩1.2 billion (single-home households) and ₩900 million (others); tax = [(sum of official prices − deduction) × fair market value ratio] × rate, assessed on holdings at 1 June and paid 1–15 December — [Korean Wikipedia, 종합부동산세](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%A2%85%ED%95%A9%EB%B6%80%EB%8F%99%EC%82%B0%EC%84%B8) [U]

**The 2020 realisation roadmap (현실화 계획) and its reversal**
- 3 Nov 2020: MOLIT's plan to raise official prices to 90% of market — about 3 percentage points a year; apartments over 5–10 years by price band, detached houses 7–15 years, land 8 years. Apartments under ₩900m (then 68.1%) to reach 70% by 2023 and 90% by 2030; detached houses under ₩900m (52.4%) to reach 55% by 2023 and 90% by 2035; MOLIT's rationale: realisation rates of 50–70% were low and uneven, causing inequity — [News Tomato, 3 Nov 2020](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=1004797) [P-news]. **Note:** later articles summarise the end-date as 2030 (Bizwatch 2025) or 2035 (Bizwatch 2024); 2035 is the end-date for the slowest category (detached houses).
- 23 Nov 2022: 2023 rates reset to 2020 levels — apartments 69.0% (planned 72.7%), standard houses 53.6%, standard land 65.5%; expected official price falls of 3.5% (apartments), 7.5% (houses) and 8.4% (land); rationale: avoid official prices exceeding transaction prices as the market fell — [Economist (Korea), 23 Nov 2022](https://economist.co.kr/article/view/ecn202211230096) [P-news]
- 21 Nov 2023: First Vice Minister Kim Oh-jin: 2024 rates held at the 2020 level; argued the plan had pushed up high-end homes and land first, widening gaps — [Newsis, 21 Nov 2023](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20231121_0002529516) [P-news]
- 19 Mar 2024: President Yoon: "we will completely abolish the reckless official price realisation plan" — [Bizwatch, 12 Sep 2024](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/real_estate/2024/09/12/0020) [P-news]

**Scenes (Korea)**
- *A multiplier for every road.* The 1990 press explained individual pricing to readers with an example: a standard house plot 500 m from a subway station at ₩6.6 million per pyeong; a similar flat plot 80 m from the station gets × 1.14 for proximity and × 1.52 for being flat — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002) [P-news]
- *"Even the remotest mountain valleys."* In August 1990 the Seoul Shinmun wrote that the government had "put a price on land even in remote mountain areas where nobody had known what land was worth" — [Seoul Shinmun 20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]

### Inferences
- Korea's official land price is a mass-appraisal system: professional appraisals for a sample (300,000 in 1990, 600,000 in 2026) and a statistical/multiplier extrapolation by local governments for tens of millions of parcels. It was created to serve new redistributive land taxes, and its first big test was in court: the 1994 ruling criticised not only taxing unrealised gains but also the weakness of the land-price rules on which the tax depended.
- Korea's "realisation rate" debate is explicitly about the gap between official values and market prices. Raising the rate is a tax rise without a rate change; freezing it while market prices rise still increases official prices (e.g. +9.13% for apartments in 2026) but keeps them at a fixed fraction of market. Since 2023 each government has frozen the ratio rather than either finishing or formally abolishing the 2020 plan.
- Because realisation rates differ by property type and price band, the policy question has shifted from "how high" to "how even": both the 2024 plan and the 2025 hearing stress "balance" (균형성) and small annual adjustments (~1.5%).

## Q4. Taiwan: equalisation of land rights (pingjun diquan) from Sun Yat-sen to 2026 — announced values, self-declaration, purchase at declared price, land value tax, land value increment tax, the gap with market prices and the 2016 combined income tax

### Takeaway
Taiwan's land taxes still follow Sun Yat-sen's formula written into the 1947 Constitution (Art. 143): private land pays tax "according to its value", the state "may purchase it at that value", and unearned increments go to the public through a land value increment tax. Local governments set two official values: an "announced land value" (公告地價) re-assessed every two years (three before 2017) on which owners declare a value within 80–120% for the annual progressive land value tax, and an "announced current land value" (公告土地現值) published every 1 January on which the increment tax is levied when land changes hands. Both sit below market; since 2016 actual sale-price gains on property are also taxed by a separate income tax. In 2025 the land value tax raised NT$98.1 billion (2.6% of national tax revenue) and the increment tax NT$68.3 billion (1.8%).

### Cited Findings

**Doctrine and constitution**
- Sun's programme had four parts: prescribe land values (規定地價), tax by value (照價徵稅), purchase by value (照價收買), and return increments to the public (漲價歸公). Owners would declare their own values; the state could tax at that value or buy at that value, so under-declaring risked purchase and over-declaring raised tax — [Chinese Wikipedia, 平均地權](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%B3%E5%9D%87%E5%9C%B0%E6%AC%8A) (summarising 國父全集 and textbooks) [U, lead]
- 1930 Land Law, promulgated 30 June 1930, in force 1 March 1936 — [law history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=D0060001) [P]; its increment tax was progressive with a top rate said to reach 100% — [Chinese Wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%B3%E5%9D%87%E5%9C%B0%E6%AC%8A) [U]
- Constitution Art. 143: "Land within the territory of the Republic of China belongs to the whole citizenry… Private land shall pay tax according to its value, and the government may purchase it according to its value (私有土地應照價納稅，政府並得照價收買)… Where the value of land has increased not through labour or capital, the State shall levy a land value increment tax, to be shared by the people (應由國家徵收土地增值稅，歸人民共享之)." It also directs land policy to favour owner-farmers — [Laws & Regulations Database, Constitution](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=A0000001) [P]

**The Equalisation of Land Rights Act and its valuation machinery**
- Enacted 26 Aug 1954 as the urban Act (47 articles); full revisions 1958, 1964, 1968; renamed and extended 2 Feb 1977 (87 articles) — [law history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]. Taiwan Province announced land values and implemented urban equalisation from July 1964 — [Chinese Wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%B3%E5%9D%87%E5%9C%B0%E6%AC%8A) [U]
- **Who values and how (Art. 15):** municipal/county authorities (1) survey land sale prices or income values of the last year by district, (2) draw land value zones and estimate zone values, submitted to the Land Value Evaluation Committee (地價評議委員會), (3) compute unit values per parcel, (4) announce values and receive owners' declarations (30 days), (5) compile land value registers — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- **How often (Art. 14):** "after land values are prescribed, they shall be re-prescribed once every two years, but may be extended when necessary" (wording since the 10 May 2017 amendment of Arts. 14 and 17) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009); [law history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]. Before 2017 the cycle was three years, and during review some legislators argued for annual re-assessment to track prices — Legislative Yuan committee record (Gazette vol. 106), known to me only from a search-engine summary; underlying page not opened [S/U]
- **Self-declared value within a band (Art. 16):** owners who do not declare are deemed to declare 80% of the announced land value; declarations above 120% are cut to 120%; declarations below 80% allow the government to "purchase at the declared price" (照價收買 zhaojia shoumai) or to treat 80% as the declared value — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- **Purchase at declared price:** procedures in Arts. 28–34 (public notice, 50 days to surrender title deeds, payment within 30 days, compensation for improvements and crops, buildings bought with the land); purchase under Art. 16 must be noticed before that year's land value tax is levied; purchase price = declared value (Art. 16 cases) or announced current value (vacant/idle land cases) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]. Purchase is also a sanction for vacant urban land not built on by a deadline (Art. 26, alternative to a 2–5× vacant land tax) and for idle farmland (Art. 26-1) — [same](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]. Under the Land Tax Act, if a declared transfer value is below the announced current value, authorities may buy at the declared value or tax on the announced value — [Land Tax Act Art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- **Announced current land value (公告土地現值, Art. 46):** governments "shall regularly survey land value movements", draw zone maps and estimate zone values, have them evaluated by the Land Value Evaluation Committee, and publish a current-value table every 1 January, used as reference for declared transfer values and as the basis for checking transfers and for compensation on expropriation — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- Statutory link to market: "announced current land values shall not be lower than a certain proportion of normal transaction values" (Equalisation Act Art. 40); "announced current land values shall be adjusted to normal transaction prices; when the national average reaches 90% or more of normal transaction prices, the rates… shall be reviewed" (Land Tax Act Art. 33, rate article amended 30 Jan 2005) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009); [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096); [Land Tax Act history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- Actual-price registration: buyers and sellers must register transaction prices, but "registered transaction prices may become a tax basis only after supporting measures are fully established and legislation is completed" (Art. 47) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]

**Land value tax (地價稅 dijia shui)**
- Charged annually on the aggregate declared value of all a person's land in each municipality/county (Land Tax Act Arts. 14–15); agricultural land and land without prescribed values pay the farmland tax (田賦 tianfu) instead (Art. 22) — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- Rates (Art. 16): basic 1%; above the "progression starting value" (average value of 7 are, i.e. 700 m², of land in the locality, excluding industrial, mining, agricultural and exempt land) 1.5% up to 5×, 2.5% (5–10×), 3.5% (10–15×), 4.5% (15–20×), 5.5% (over 20×) — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- Self-occupied residential land: 0.2% on up to 3 are (urban) or 7 are (non-urban), one site per family (Land Tax Act Art. 17) — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]. **Conflict:** the Equalisation Act's parallel Art. 20 still says 0.3% (千分之三) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]; which governs in practice is not confirmed in sources I opened (0.2% is the figure usually quoted) [U].
- Industrial/mining land, private parks, temples, petrol stations: flat 1% (Art. 18); reserved public-facility land in use: 0.6% (Art. 19); vacant land surcharge 2–5 × basic tax (Art. 21) — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- Hardship relief when announced values are raised: deferral up to 6 months or instalments up to a year (Equalisation Act Art. 17, 2017) — [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]

**Land value increment tax (土地增值稅 tudi zengzhi shui)**
- Levied at transfer (or creation of dian rights) on the "total increase" = transfer value (checked against the announced current value) − original prescribed value or previous transfer value (adjusted by the price index) − improvement costs; inheritance exempt; extra land value tax paid because of re-assessment can be credited — [Land Tax Act Arts. 28, 30–32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096); [Equalisation Act Arts. 36, 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- The statutory "original prescribed value" is the value prescribed in 1964 (or the first prescription for land valued earlier under the Land Law or later) — [Equalisation Act Art. 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- Rates (Art. 33; Equalisation Act Art. 40): 20% on increases under 100% of the base value; 30% on the part between 100% and 200%; 40% above 200%; central government compensates local governments for revenue lost by the rate cut — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]. Long-holding reductions exist (not extracted) [U].
- Self-occupied home: 10% on up to 3 are (urban)/7 are (non-urban), once per lifetime, with a narrower repeat option (≤1.5/3.5 are, no other home, held ≥6 years) — [Land Tax Act Art. 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]
- Exemptions: expropriated land and voluntary sales to acquiring bodies; reserved public-facility land before expropriation; 40% reduction on first transfer after land readjustment — [Land Tax Act Arts. 39, 39-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096) [P]

**Combined housing-and-land income tax (房地合一稅), 2016 and 2.0 (2021)**
- For property acquired from 1 Jan 2016, gains on houses and land are taxed on actual sale price minus cost, separately from other income; the gain is reduced by the land increment already measured on announced current values (to avoid double taxation with the increment tax) — [Income Tax Act Arts. 4-4, 14-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340003); [history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=G0340003) [P]
- Current resident rates (after 2.0, in force 1 July 2021): 45% if held ≤2 years; 35% for 2–5 years; 20% for 5–10 years; 15% over 10 years; 10% on owner-occupied homes for gains above NT$4 million (gains up to NT$4 million exempt); 20% for involuntary sales within 5 years; non-residents 45%/35% — [Income Tax Act Art. 14-4, 4-5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340003) [P]

**Scenes (Taiwan)**
- *Sun Yat-sen's self-enforcing price, 1912.* In an April 1912 farewell speech to the Tongmenghui in Nanjing, Sun argued that if deeds stated that the state could buy at the declared price, owners would not inflate their price (they would pay high tax every year) nor deflate it (the state might buy): "with these two methods working together, there is no need to fix the price — the price fixes itself" (不必定價而價自定矣) — [Chinese Wikipedia, 平均地權](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%B3%E5%9D%87%E5%9C%B0%E6%AC%8A) (quoting 國父全集) [U, lead]
- *Chiang's telegram, 1951.* On 8 February 1951 Chiang Kai-shek wired Premier Chen Cheng that, after two years of rent reduction, "this year the land tax should be reformed according to the principle of equalisation of land rights… as a central task of provincial government" — [Chinese Wikipedia, 平均地權](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%B3%E5%9D%87%E5%9C%B0%E6%AC%8A) [U, lead quoting the telegram]

### Inferences
- Taiwan is the clearest surviving example of a statutory self-assessment-plus-purchase-option design, but in practice the self-assessment is anchored to a government valuation: owners can only move within 80–120% of the announced value, silence defaults to 80%, and the purchase threat applies only below 80%. The default-80% rule means most land value tax is charged on less than the already-below-market announced value.
- The statute itself concedes the gap with market prices: the Land Tax Act requires announced current values to be adjusted toward normal transaction prices and promises a rate review once they reach 90%, and the Equalisation Act bars using registered real transaction prices for tax until further legislation. The 2016 income tax on actual gains is the route by which Taiwan taxed real market gains without dismantling the announced-value system.
- The jump in land value tax from NT$71.1bn (2015) to NT$93.9bn (2016) is consistent with a large upward re-assessment of announced land values effective 2016; I did not find an official statement confirming the 2016 re-assessment size [inference].
- The slide in increment tax (from 5.1% of taxes in 2014 to 1.8% in 2025) reflects both fewer and smaller transfers and the shift of property-gains taxation to the combined income tax, which exceeded the increment tax from 2024 [computed].

## Q5. Across the three: who values land, how often, how official values relate to market prices, and what happened when governments tried to move official values towards market prices

### Takeaway
All three countries tax land on administratively produced values rather than on transaction prices, and all three keep those values deliberately below market: Japan by fixed discounts (≈80% for inheritance, ≈70% for fixed asset tax) against a 100% "published price"; Korea by "realisation rates" of about 54–69%; Taiwan by announced values plus an 80% default declaration. Each has tried to close the gap — Japan in 1898, 1910 and 1991–94; Korea in 1990 and 2020; Taiwan by statute in 2005 and through actual-price registration and the 2016 income tax — and each push produced phase-ins, court challenges or reversals.

**Episodes of moving official values toward market — and what followed**
- Japan 1898: government argued ledger values were a half to a tenth of sale prices, but the Diet accepted only a time-limited rate rise plus a (downward) revision of field values — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- Japan 1910: residential values reset to rental value; values ×4.44 on average but total tax unchanged (redistribution, not a tax rise); politics moved into elected valuation committees — [NTA essay](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/network/302.htm) [P]
- Japan 1991–1997: land value tax introduced at the bubble's end (revenue peaked FY1993 then fell by two-thirds); fixed-asset values lifted to 70% of published prices in FY1994 with years of burden adjustment; the land value tax suspended in 1998 and the special land holding tax in 2003 — [Cabinet Office 2002](https://www.cao.go.jp/zei-cho/history/1996-2009/gijiroku/kiso/2002/pdf/kiso14i.pdf); [MIC](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html); [MLIT 2003](https://www.mlit.go.jp/yosan/yosan03/zeisei03g/zeisei03g_.html) [P]
- Korea 1990: switching tax bases from values at 35–40% (or 70–80%) of market to official prices around 80–90% was expected to make capital gains, gift and inheritance taxes "much heavier" — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002); [20 Aug 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/08/20/19900820005001) [P-news]. The taxes most dependent on official values (the excess land gains tax) were struck down in 1994 partly for reliance on deficient land price rules — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730001002) [P-news]
- Korea 2020–2026: 90% roadmap (2020) → rollback to 2020 levels for 2023 → freeze 2024 → abolition pledge and rationalisation plan (2024) → fourth-year freeze (2026) — sources in Q3 [P-news/P]
- Taiwan: statute requires announced current values to approach normal prices (Land Tax Act Art. 33 since 2005); actual-price registration since 2012 is barred from use as a tax base without further legislation (Art. 47); the political solution was a separate income tax on actual gains (2016, tightened 2021) — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096); [Equalisation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009); [Income Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340003) [P]

**Plain-language concepts for newcomers**
- *Assessed (official) value vs market value.* A market value is what a willing buyer would pay. An official value is the number a government agency writes down for tax purposes. In all three countries the official number is produced by appraisers or officials, on a set date, and is usually below the market value.
- *Capitalised value (Meiji chika).* If a field yields a net income of ¥2.45 a year and money earns 6% a year, the field is "worth" ¥2.45 ÷ 0.06 ≈ ¥40.80. Meiji Japan taxed that computed value, not a sale price — [Kotobank (Nipponica)](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E7%A7%9F%E6%94%B9%E6%AD%A3) [S]
- *Rental value basis (賃貸価格).* Taxing land on the annual rent it would fetch. Japan used it for house plots from 1910 and for all land 1931–1950 — [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *Standard sites/parcels and mass appraisal.* Appraisers value a sample of representative plots in detail; every other plot is priced from its nearest sample using adjustment factors (road width, distance to station, shape). Korea calls the factor table a 토지가격비준표 — [Seoul Shinmun 12 Apr 1990](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1990/04/12/19900412005002) [P-news]
- *Route prices (rosenka).* A price per m² is attached to each street segment for a standard plot fronting it; individual plots are adjusted for depth and shape — [Kotobank, 路線価](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B7%AF%E7%B7%9A%E4%BE%A1) [S]; [NTA vol. 2](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/02chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *Realisation rate (현실화율 hyeonsilhwa-yul; also 시세반영률 "market reflection rate").* Official price ÷ estimated market price. Raising it raises taxes without changing tax rates — [News Tomato](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=1004797) [P-news]
- *Fair market value ratio (공정시장가액비율).* In Korea a further percentage applied to official prices to get the taxable base for property tax and CREHT — [Korean Wikipedia, 종합부동산세](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%A2%85%ED%95%A9%EB%B6%80%EB%8F%99%EC%82%B0%EC%84%B8) [U]
- *Revaluation cycle and freezing.* The law may require values to be redone every 1, 2, 3 or 10 years; if not redone, values "age" and drift from market. Meiji Japan's five-year rule became a "dead letter" — [NTA vol. 1](https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/01chiso/kaidai.htm) [P]
- *Burden adjustment (負担調整 futan chōsei) and burden level (負担水準).* When assessed values jump, the tax base is raised only a slice each year toward the new value; the "burden level" measures how far along a plot is — [MIC](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/149767_08.html) [P]
- *Self-assessment with a purchase option (申報地價 + 照價收買).* Owners name their land's value; the state taxes that value and may buy the land at it — [Equalisation Act Art. 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- *Announced land value vs announced current land value (公告地價 vs 公告土地現值).* Taiwan's two official values: the first (every two years) for the annual land value tax, the second (every 1 January) for the tax on increments when land is sold — [Equalisation Act Arts. 14, 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0060009) [P]
- *Land value increment tax vs capital gains tax.* The first taxes the rise in the land's official value between two dates; the second taxes the actual profit (sale price minus cost) on land and buildings — [Land Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340096); [Income Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340003) [P]
- *Unrealised gains tax.* Korea's 1989 excess land gains tax taxed increases in official values on idle land before any sale; the Constitutional Court objected to taxing "unrealised, notional income" — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730001002) [P-news]
- *"Incompatible with the Constitution" (헌법불합치) vs "unconstitutional" (위헌).* The first lets the law formally survive (often suspended) until the legislature fixes it; the second voids it at once — [Seoul Shinmun 30 Jul 1994](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/1994/07/30/19940730001002); [Seoul Shinmun 14 Nov 2008](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003004) [P-news]
- *Household vs individual aggregation.* Adding up all property of a household (couple) rather than each person for a progressive tax; struck down for CREHT in 2008 — [Seoul Shinmun 14 Nov 2008](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2008/11/14/20081114003006) [P-news]
- *Acquisition-cost base.* Japan's special land holding tax was charged on what the owner paid, not an official value — [Tokyo Metropolitan Tax Bureau](https://www.tax.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kazei/real_estate/special) [P]

### Inferences
- Valuation is the political choke-point in all three systems. Rates were changed often (Japan 1877, 1898, 1904–05, 1910, 1931; Korea's CREHT many times; Taiwan's increment tax 2002–05), but the hardest step was revaluing land toward market. When it happened it was offset by deliberate discounts (Japan), frozen ratios (Korea) or default under-declaration (Taiwan).
- Each country uses a reference valuation produced by licensed private appraisers (Japan, Korea) or local land value committees (Taiwan, and historically Japan's elected committees of 1910–1927) and then derives tax values from it by formula — a way of keeping appraisal expertise central while making tax values politically adjustable.
- The courts mattered most in Korea, where constitutional review (1994, 1999, 2008) cut back land taxes built on official values; in Japan the courts have mainly policed whether municipalities follow national valuation standards; in Taiwan the constitutional text itself mandates value-based land taxation and the increment tax.
