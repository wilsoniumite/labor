## Q1. The Prussian land tax reform of 1861: assessment by cadastral net yield, and how long the valuations lasted

### Takeaway
On 21 May 1861 Prussia replaced a patchwork of provincial land taxes with one land tax assessed on the estimated cadastral net yield (Reinertrag) of each parcel, plus a separate building tax on buildings' use value. I could confirm the laws and the general method; the commonly cited numbers (a fixed 10-million-taler yield, completion in 1865, a rate near 9.5%) could not be checked in an accessible primary or scholarly source and are marked unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis [P/S].** On 21 May 1861 the Prussian House of Representatives, after years of debate and with the Herrenhaus's agreement, adopted a reform of the land and building tax for the whole Prussian state ("Reformierung der Grund- und Gebäudesteuer für das gesamte Staatsgebiet Preußens") — [Bundestag Wissenschaftliche Dienste, WD 4-3000-026/18 (2018)](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/547158/6ff041fce870e40344bd7424a2863154/WD-4-026-18-pdf.pdf), citing Brands/Gradtke-Hanzsch/Olschewski, "140 Jahre Grundsteuerreform", *Vermessung Brandenburg* 2/2001.
- **Companion instruments [P: titles].** An 1882 official cadastral handbook reprints "Auszug aus den allgemeinen Grundsätzen für die Abschätzung des Reinertrages der Liegenschaften vom 21. Mai 1861 (Gesetzsammlung für 1861, Seite 312)", "Auszug aus dem Gesetze vom 21. Mai 1861, betreffend die Einführung einer allgemeinen Gebäudesteuer (Gesetzsammlung für 1861, Seite 317)", and "Veranlagungsgrundsätze für die Gebäudesteuer vom 1. Februar 1878". The same volume is the "Anweisung vom 25. Oktober 1881 für das Verfahren bei Erneuerung der Karten und Bücher des Grundsteuerkatasters" (Berlin 1882) — [TIB digitised copy](https://goobi.tib.eu/viewer/fullscreen/886575389/379/LOG_0076/); [TIB p. 354](https://goobi.tib.eu/viewer/fullscreen/886575389/354/). This shows the 1861 cadastre was still being maintained and "renewed" 20 years later.
- **Method (what was valued) [S].** German land taxes before the cadastres used rough area measures (Hufe, Morgen). From the 18th century cadastres added "Bonitierung nach Kulturart und Bodenqualität" (grading by type of cultivation and soil quality), and the 19th-century land-tax laws, including Prussia's of 1861, were built on this — [WD 4-3000-026/18](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/547158/6ff041fce870e40344bd7424a2863154/WD-4-026-18-pdf.pdf). So the base was an estimated normal annual net yield of land (a rental/yield concept), not a sale price, and buildings were taxed separately under the Gebäudesteuer.
- **Model for the 1861 cadastre [S].** A special cadastre for Rhineland and Westphalia, finished in 1835 and given force by a law of 21 January 1839, served as the model for the cadastre "jetzt gleichmässig im ganzen Staate" — 1861 source quoted in the search snippet of [a digitised Prussian text](https://lessing.ub.uni-duesseldorf.de/download/pdf/8830472.pdf) (not opened in full; treat as [S]).
- **Later fate [S].** Through the Miquel tax reform of 1891/93 the Prussian land tax was "grundsätzlich den Gemeinden überlassen" (handed to the municipalities) — [WD 4-3000-026/18](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/547158/6ff041fce870e40344bd7424a2863154/WD-4-026-18-pdf.pdf). After 1920 the Länder were obliged to exploit the tax; differing state laws were replaced only by the 1936 Reich Grundsteuergesetz — same source.
- **Prussian municipal option of 1893 [S].** The Kommunalabgabengesetz of July 1893 (§ 25) allowed municipalities special land taxes measured on "gemeiner Wert": for farmland and house plots oriented to net yield, for other land (e.g., building plots) to sale value — [Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69) (citing Freese 1918, pp. 148 ff.). This is the legal hook for later municipal value-based and increment taxes.
- **Literature leads (not accessed) [U].** Specialist cadastral histories exist: Ufer, "Die preußische Grundsteuerreform von 1861 und die Entstehung der Katasterämter"; Unger, "Die preußischen Grundsteuergesetze vom 21. Mai 1861 und das Liegenschaftskataster"; Brügmann (1904) — listed in the [DVW Lower Saxony/Bremen cadastral history bibliography](https://dvw.de/api/assets/downloads/niedersachsen_bremen/geschichte_katasteraemter/nds.-kataleiter---literaturverzeichnis.pdf).

### Inferences
- The 1861 system is a clear example of a yield-based (not sale-price-based) valuation made once by commissions and then left largely fixed; the land tax was a distributed burden rather than a tax on current market value. That the Prussian cadastre still had to be "renewed" (1875 law, 1881 instruction) suggests the maps and books, not the valuation standard, were updated.
- The 1893 Prussian municipal law created a split: state land tax based on old yield estimates was abandoned to municipalities, while municipalities gained the right to tax by current market value, which reformers used for increment taxes after 1900.

### Gaps
- Not verified in any accessible source: the fixed total yield ("Kontingent", commonly given as 10 million taler), the rate as a share of net yield (often given as about 9.5%), the classification tariffs (up to eight quality classes per culture type), completion of the assessment in 1865, the building-tax rates (often given as 4% of use value for dwellings, 2% for commercial buildings) and the 15-year building-tax revision cycle [U]. The primary text (Gesetzsammlung 1861 pp. 253 ff./312/317) was behind bot protection on the TIB viewer.
- How long the 1861 net-yield figures were used: commonly said to have remained the municipal land-tax base in Prussia until the 1920s–1936 reforms and to have survived as a soil-quality indicator until the Reich soil survey of 1934 [U]. Not confirmed.

## Q2. The German land-reform movement (Bodenreform), municipal increment taxes from about 1902–04, and the Reich increment tax of 1911 and its handover in 1913

### Takeaway
The land reformers moved from Henry George's single tax toward a menu of practical measures, above all taxing value increments when land was sold. Hundreds of municipalities adopted increment taxes; the Reich nationalised the tax in 1911 and gave it up in 1913. Its most durable results were constitutional and housing-law texts: Weimar Art. 155 and the 1920 Reich Homestead Act.

### Cited Findings
- **Origins [S].** Adolph Wagner demanded taxation of unearned urban increments in 1877 ([Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69)). Henry George's *Progress and Poverty* appeared in German translation (1880/1891). Michael Flürscheim co-founded a short-lived Land-Liga (1886–87); the Bund für Bodenbesitzreform was founded in September 1888, with George listed as a member; Flürscheim declined the chair, which went to Heinrich Wehberg — same source.
- **Flürscheim's 1889 draft [S].** Registration and valuation of urban plots, values set as five-year averages, deduction of maintenance costs, "Wegsteuerung" (taxing away) of the increment, plus a broad municipal purchase right — [Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69).
- **Damaschke and the Bund deutscher Bodenreformer [S].** Damaschke, a Berlin schoolteacher, joined in 1890, became deputy chair in 1893; after mergers with the Kiel "Deutscher Volksbund" the body was renamed "Bund deutscher Bodenreformer" in 1898, with Damaschke elected chair, a post he held until his death in 1935. The 1898 programme called for planned expansion of municipal land ownership and a housing law against speculative use of land. The single-tax goal was dropped for "realitätsnähere Maßnahmen": the increment tax became the main demand, together with public land purchase (with compensation), Erbbaurecht (ground leases), repurchase rights and limits on mortgage credit — [Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69). Heinrich Freese chaired the earlier Deutscher Bund für Bodenbesitzreform 1890–98 — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- **Municipal increment taxes [S].** Before the Reich law of 14 February 1911 "rund 470 deutsche Gemeinden" levied increment taxes. They were triggered by a change of ownership and compared the sale price with the earlier purchase price; part of the gain was exempt; rates were progressive and varied by city; often the tax applied only if the land had changed hands less than 5 years earlier (built land) or 10 years (unbuilt land), the longer period aiming at speculation in vacant land — [Bundestag WD 4-3000-133/20 (2020)](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf), citing Mönter/Ziemer, IFSt-Brief 141 (1973) and Troll (1972).
  - Start date: Matzat says increment taxes were introduced "ab 1902/03 in einer ganzen Reihe deutscher Gemeinden" and that the Bund used the Kiautschou ordinance to promote them — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf) [S]. The frequently repeated claim that Frankfurt am Main (Mayor Adickes) introduced the first big-city increment tax in 1904 and Cologne in 1905 came to me only via an advocacy wiki [U]. A figure of 650 municipalities by 1911 also circulates [U]; sources disagree.
- **Reich increment tax of 14 February 1911 (RGBl. 1911 p. 33) [S].**
  - *What was taxed:* the gain realised on transfer of ownership. *Valuation:* taxable increment = sale price minus purchase price, adjusted for improvements by the seller and certain compensation — no periodic valuation of land was needed.
  - *Exemptions:* transfers of built property up to 5,000 Mark sale price where seller and spouse had income up to 2,000 Mark and were not professional dealers; transfers on death; other cases.
  - *Rates:* progressive. The WD paper's sentence reads "Der Steuersatz betrug 10 Prozent bei einer Wertsteigerung von mehr als 290 Prozent", which looks garbled; the usual description is 10% rising to 30% for increments above 290% [U].
  - *Revenue sharing:* 50% Reich, normally 10% state, 40% municipality — all from [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
  - *Revenue:* not confirmed. A figure of 20.8 million Mark for 1912, 0.7% of Reich taxes in 1911, and "55% of Württemberg municipalities with no yield in 1911–12" appeared only in search snippets from unclear/advocacy sources [U].
- **Withdrawal in 1913 [S].** "Die Zuwachssteuer erfüllte offenbar nicht die vom Reich in sie gesetzten Erwartungen": with the Gesetz über Änderungen im Finanzwesen of 3 July 1913 (RGBl. p. 521) the Reich withdrew; the Act was not repealed, but the Reich share was dropped or temporarily collected for municipalities that had had their own increment tax; states could revive pre-1911 municipal statutes or legislate differently. The Besitzsteuergesetz of the same date (RGBl. p. 524) instead taxed increases in total net wealth over three-year periods from 1 Jan 1914, valuing land at cost or capitalised yield; rates 0.75%–1.5% plus surcharges — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **Scholarly assessment [S, abstract only].** Anna Grotegut, "Gegen Immobilienspekulation und steigende Mieten? Die Wertzuwachssteuer im Deutschen Reich 1911–1913", *Jahrbuch für Wirtschaftsgeschichte* 63(1) (2022): 169–197, DOI 10.1515/jbwg-2022-0007: the tax "neither fulfilled the land reform goal of combating speculation nor generated enough revenue", but created the first direct fiscal link between Reich and municipalities; a changed political landscape enabled a property-type tax that many thought made the increment tax unnecessary — abstract via [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bpj:jbwige:v:63:y:2022:i:1:p:169-197:n:7). Full text blocked (Access log).
- **Afterlife of increment taxation [S].** 1923: the Finanzausgleichsgesetz § 16 required increments to be computed on inflation-adjusted marks; an attempt (1923/1925) to skim inflation gains on sales of land acquired 1919–1924 failed for lack of a firm base. In 1944 the Zuwachssteuergesetz was repealed and replaced by a 2% surcharge on real-estate transfer tax, "damit war praktisch der Gedanke einer besonderen Besteuerung des Bodenwertzuwachses aufgegeben" — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **Constitutional and housing-law results [S].** In 1919 a cross-party group placed Art. 155 in the Weimar Constitution, including: "Die Wertsteigerung des Bodens, die ohne eine Arbeits- oder Kapitalaufwendung auf das Grundstück entsteht, ist für die Gesamtheit nutzbar zu machen." The Reichsheimstättengesetz followed on 10 May 1920 (RGBl. 1920, 962–970): owner-occupied homes, mostly on ground-lease land, with garden plots, protection against forced sale and a pre-emption right for the issuing body to prevent speculative resale — [Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69). Diefenbacher calls Art. 155 "vielleicht [der] bedeutendste[] Erfolg" of the Bund.
- **End under National Socialism [S].** The NSDAP programme (point 17) demanded a "Bodenreform" of its own; Darré attacked the Bund in 1931; the Reichserbhofgesetz's hereditary-farm rules had nothing in common with the Bund's ideas; Damaschke made rhetorical compromises before his death in 1935; the Bund ceased activity in 1942; a post-war revival ("Bund für Land und Freiheit") remained insignificant — [Diefenbacher 2022](https://doi.org/10.3790/vjh.91.1.69).

### Inferences
- The German movement's main practical contribution to valuation was to shift the debate from annual taxation of assessed land value (George) to taxing realised gains at sale, which needs no cadastral valuation at all, only recorded prices. That made it easier to adopt locally but tied its yield to the property market's turnover.
- The rapid 1911 to 1913 sequence shows how a Reich-level land-increment tax could be displaced by broader wealth-increase taxes when the Reich needed revenue for armaments (the 1913 package accompanied the Wehrbeitrag). This is my reading, consistent with Grotegut's abstract.

### Gaps
- Membership figures of the Bund deutscher Bodenreformer over time were not found in an accessible source [U].
- Exact dates and rates of the first municipal increment taxes (Frankfurt, Cologne, Kiel, etc.) are unresolved; the IFSt 1973 brief that the Bundestag paper relies on was blocked.
- Reich increment tax revenue 1911–1913 and its share of Reich revenue are unverified.
- Erbbaurecht: the 1919 Erbbaurechtsverordnung is commonly linked to the reformers [U]; not verified here.

## Q3. Kiautschou (Qingdao), 1898–1914: Schrameier's land ordinance, how land was valued, revenue, and practice versus later promotion

### Takeaway
The German naval administration in Tsingtau bought up land at frozen prices, resold building plots by auction with building obligations, levied an annual 6% tax on assessed land value (excluding improvements) and took one-third of net resale gains (or of the value increase after 25 years without sale). Land reformers and Georgists promoted it as applied single tax and a model for Sun Yat-sen and Taiwan. Historians and contemporaries dispute its motives, its effect on rents and its fiscal weight, and its "Georgist" character. The colony's own revenue, including land revenue, was small next to Reich subsidies.

### Cited Findings
**Origins and authorship**
- On the day of occupation (14 Nov 1897) von Diederichs decreed that Chinese residents could sell land only to the German authority; transfers required the governor's consent — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf); [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727) [S]. Tirpitz annotated his copy of Schrameier's book that this had been "ordered by telegraph from Berlin" — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727) [S].
- Wilhelm Schrameier (1859–1926), a consular interpreter, drafted the Land- und Steuerordnung; his handwritten July 1898 draft with marginal notes by Governor Rosendahl, judge Gelpcke and Intendanturrat Schilasky was found in 1991 in the Qingdao municipal building office archive — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf) [S]. The ordinance was proclaimed on 2 September 1898 — same source; [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm) [S].
- Influence of Henry George is disputed. Silagi (1984, Georgist-funded translation; Robert Schalkenbach Foundation grant) traces a chain from the Bund's journal *Frei Land* (1896) through the Colonial Office to Tirpitz's Navy Office and argues Schrameier knew George's ideas in 1898 while later denying direct influence; Schrameier wrote in 1912 that Kiautschou was "a complete execution of Henry George's principles, but without knowledge of Henry George" — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727). Matzat (1998) rejects Silagi's chain and says the increment tax was not Georgist at all because George opposed increment taxes — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf). Sources disagree; label [S], unresolved.

**What was taxed, how valued, by whom, how often [S]**
- *Land acquisition:* lacking money and staff, Diederichs first secured a pre-emption right, paying owners twice their annual land tax and letting them keep farming; Schrameier began purchases in December 1897 at uniform prices agreed with local leaders, based on what General Zhang had paid for garrison land in 1891–97, with buildings compensated separately — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm).
- *Prices:* in March 1900 the government reported buying land in 1898 at an average 0.05 Mark/m² and selling it (Oct 1898 to spring 1900) at an average 1.744 Mark/m² — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- *Sale:* plots were surveyed before auction, which let planners lay out the city regardless of prior ownership; first auctions followed the 2 Sept 1898 development plan — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm). Buyers had to submit a building plan; Matzat refers to a three-year building deadline — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- *Annual land tax:* 6% per year on land value, re-ascertained at intervals, deducting the value of the owner's improvements (Article 8) — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727). Schrameier's July 1898 draft: in the first three years the price paid to the government is the base; thereafter, "am besten alle 3 Jahre", a valuation derived from sales and land-register notifications, assessed by the Landamt (land office); public streets, squares, buildings and charitable institutions could be exempt — [Matzat 1998, transcription of the draft](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf). Schrameier justified 6% as matching a normal return on capital, low compared with Hong Kong's 13% rent tax and Shanghai's 8–10% rent tax plus 0.4% land tax — same draft.
- *Penalty for non-building:* if a plot was not built on per plan within three years, the land-tax rate rose by 3 points every three years up to 24% — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm).
- *Increment tax:* one-third of the increase in value of "naked" land on transfer, or at least every 25 years if not sold (Articles 6 and 7); on resale, improvements plus 6% interest were deducted to compute the net gain — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727) (also correcting Geiger's 1933 account). Schrameier aimed the 25-year rule notably at the Catholic Steyl mission, which held property indefinitely — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf). A 2% transfer fee also applied — Corbach 1904 in [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- *Rent-tax clause:* § 3 of the proclaimed tax ordinance foresaw possibly converting part of the land tax into a rent tax after 1 Jan 1902; Schrameier said this was added at the last minute under pressure from opinion that saw "eine unerträgliche Last in der 6%igen Grundsteuer" compared with Hong Kong and Shanghai. No rent tax was ever introduced; the land-value tax stayed the only direct tax 1898–1914 — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).

**Revenue [S/P]**
- Corbach (1904), citing the colony's statistics: regular revenues 1901/02 of 232,841.49 Mark, of which land taxes 62,956.87 Mark (about 27%, computed); 1902/03 regular revenues 262,591.73 Mark, of which revenue "aus Grundstücken" 63,961.82 Mark — "noch nicht einmal ein Viertel" — [Matzat 1998 reprint](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf) [S].
- Matzat: in the first ten years the land tax plus land sales were among the most important own revenues (table 1902–09 in Matzat 1992, p. 33, not accessed); "Die Einkünfte aus der Zuwachssteuer waren minimal" — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf) [S].
- Fiscal scale: the 1908 Schutzgebiete budget law lists Kiautschou's own revenues at 1,725,800 Mark against a Reich subsidy of 9,739,953 Mark (total 11,465,753 Mark) — [RGBl. 1908 Nr. 14, pp. 118–130, law of 31 March 1908, via Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesetz,_betreffend_die_Feststellung_des_Haushalts-Etats_f%C3%BCr_die_Schutzgebiete_auf_das_Rechnungsjahr_1908) [P]. Own revenues (all sources) were thus about 15% of the colony's budget (computed).

**Practice and outcomes [S]**
- Warner (architectural historian; dissertation on Tsingtau's planning): speculation was limited, but building regulations raised construction costs; rents in Qingdao were 30–50% higher than in Shanghai despite the ordinance and low land prices, and living costs were among the highest in China; by 1912 about 80 plots (18 ha) had been sold to Chinese buyers; restrictions on Chinese settlement were largely lifted in 1914 — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm).
- Corbach (1904; editor dismissed by the governor): the ordinance's motive "war nie etwas anderes als das nackte finanzielle Interesse der Verwaltung"; by setting minimum prices and withholding the best plots for future fiscal purposes the administration itself conducted "eine Spekulation großen Stiles"; claims of cheap land and rents contradicted the facts — [Matzat 1998 reprint](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- Historians disagree: Mühlhahn (1997, in Leutner's source edition) notes that official sources nowhere mention a social-policy motive; Felber (1991) wrote that the increment tax became the administration's most important revenue — Matzat calls Felber's claim "ein komplettes Mißverständnis" and disputes Mühlhahn, quoting the first official Denkschrift (Oct 1898) that land prices were kept low so that "dem weniger Bemittelten" could buy land — all as reported in [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
- Silagi (1984) calls the statute "extraordinarily successful", citing Schrameier's own book; he also reports a Reichstag resolution of 13 May 1914 asking that expropriated land in Cameroon be administered "according to the Kiaochow Land Statute" (citing Damaschke's memoir) — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727) [S; partly self-reporting by protagonists].

**Promotion and legacy [S]**
- Damaschke called the ordinance a "Geschenk des Himmels"; Tirpitz defended it in the Reichstag (31 Jan 1899); Damaschke's March 1900 pamphlet publicised it — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm). Poultney Bigelow told the International Geographical Congress (3 Oct 1899) that George's principles had been put into practice "for the first time" — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727).
- Schrameier retired in 1910; in 1924 he went to Canton as land-tax adviser at Sun Yat-sen's invitation and died there in a traffic accident in 1926 — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727). The Guomindang's 1930 land law drew on his programme and was later applied in Taiwan — [Warner, DHM](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/tsingtau/katalog/auf1_6.htm); Matzat says its principles have applied in Taiwan since 1954 — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).

### Inferences
- Valuation in Kiautschou was simple because the state was the first seller: the auction price became the initial assessed value, later values were to be inferred from recorded sales. This is a "public land bank + sale-price assessment" model rather than a cadastral appraisal of an existing private market.
- The fiscal story and the promotional story diverge: land taxes were a meaningful share of the colony's small own revenue (about a quarter of regular revenue in 1901–03), but the colony as a whole depended on Reich subsidies roughly six times its own revenue (1908), and the increment tax produced little. The strongest evidence for "success" (Silagi, Schrameier, Damaschke) comes from protagonists and reformers; the strongest evidence against (Corbach, Warner on rents) comes from a hostile contemporary and from a later historian. Effects on speculation and rents remain disputed.

### Gaps
- Not accessed: Schrameier, *Aus Kiautschous Verwaltung* (Jena 1914) and *Kiautschou, seine Entwicklung und Bedeutung* (Berlin 1915); Mühlhahn, *Herrschaft und Widerstand in der "Musterkolonie" Kiautschou* (Munich 2000); Schrecker, *Imperialism and Chinese Nationalism: Germany in Shantung* (1971); the official annual Denkschriften to the Reichstag with land-sale and tax tables; Matzat 1992's 1902–09 revenue table. Revenue series by year and total area sold remain unconfirmed.
- Exact reassessment practice after 1901 (how often the Landamt actually revalued, and on what evidence) is unverified.

## Q4. The Weimar house-rent tax (Hauszinssteuer, 1924): capturing owners' inflation gains

### Takeaway
After the 1923 hyperinflation wiped out most mortgage debt on pre-war buildings, the Reich required the states (from 1 April 1924) to tax built property to recoup part of owners' windfall; the base was the frozen 1914 "peace rent" or a multiple of the state property tax. It became a mainstay of state and municipal budgets and of Weimar social housing finance, outlived its planned end in 1926, and was bought out in 1942/43.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis [S].** Third Emergency Tax Decree of 14 Feb 1924 (RGBl. p. 74), issued under the enabling law of 8 Dec 1923, regulating at the same time the revaluation (Aufwertung) of mortgages and a "Geldentwertungsausgleich" (inflation-loss compensation) for the states; states (and, by state law, municipalities) had to levy a tax on built property (§§ 26–32) — [Bundestag WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **Rationale [S].** The tax rested on "der Gedanke ..., daß die Beschränkung der Hypothekenaufwertung dem Hausbesitzer gewisse Vorteile gebracht habe, weil er sein in bebautem Grundbesitz angelegtes Vermögen durch die Inflationszeit hindurch zu einem erheblichen Teil habe erhalten können", and was to fund cheap mortgages for new housing — Fabricius (1942) quoted in [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **Base and valuation [S].** Two forms: a special revaluation tax as a percentage of the "Friedensmiete" (the gold-mark rent agreed for the tenancy beginning 1 July 1914), to be passed on to tenants under state rent rules; or a surcharge on the property tax, computed separately. Rents were to rise gradually toward peace-rent levels; owners had to retain at least 30% of the peace rent from 1 April 1924; buildings ready after 1 July 1918 were exempt unless publicly subsidised; the rules were to expire on 31 March 1926 — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf). (Valuation note: the base was a 1914 rent frozen in gold marks, not a current value.)
- **Rates [S].** In 1925 all states except Württemberg levied it, from 16% of peace rent (Waldeck) to 36% (Bavaria); in 1928 from 20% (Bremen) to 51% (Saxony) (Führer 1995). In Prussia it was generally 1,200% of the Grundvermögensteuer (2.40 Mark per 1,000 Mark of value), equal to about 48% of peace rent, staggered by mortgage burden from 375% to 1,200%; 960% from the 21 Oct 1932 ordinance — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf). Names: Hauszinssteuer (Prussia, Bavaria), Mietzinssteuer (Hamburg), Aufwertungsteuer (Saxony), Gebäudesondersteuer (Baden); these five states accounted for 90% of yield — same source.
- **Revenue and use [S].** "Die gesamten Einnahmen der Länder und Gemeinden wurden zu über 10%, am Ende des Jahrzehnts sogar zu 20% aus dieser Steuer bestritten"; it financed 35.3% of the 1.9 billion RM of housing investment in 1926, 29% of 2.4 billion RM in 1930, and only 6.3% of 0.8 billion RM in 1932 (Häußermann/Siebel 1996); about 6 billion RM in subsidised mortgages in total (Fabricius 1942); earmarking for housing ended in 1931 — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf). Annual yield about 850 million RM (Fabricius 1942, quoted ibid.).
- **Abolition [S].** A 1935 housing law forced owners to lend the planned reduction to the Reich; the Nazi promise to abolish the tax was broken (Haerendel 1999). The decree of 31 July 1942 (RGBl. p. 501) abolished it as the last state tax: owners paid a buy-out of ten times the annual amount, treating them "als wenn die Steuer erst im Jahre 1956 ausliefe" (32 years, 1924–1956), bringing the Reich "gegen 8 Mrd. RM" for war finance; tax offices had been booking monthly payments for about 3 million properties — Fabricius (1942) quoted in [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf). Effective date 1 Jan 1943 and buy-out of about 8.1 billion RM per Wikipedia lead [U].

### Inferences
- The Hauszinssteuer is an example of a tax that captured a one-off windfall (debt relief by inflation) through a recurring levy tied to a frozen historic base (1914 rents), which then turned into a general revenue source. Its complexity came from having to be reconciled with rent control, mortgage revaluation and differing state land-tax bases.

### Gaps
- Mortgage revaluation rates (15% in 1924, 25% under the 1925 Aufwertungsgesetz) [U]; the 1926 extension law (RGBl. 1926 p. 251, per Wikipedia lead) [U]; a full annual revenue series and share of GDP not found.

## Q5. The unit value system (Einheitswerte; Reichsbewertungsgesetz 1934): base years 1935 and 1964, why never updated, and the consequences

### Takeaway
Germany intended to reassess all property every six years at approximate market value, but did so only twice (1935 and 1964). The 1964 values were applied only from 1974 and then frozen in the West until 2024; the East kept 1935 values plus a floor-area substitute. Courts finally held that the frozen values had become arbitrary.

### Cited Findings
- **Design [P].** Unit values were set under the Valuation Act for several taxes, latterly mainly for the Grundsteuer; § 21(1) BewG provided for general reassessments "in Zeitabständen von je sechs Jahren". A main assessment was made as of 1 January 1935 under the 1934 law; later ones were suspended — [BVerfG 1 BvL 11/14, Rn. 2–4](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html). The target value ("gemeiner Wert") is in substance market value — [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1).
- **1964 assessment [P].** The full revaluation as of 1 Jan 1964 was ordered by the BewÄndG 1965 (13 Aug 1965); it took until the early 1970s and the new values were "erstmals zum 1. Januar 1974 zur Anwendung gebracht" — [BVerfG Rn. 5](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html). Methods for land: §§ 68–94 BewG (income method with multipliers on annual rent, or cost method) — BVerfG Rn. 8.
- **Why never updated [P].** The law of 22 July 1970 (BGBl. I p. 1118) left the date of the next main assessment to a separate law; "Ein solches Gesetz ist bis heute nicht verabschiedet worden" — [BVerfG press release 21/2018](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html); "für eine neue Hauptfeststellung ... fehlt es ... an einer Rechtsgrundlage" (Rn. 5). Updates thereafter happened only by event-driven Fortschreibungen/Nachfeststellungen, still using 1964 value relationships (Rn. 6; § 27 BewG per [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)); tax offices depended on notices from other authorities because owners had no general duty to report (Rn. 7). The government argued administrative effort; the court rejected this as justification for "Hinausschieben einer neuen Hauptfeststellung um Jahrzehnte" (Rn. 130).
- **East Germany [P].** In the new Länder § 129(1) BewG kept unit values based on 1 January 1935; for rental buildings and single-family houses without a 1935 value, a substitute base under § 42 GrStG applied — [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1); also [BewG § 266(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__266.html), which lists §§ 125, 129 BewG and § 42 GrStG among the rules lapsing 31 Dec 2024. The BVerfG case covered only the West but noted the East kept the 1935 assessment (Rn. 154).
- **Scale and revenue [P].** About 35 million economic units, ~30 million of them Grundvermögen; annual revenue "rund 13 Mrd. Euro"; 2013 revenue €11.024 bn excluding city-states; third-largest municipal revenue after trade tax and income-tax share — [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1). Revenue rose "von 12 auf zuletzt knapp 14 Milliarden €" (BVerfG Rn. 142).
- **Distortions [P].** Examples in the judgment: equipment classes reflect 1 Jan 1964, so "eine thermostatgeregelte Warmwasserheizung mit flüssigen Brennstoffen oder Gas bereits als sehr gute Ausstattung" counts (Rn. 125); post-war buildings are in the newest age group and get "den gleichen Vervielfältiger ... wie ein Gebäude aus dem Jahr 1950" (Rn. 122); more than half of housing stock post-dates 1964 (Rn. 39) — [BVerfG](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html). The BFH in its 2014 referrals listed causes: deep changes in building stock and markets, 1964 equipment standards, rapid urban development, no age depreciation since the valuation date in the cost method — [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1).
- **Judicial escalation [P].** BFH "Appellentscheidungen" of 30 June 2010 (BVerfG Rn. 25); BFH referrals of 22 Oct 2014 (II R 16/13; II R 37/14) and 17 Dec 2014 (II R 14/13), holding the rules unconstitutional at the latest from valuation date 1 Jan 2008 — [BR-Drs. 515/16](http://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2016/0501-0600/515-16.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1).

### Inferences
- The unit-value story is the clearest German case of "valuation ageing": the statute promised six-year cycles, practice delivered one update in 50 years, and the frozen base survived because the tax was small per owner and municipalities could compensate with higher multipliers (Hebesätze). It ended through the courts, not through politics.
- The East–West difference (1935 vs 1964) meant two frozen bases ran side by side for 34 years after reunification.

### Gaps
- Other consequences commonly cited: the BVerfG struck down the wealth tax (Vermögensteuer) in 1995 (2 BvL 37/91) and the inheritance-tax valuation in 2006 (1 BvL 10/02), partly because of outdated unit values [U]; not verified here. Typical ratio of 1964 unit values to market values not found in a primary source. The 1934 Reichsbewertungsgesetz date (16 Oct 1934) and 1936 Grundsteuergesetz date (1 Dec 1936) are from background knowledge [U]; the years are confirmed.

## Q6. Grundsteuer C: the 1961–62 tax on undeveloped building land, its quick abolition, and the municipal option from 2025

### Takeaway
West Germany tried a steeply rising land tax on vacant building-ready plots in 1961–62 when it freed land prices after a 24-year price freeze; it was abolished in 1964 (retroactive to 1963) as ineffective and constitutionally doubtful. A milder municipal option returned in 2025, and in its first year it raised only €5.8 million nationwide, two-thirds of it in Hamburg.

### Cited Findings
- **Background [S].** After the price-stop decree of 26 Nov 1936 there was for over 20 years no open land market, but a "grauer Markt"; the Bundesbaugesetz of 23 June 1960 (BGBl. I p. 341) was meant to curb "Bodenwucher" while lifting the price stop — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **Design 1961 [S].** New §§ 12a–c GrStG: the tax-base rate (Steuermesszahl) for vacant building-ready plots, "regulär 10 ‰", rose to 20‰ for the first two years after building readiness, 25‰ for the next two and 30‰ thereafter; if the plot was built on, the higher tax for two years before was refunded — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf). A Wikipedia lead gives the regular rate as 5‰ [U] — sources disagree.
- **Abolition [S].** In force from 1 Jan 1961 "gut ein Jahr" when an FDP motion led to the Gesetz zur Änderung grundsteuerlicher Vorschriften of 10 June 1964 (BGBl. I p. 347), abolishing it from 1 Jan 1963, because it was "kein geeignetes Mittel ..., um den Baulandmarkt in dem gewünschten Sinne zu beeinflussen" (Finance Committee report of 23 April 1964, on BT-Drs. IV/2142) and because of constitutional doubts, which the BFH later did not confirm (judgment of 19 April 1968, III R 78/67) — [WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
- **2019 reintroduction [P].** Coalition bill BT-Drs. 19/11086 (25 June 2019): plots are held as "Spekulationsobjekt" in tight markets; municipalities may set a separate, higher, uniform Hebesatz on building-ready land; the bill notes the precedent: "Eine gesonderte Besteuerung baureifer Grundstücke erfolgte bereits im Zeitraum 1961/1962 durch § 12a GrStG" — [BT-Drs. 19/11086](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/19/110/1911086.pdf). The draft limited it to "Gebieten mit besonderem Wohnraumbedarf"; the enacted § 25(5) GrStG instead allows it "aus städtebaulichen Gründen" (housing/workplace needs, infill, inner development), possibly limited to a part of the municipality covering at least 10% of its area with several such plots, designated by public general order with a map and reasons; the rate must be uniform and higher than the ordinary B rate — [GrStG § 25](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__25.html) [P]. Applies from the 2025 main assessment — [GrStG § 37(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__37.html) [P].
- **State variations [P].** Bavaria excludes it: "§ 25 Abs. 5 GrStG findet keine Anwendung" — [BayGrStG Art. 5(2)](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayGrStG/true). Hesse allows the C rate to be graded by the duration of building readiness, with a cap — [Hessian tax administration](https://finanzamt.hessen.de/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-c-in-hessen). Hamburg's C rate is 8,000% — [Haufe, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.haufe.de/steuern/finanzverwaltung/grundsteuer-in-den-verschiedenen-bundeslaendern_164_626100.html) [S].
- **Uptake 2025 [P].** "Die Möglichkeit, eine Grundsteuer C ... festzusetzen, haben nur wenige Gemeinden genutzt"; revenue €5.8 million in 2025 — [Destatis press release 369, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/10/PD26_369_712.html). By state (thousand €): Hamburg 3,965.5; NRW 900.6; Baden-Württemberg 831.8; Hesse 73.0; Rhineland-Palatinate 4.8; total 5,775.6 — [Destatis Realsteuervergleich 2025, table 71231-01](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Staat/Steuern/Steuereinnahmen/Publikationen/Downloads-Realsteuern/statistischer-bericht-realsteuervergleich-2141010257005.xlsx?__blob=publicationFile&v=3).

### Inferences
- Both German experiments targeted withheld building land rather than land value as such. The 1960s version was a nationwide automatic surcharge; the 2025 version is optional, local and procedurally demanding (maps, published reasons), which may explain the low uptake. Whether it mobilises land is not yet measurable from the data found.

### Gaps
- Revenue of the 1961–62 Baulandsteuer and evidence of its effect on land supply were not found. The number of municipalities using Grundsteuer C in 2025–26 was not found (only revenue).

## Q7. The Federal Constitutional Court judgment of 10 April 2018 (1 BvL 11/14 and related cases): reasoning and deadlines

### Takeaway
The court held the unit-value rules for West German property incompatible with equal treatment (Art. 3(1) GG) at least since 2002, because freezing values at 1964 produced serious and widespread distortions that administrative convenience, low tax amounts or typification could not justify. It allowed the old rules until a new law (deadline 31 Dec 2019) and then for five more years, at most to 31 Dec 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Cases [P].** 1 BvL 11/14, 1 BvL 12/14, 1 BvL 1/15 (BFH referrals), 1 BvR 639/11, 1 BvR 889/12 (constitutional complaints); First Senate; press release 21/2018 — [BVerfG press release](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html).
- **Holding [P].** §§ 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 27, 76, 79(5), 93(1) sentence 2 BewG (in connection with the 1970 suspension) "sind jedenfalls seit dem Beginn des Jahres 2002 mit dem allgemeinen Gleichheitssatz unvereinbar"; holding the 1964 date "führt zu gravierenden und umfassenden Ungleichbehandlungen bei der Bewertung von Grundvermögen" — [press release](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html); the list of provisions is mirrored in [BewG § 266(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__266.html).
- **Headnotes [P].** The legislature has wide latitude in choosing the tax base, but valuation rules must capture values realistically and in proportion; unrealistic rules cannot be justified by "die Vermeidung eines noch so großen Verwaltungsaufwands", nor by a low burden; suspending periodic main assessments since 1964 leads to unjustified unequal treatment — [BVerfG judgment](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html).
- **Reasoning details [P].** Distortions reach the core of valuation, not just atypical cases (Rn. 128, 137); low absolute burden, general undervaluation, supplementary assessments and Hebesatz changes do not compensate (Rn. 141–145); the tax matters because of its size (Rn. 142) — [BVerfG](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html).
- **Deadlines [P].** "spätestens bis zum 31. Dezember 2019 eine Neuregelung zu treffen"; after promulgation the old rules may be applied for five more years, "längstens aber bis zum 31. Dezember 2024" (Tenor no. 2) — [press release](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html). Reasons: risk that "viele Gemeinden ohne die Einnahmen aus der Grundsteuer in gravierende Haushaltsprobleme gerieten" (Rn. 173), and the need to revalue about 35 million units, for which the government cited a minimum implementation time of six years (Rn. 178) — [BVerfG](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html).

### Inferences
- The judgment did not prescribe a method (land value, building value, area), only that whatever base is chosen must be applied consistently and kept realistic. That neutrality is what later allowed the Länder to choose very different models.

### Gaps
- None material for this question.

## Q8. The 2019 reform: the federal model and the state models

### Takeaway
In November 2019 the Basic Law was amended to give the Federation clear power over the land tax while letting each Land deviate from 2025. The federal model (used by 11 Länder, two of them with their own rate tweaks) values property by a typified income method (statistical rents + standard land values) or cost method, every seven years. Baden-Württemberg taxes land value only; Bavaria taxes area only; Hamburg, Hesse and Lower Saxony tax area adjusted by location.

### Cited Findings
**Constitutional basis [P]**
- Art. 105(2) GG now reads "Der Bund hat die konkurrierende Gesetzgebung über die Grundsteuer"; Art. 72(3) no. 7 lets Länder deviate on "die Grundsteuer"; Art. 125b(3): deviating Land law may apply only "für Zeiträume ab dem 1. Januar 2025" — [GG Art. 105](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gg/art_105.html); [Art. 72](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gg/art_72.html); [Art. 125b](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gg/art_125b.html). Rationale: federal competence under the 1994 version of Art. 72(2) was doubted; the land tax suits Länder autonomy "aufgrund der Immobilität des Steuerobjekts" — [BT-Drs. 19/11084](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/19/110/1911084.pdf). The BFH confirmed Art. 105(2) was inserted in November 2019 before the reform law took effect — [BFH press release 078/25](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/de/presse/pressemeldungen/detail/bundesfinanzhof-haelt-grundsteuer-bundesmodell-fuer-verfassungskonform/).

**Federal model (Bundesmodell) [P]**
- Three steps unchanged: value (Grundsteuerwert) × tax-base rate (Steuermesszahl, per mille) = base amount (Messbetrag) × municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) — [GrStG § 13](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__13.html); [§ 25](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__25.html).
- Valuation: vacant land = area × Bodenrichtwert (standard land value); differences from the reference plot are generally ignored — [BewG § 247](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__247.html). Single/two-family houses, rental buildings and condominiums: income method (capitalised net yield + discounted land value), using statutory net cold rents per m² "nach Land, Gebäudeart, Wohnfläche und Baujahr" with surcharges/discounts by rent-level class (Mietniveaustufe) — [§ 250](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__250.html), [§ 252](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__252.html), [§ 254](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__254.html). Business, mixed-use, part-ownership and other buildings: cost (Sachwert) method — § 250(3). The rents derive from the Mikrozensus; "45 verschiedene Nettokaltmieten" apply in total — [BFH II R 25/24](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/en/entscheidungen/entscheidungen-online/decision-detail/STRE202620008/).
- Rates: 0.34‰ vacant land; 0.31‰ residential (houses, rental buildings, condominiums); 0.34‰ other built property; −25% for subsidised social housing, municipal/non-profit housing companies and cooperatives; −10% for listed monuments — [GrStG § 15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__15.html).
- Revaluation: "in Zeitabständen von je sieben Jahren" — [BewG § 221](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__221.html); first main assessment 1 Jan 2022 for the main tax assessment 1 Jan 2025 — [§ 266(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__266.html); [GrStG § 36](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/grstg_1973/__36.html).
- Safety valve: lower market value applies if the taxpayer proves the Grundsteuerwert exceeds it "um mindestens 40 Prozent" (a sale within a year of the valuation date can serve as proof) — [BewG § 220(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__220.html). (Introduced in 2024 after the 2023–24 court doubts [U on the amending act].)
- Land-level variants of the federal model: Saxony and Saarland use different rates (e.g., heavier on non-residential) [S, values U]; Berlin sets 0.31‰ residential and 0.45‰ for non-residential and vacant land and cut its Hebesatz from 810% to 470%; Bremen 0.31‰ / 0.75‰ with Hebesätze 755% (Bremen city) and 896% (Bremerhaven); NRW (law of 4 July 2024), Saxony-Anhalt and others allow differentiated Hebesätze — [Haufe overview, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.haufe.de/steuern/finanzverwaltung/grundsteuer-in-den-verschiedenen-bundeslaendern_164_626100.html) [S]. Federal-model states: Berlin, NRW, Saxony, Brandenburg, Bremen, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saxony-Anhalt, Saarland, Schleswig-Holstein, Thuringia — [BFH press release 078/25](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/de/presse/pressemeldungen/detail/bundesfinanzhof-haelt-grundsteuer-bundesmodell-fuer-verfassungskonform/).

**Baden-Württemberg: modified land-value model [S/P]**
- Landesgrundsteuergesetz of 4 Nov 2020: Grundsteuerwert = plot area × Bodenrichtwert set by the local Gutachterausschuss; buildings ignored; Messzahl 0.91‰ for usual residential plots — [PwC on FG BW, 26 Jun 2024](https://blogs.pwc.de/de/steuern-und-recht/article/243959/grundsteuer-b-nach-dem-landesgrundsteuergesetz-baden-wuerttemberg-ist-verfassungsmaessig/) [S]. The BFH treated residential use as reflected through a 30% lower rate — [kommunal.de](https://kommunal.de/grundsteuer-Bundesverfassungsgericht) [S] (implying a general rate of 1.30‰; computed, statute not opened).
- Owners may first ask the Gutachterausschuss to correct the land value, then prove a deviation of at least 30% by expert appraisal; appraisals commissioned by 30 June 2025 apply retroactively from 1 Jan 2025; the ministry expects lower tax for flats and multi-family buildings — [BW Finance Ministry FAQ](https://fm.baden-wuerttemberg.de/de/service/presse-und-oeffentlichkeitsarbeit/meldung/pid/die-wichtigsten-fragen-zur-grundsteuer) [P].

**Bavaria: pure area model [P]**
- Bayerisches Grundsteuergesetz of 10 Dec 2021 (GVBl. p. 638). Base amount = land area × €0.04/m² plus building floor area × €0.50/m², each times a Messzahl of 100%, reduced to 70% for residential floor area; land beyond ten times the living area counts at 50% for predominantly residential plots; a degressive formula applies to large mostly unsealed plots over 10,000 m²; further 25% reductions for farm housing, listed buildings and social housing; garages up to 50 m² and small outbuildings up to 30 m² ignored; § 25(5) GrStG (Grundsteuer C) excluded; hardship remission where location deviates strongly from local norms — [BayGrStG Arts. 1–5, 8](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayGrStG/true).

**Hamburg: residential-location model [P/S]**
- Area-based (land and building areas); residential Messzahl reduced to 70%, and in "normale Wohnlage" by a further 25% to 52.5%; classification by the Wohnlagenverzeichnis (first published 10 Sept 2024); Hebesatz 975%; ~430,000 Grundsteuer B cases — [Hamburg Finanzbehörde, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.hamburg.de/politik-und-verwaltung/behoerden/finanzbehoerde/aktuelles/grundsteuer-bleibt-insgesamt-aufkommensneutral-1234162) [P]. Non-residential Messzahl 0.87 — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/steuern/finanzverwaltung/grundsteuer-in-den-verschiedenen-bundeslaendern_164_626100.html) [S]. Statute date (HmbGrStG, Aug 2021) [U].

**Hesse: area-factor model [P]**
- Hessisches Grundsteuergesetz (passed Dec 2021): land €0.04/m²; residential floor area €0.50/m² × 70% = effectively €0.35/m²; non-residential €0.50/m²; the sum is multiplied by a factor comparing the plot's Bodenrichtwert with the municipal average (formula in the law; average values published in Staatsanzeiger 37/2022 of 12 Sept 2022). The model is "wertunabhängig": changes in land values within a municipality have no effect "mindestens bis zur nächsten Hauptveranlagung im Jahr 2036" — [Hessian tax administration](https://finanzamt.hessen.de/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-b-in-hessen). Exponent 0.3 in the factor formula [U].

**Lower Saxony: area-location model [P]**
- Niedersächsisches Grundsteuergesetz of 7 July 2021: same area amounts (€0.04 land, €0.50 buildings, residential at 70%), multiplied by a "Lage-Faktor" in which the plot's Bodenrichtwert is compared — "gedämpft" (dampened) — with the municipal average; about 3.6 million properties; "Es gibt nur noch eine neue Hauptfeststellung. Danach werden nur noch Anpassungen vorgenommen"; "keine automatischen Wertsteigerungen durch steigende Preise" — [LStN Niedersachsen](https://lstn.niedersachsen.de/steuer/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-b-grundvermogen-209755.html). The Lower Saxony FG accepted the €0.04/€0.50 figures as within legislative latitude "auch wenn die Zahlen nicht empirisch hergeleitet worden seien" — [FG Niedersachsen press release](https://finanzgericht.niedersachsen.de/aktuelles/presseinformationen/entscheidung-zur-neuen-grundsteuer-in-niedersachsen-251771.html).

### Inferences
- The reform produced three valuation philosophies in one country: (1) market-value-oriented and periodically updated (federal model, BW), (2) value-independent area taxation justified by the benefit principle (Bavaria), and (3) hybrids that use land values only as a relative location index frozen at one date (Hesse, Lower Saxony, Hamburg's two-class location list). This sets up very different futures: models (1) need repeated mass revaluation; models (2)–(3) avoid it but cannot track market change.
- Only Baden-Württemberg's model is close to a classic land value tax (land only, buildings exempt).

### Gaps
- Exact statutory texts for BW (§§ 38, 40 LGrStG), Hamburg and Hesse were not opened (JavaScript-only law portals). Bundestag/Bundesrat vote dates (18 Oct 2019 and 8 Nov 2019, per a Wikipedia lead) and BGBl. citations of the three 2019 laws are unverified [U]. Saxony and Saarland rate values not verified.

## Q9. The 2022–23 declaration campaign: number of properties, filing process, deadlines and compliance

### Takeaway
Every owner of about 36 million units had to file an electronic declaration for the valuation date 1 January 2022. The original deadline (end of October 2022) was extended to 31 January 2023 (Bavaria: end of April 2023); about 90% had filed by the end of May 2023, with hundreds of thousands still missing in mid-2023 and estimates and late-filing surcharges as backstop.

### Cited Findings
- **Scale [P].** Roughly 36 million economic units ("in jedem von rund 36 Mio. Einzelfällen") — [BFH II R 25/24](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/en/entscheidungen/entscheidungen-online/decision-detail/STRE202620008/); about 35 million in 2018 — [BVerfG Rn. 178](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html). Lower Saxony 3.6 million — [LStN](https://lstn.niedersachsen.de/steuer/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-b-grundvermogen-209755.html).
- **Legal duty [P].** Owners file when requested; the request may be made by public announcement by the BMF with the Länder; deadline at least one month; electronic filing by official data record, paper only on hardship application; declarations are tax returns to be signed — [BewG § 228](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__228.html).
- **Channels [P].** ELSTER; plus a federal simplified service "Grundsteuererklärung für Privateigentum" (4 July 2022 – 31 Aug 2023), through which one million declarations were filed — [grundsteuererklaerung-fuer-privateigentum.de](https://www.grundsteuererklaerung-fuer-privateigentum.de/). In Hesse and Lower Saxony owners report only plot area and floor areas; land values are supplied automatically by the cadastre administration — [Hesse](https://finanzamt.hessen.de/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-b-in-hessen); [Lower Saxony](https://lstn.niedersachsen.de/steuer/grundsteuer/grundsteuer-b-grundvermogen-209755.html).
- **Deadlines [P/S].** ELSTER: owners on 1 Jan 2022 "müssen ... bis zum 31. Januar 2023 eine Erklärung abgeben" — [ELSTER](https://www.elster.de/eportal/infoseite/grundsteuerreform) [P]; the duty continues after that date — [federal service](https://www.grundsteuererklaerung-fuer-privateigentum.de/) [P]. Original deadline end of October 2022, extended to end of January 2023, Bavaria to end of April 2023 — [Haufe, 28 Jul 2023 (dpa survey)](https://www.haufe.de/steuern/finanzverwaltung/grundsteuererklaerungen-in-deutschland-noch-nicht-abgegeben_164_601820.html) [S].
- **Compliance [S].** BMF: nearly 90% filed nationwide at the end of May 2023. Still missing (dpa survey, July 2023): NRW about 600,000 (just over 90% received), Bavaria about 500,000, Lower Saxony over 200,000, Thuringia just under 150,000, Berlin about 90,000, Saarland about 64,000, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern just under 25,000; Baden-Württemberg about 86% filed; Hamburg over 100% (duplicates). Non-filers get reminders, then estimates and late-filing surcharges — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/steuern/finanzverwaltung/grundsteuererklaerungen-in-deutschland-noch-nicht-abgegeben_164_601820.html).

### Inferences
- The campaign shifted much of the data-gathering burden of a mass revaluation onto owners; the extensions and the long tail of non-filers show the administrative difficulty the BVerfG anticipated when it allowed a five-year transition.

### Gaps
- An official BMF compliance time series (e.g., share filed at 31 Oct 2022 and 31 Jan 2023) was not accessible (BMF site returned 503; a Handelsblatt report of Dec 2022 was paywalled). The BMF public announcement date and BStBl reference are unverified.

## Q10. Standard land values (Bodenrichtwerte) and expert valuation committees (Gutachterausschüsse): legal basis, history, method, BORIS

### Takeaway
Independent local committees collect every land sale contract, derive zone-average land values for unbuilt land at least every two years, and now also deliver values to tax offices for the land tax. Their figures are the shared valuation backbone of the federal, Baden-Württemberg, Hesse and Lower Saxony models, and the courts have accepted their typical ±30% zone tolerance.

### Cited Findings
- **Committees [P].** "selbständige, unabhängige Gutachterausschüsse" with a chair and honorary experts; members must not administer the local authority's own land; for land values "ist ein Bediensteter der zuständigen Finanzbehörde mit Erfahrung in der steuerlichen Bewertung von Grundstücken als Gutachter hinzuzuziehen" — [BauGB § 192](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbaug/__192.html). They keep the purchase-price collection, derive land values and other market data (e.g., capitalisation rates) and give non-binding valuations on request — [§ 193](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbaug/__193.html).
- **Data source [P].** Every contract to transfer land for consideration (and ground-lease grants, expropriations, forced-sale awards) must be sent in copy by the notary/authority to the committee — [BauGB § 195](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbaug/__195.html).
- **Definition and frequency [P].** Bodenrichtwerte are area-wide "durchschnittliche Lagewerte für den Boden", determined from the purchase-price collection; in built-up areas as if the land were unbuilt; zones of broadly similar use; "jeweils zu Beginn jedes zweiten Kalenderjahres" unless more often; for tax valuation also at each main assessment date per tax-administration specifications; published and transmitted electronically to tax offices; anyone may obtain them — [BauGB § 196](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbaug/__196.html); duty to supply values at the valuation date also in [BewG § 247(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bewg/__247.html).
- **Method [P].** One value per zone, for a fictitious unbuilt reference plot, per m², no ranges allowed; derived mainly by the comparison method, with prices from comparable areas or earlier years or deductive methods where sales are scarce; zones drawn so that location-related differences "grundsätzlich nicht mehr als 30 Prozent betragen" — [ImmoWertV § 13](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/immowertv_2022/__13.html), [§ 14](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/immowertv_2022/__14.html), [§ 15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/immowertv_2022/__15.html).
- **Market transparency role [P].** Committees provide general transparency through land values and market reports and case-specific transparency through valuations; upper committees produce supra-regional analyses — [AK OGA (working group of upper valuation committees)](https://redaktion-akoga.niedersachsen.de/gutachterausschuesse/aufgaben/aufgaben-der-gutachterausschuesse-72297.html).
- **BORIS [P].** BORIS-D is "ein Gemeinschaftsprojekt mehrerer Bundesländer" to provide committees' land values uniformly online, alongside the official Länder portals; operated by IT.NRW — [BORIS-D](https://www.bodenrichtwerte-boris.de/boris-d/).
- **Courts on land values in tax [P/S].** BFH: committees have "eine vorgreifliche Kompetenz"; courts may use land values "grundsätzlich ohne weitere Sachaufklärung"; deviations within a zone up to 30% are constitutionally tolerable, given that individual valuations themselves scatter by about 20% — [BFH II R 25/24](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/en/entscheidungen/entscheidungen-online/decision-detail/STRE202620008/) [P]. FG Baden-Württemberg: committees have an assessment latitude; review only for factual or methodological errors — [PwC](https://blogs.pwc.de/de/steuern-und-recht/article/243959/grundsteuer-b-nach-dem-landesgrundsteuergesetz-baden-wuerttemberg-ist-verfassungsmaessig/) [S]. Lower Saxony FG: land values "hätten sich bereits in der Vergangenheit bei der Grundbesitzbewertung für steuerliche Zwecke bewährt" — [FG Niedersachsen](https://finanzgericht.niedersachsen.de/aktuelles/presseinformationen/entscheidung-zur-neuen-grundsteuer-in-niedersachsen-251771.html) [P].

### Inferences
- Germany's land tax reform rests on an existing, sales-based valuation infrastructure built for planning and market transparency rather than taxation. Its two-year update cycle means the federal and BW models could in principle track land markets closely; the area-location models deliberately use the same data only once.

### Gaps
- Origin in the Bundesbaugesetz of 23 June 1960 (BBauG §§ 136 ff.) is background knowledge [U]; the 1960 text was not opened. The national VBORIS standard and its launch (Sept 2006, per a Wikipedia lead) [U]. The number of committees nationwide was not found.

## Q11. 2025–2026 outcomes: revenue neutrality, multipliers, burden shifts, and court challenges

### Takeaway
Nationally the reform was revenue-neutral in 2025 (€16.1 bn, unchanged), but totals moved by state (Bavaria +10%, NRW −4%) and within municipalities burdens shifted between owners. Every model challenged so far has been upheld by the finance courts and the BFH (federal model November 2025; Baden-Württemberg April 2026); four constitutional complaints were pending at the Federal Constitutional Court as of autumn 2026, and the BFH was due to hear Hesse and Hamburg on 11 November 2026, after the cut-off of these notes.

### Cited Findings
**Revenue and multipliers [P]**
- 2025: Grundsteuer A €0.3 bn (−19.6%), B €15.8 bn (+0.7%), C €5.8 m; total "unverändert bei 16,1 Milliarden Euro" — "Einnahmen aus der Grundsteuer 2025 aufkommensneutral" — [Destatis press release 369, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/10/PD26_369_712.html). Total German tax revenue 2025: €989.8 bn — [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Staat/Steuern/Steuereinnahmen/_inhalt.html); the land tax was therefore about 1.6% of all taxes (computed).
- 2024: B €15.6 bn (+3.8%); weighted average B multiplier 506% (+13 points) — [Destatis press release 309, 21 Aug 2025](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/08/PD25_309_713.html). From 2025 "ist ... keine bundeseinheitliche Berechnung von durchschnittlichen Hebesätzen für die Grundsteuer B und C ... mehr möglich" because models differ — [Destatis 369](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/10/PD26_369_712.html).

The open question for the BVerfG is whether typified values for current dates are acceptable where frozen 1964 values were not.

### Gaps
- Status of the BVerfG complaints after August 2026 and of the 11 Nov 2026 BFH hearings could not be confirmed (the hearings are after the cut-off date). No national data on how many municipalities used split multipliers, or on distribution of bill changes by property type, were found.

## Q12. Austria: the land tax on unit values fixed in 1973 and the debate over updating them, to 2026

### Takeaway
Austria still levies its municipal land tax on unit values whose main assessment date for non-farm property is 1 January 1973; farmland and forests were reassessed as of 2023. As of the Finance Ministry's January 2026 update, nothing had changed for urban property; I could not confirm any 2025–2026 reform decision.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis and mechanics [P].** Grundsteuergesetz 1955 (BGBl. 149/1955): municipal tax on domestic real estate, Grundsteuer A (agriculture/forestry) and B (other property); base amount set by the tax office from the unit value with staggered per-mille rates (§§ 18–19 GrStG); municipalities apply a uniform multiplier of up to 500% under the fiscal-equalisation law; paid quarterly above €75; can be passed to tenants as operating costs; statutory lien — [BMF Austria, Grundsteuer](https://www.bmf.gv.at/themen/steuern/immobilien-grundstuecke/grundbesitzabgaben-einheitsbewertung/grundsteuer.html) (last updated 1 Jan 2026).
- **Rates example [P].** For a €20,000 unit value: single-family house 0.5‰ on the first €3,650, 1.0‰ on the next €7,300, 2.0‰ on the rest (base amount €27.22); rental/mixed property 1.0‰, 1.5‰, 2.0‰ (€34.52); business/vacant 1.0‰ then 2.0‰ (€36.35); farms 1.6‰ then 2.0‰ (€38.54) — [BMF Austria, Grundsteuermessbetrag](https://www.bmf.gv.at/themen/steuern/immobilien-grundstuecke/grundbesitzabgaben-einheitsbewertung/grundsteuermessbetrag.html).
- **Valuation dates [P].** Bewertungsgesetz 1955 (BGBl. 148/1955); main assessment dates: "1. Jänner 1973 - Grundvermögen und dazugehörige Betriebsgrundstücke"; "1. Jänner 2023 - land- und forstwirtschaftliches Vermögen" (§ 20d BewG). Later changes (rezoning to building land, extensions, new owners) are handled by Fortschreibung/Nachfeststellung using the value relationships of the main assessment date, e.g., 1 Jan 1973 — [BMF Austria, Einheitsbewertung](https://www.bmf.gv.at/themen/steuern/immobilien-grundstuecke/grundbesitzabgaben-einheitsbewertung/einheitsbewertung.html) (last updated 1 Jan 2026).
- **Debate [U].** Wikipedia (lead only) says the unit value "für die meisten Grundstücke seit 1973 nicht valorisiert wurde" and lies far below market value, and that reform of the land tax has been raised repeatedly in wealth-tax debates since 2008/09 — [Wikipedia: Grundsteuer (Österreich)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundsteuer_(%C3%96sterreich)).

### Inferences
- Austria shows the German unit-value problem without a court-forced ending for the land tax itself: a 1973 base persists for urban land while agricultural values were refreshed (2014 and 2023 cycles), which is the reverse of what one might expect if updating followed market change.

## Plain-language glossary (for newcomers)

- **Grundsteuer** — the annual municipal tax on land and buildings. **A** = farms and forests; **B** = other property; **C** = optional higher rate on vacant building-ready plots (1961–62, and from 2025).
- **Three-step calculation** — the tax office sets a value, multiplies it by a small statutory rate (**Steuermesszahl**, in per mille) to get a **Messbetrag** (base amount), and the municipality multiplies that by its **Hebesatz** (multiplier, in %, e.g., 500%). The municipality controls the final bill through the Hebesatz.
- **Reinertrag (net yield)** — the estimated normal annual income from land after costs; Prussia's 1861 tax used cadastral net-yield estimates rather than prices.
- **Einheitswert (unit value)** — a single tax value per property set at a "main assessment" (Hauptfeststellung) date and meant to approximate market value at that date; used 1935–2024 in Germany and still used in Austria.
- **Hauptfeststellung / Fortschreibung / Nachfeststellung** — general revaluation of all property / update of one property after a change / first valuation of a new unit. Updates use the old valuation date's price levels, so values never catch up unless a new general revaluation is made.
- **Gemeiner Wert / Verkehrswert** — market value: the price obtainable in ordinary dealings.
- **Bodenrichtwert (standard land value)** — average price per m² of unbuilt land in a zone, derived by local valuation committees from all recorded sales, at least every two years; zones are drawn so that values within them differ by no more than about 30%.
- **Gutachterausschuss** — independent local committee of valuation experts (with a tax official involved for land values) that keeps the database of all land sales.
- **Ertragswertverfahren / Sachwertverfahren** — income method (value from capitalised rent plus land) / cost method (land value plus depreciated building cost).
- **Mietniveaustufe** — a municipality's rent-level class, used to adjust statutory average rents in the federal model.
- **Äquivalenzzahl / Äquivalenzprinzip** — in area models, a fixed euro amount per m² of land or floor area; the "benefit principle" that the tax pays for local public services that property owners can use.
- **Lagefaktor** — a location multiplier (Hesse, Lower Saxony) comparing a plot's standard land value with the municipal average.
- **Aufkommensneutralität** — the political promise that total land-tax revenue would not rise because of the reform; municipalities published "revenue-neutral" Hebesätze; individual bills could still rise or fall.
- **Wertzuwachssteuer (increment value tax)** — a tax on the rise in land value, usually charged at sale on the difference between purchase and sale price.
- **Hauszinssteuer** — Weimar-era tax on pre-1918 buildings to claw back owners' gains from the inflationary wiping out of mortgage debt.
- **Aussetzung der Vollziehung (AdV)** — court-ordered suspension of enforcement of a tax assessment while it is under challenge.

## Scenes and anecdotes (each sourced)

1. **The lost draft found 300 metres away.** Schrameier's handwritten summer-1898 draft of the Tsingtau land and tax ordinance, with marginal notes by the governor, a judge and a navy accountant, was discovered in 1991 in the archive of Qingdao's municipal building office, "nur 300 m von seinem Ursprungsort entfernt" (his office in the old yamen) — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
2. **"The most hated man in the colony."** A 1902 reader's letter to the *Deutsch-Asiatische Warte* reported that for a year (1898) Schrameier "der bestgehaßte Mann in der Kolonie war", because German merchants disliked the high land-value tax and unfamiliar increment tax — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
3. **From 5 pfennigs to 1.74 marks.** The colonial government reported in March 1900 that it had bought land in 1898 at an average of 0.05 Mark/m² and sold plots from October 1898 to spring 1900 at an average of 1.744 Mark/m² — [Matzat 1998](https://tsingtau.org/wp-content/uploads/neues_zu_schrameier_tsingtau_org.pdf).
4. **A modern heating system as "luxury".** Because valuation tables still reflected 1 January 1964, the 2018 judgment notes that a thermostat-controlled hot-water heating system fired by oil or gas counted as "sehr gute Ausstattung" — [BVerfG, Rn. 125](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2018/04/ls20180410_1bvl001114.html).
5. **Buying out a tax with ten years' payments.** In 1942 owners of old buildings were told to pay ten times the annual Hauszinssteuer to be rid of it, as if it had run until 1956; the Reich expected almost 8 billion RM for war finance — Fabricius (1942) in [Bundestag WD 4-3000-133/20](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/817898/af9a120db39ad0dfc3274152b3d0f2e9/WD-4-133-20-pdf-data.pdf).
6. **€59 a year.** In upholding the federal model, the BFH calculated that even a 50% error in the statutory rent would change the Cologne plaintiffs' annual bill by about €59 at a 475% multiplier — [BFH II R 25/24](https://www.bundesfinanzhof.de/en/entscheidungen/entscheidungen-online/decision-detail/STRE202620008/).
7. **An appraisal that came too late.** A Karlsruhe owner's expert valued her plot at €361,000, 36% below the flat €565,000, but submitted it only at the revision stage, where the BFH could not consider new facts — [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsprechung/bfh-iir26-24-grundsteuer-baden-wuerttemberg-bodenwert-grundstueck-wert-2026-05-20).
8. **A golf course as test case.** The Hessian FG's first serious doubts about the area-factor model came from a leased golf course outside a built-up area — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/de/de/themen/2026/07/fg-hessen-grstg-adv.html).
9. **26,000 addresses change "quality".** On 3 November 2026 Hamburg's Senate reclassified about 13,000 properties from good to normal residential location and 12,000 the other way (plus ~6,000 first classifications), each move changing the residential rate by a quarter — [Hamburg Finanzbehörde](https://www.hamburg.de/politik-und-verwaltung/behoerden/finanzbehoerde/aktuelles/grundsteuer-bleibt-insgesamt-aufkommensneutral-1234162).
10. **A Prussian reformer's death in Canton.** Schrameier, adviser to Sun Yat-sen, was killed in a traffic accident in Canton in 1926; Sun Fo erected a monument to him in the municipal cemetery — [Silagi 1984](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3486727).
