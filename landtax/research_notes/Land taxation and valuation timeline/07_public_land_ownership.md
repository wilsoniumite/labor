# Public land ownership and leasing as land-value collection: Hong Kong, Singapore, mainland China, Canberra (ACT), Amsterdam — origins to 4 November 2026

Compiled 4 November 2026. Scope: how governments that own the land collect its value through land sales, lease premiums, lease-modification charges, annual ground rents and land conveyance fees rather than (or as well as) a land tax; how that value is assessed, by whom and how often; revenue; and what happened when valuations aged, were frozen, challenged or abandoned.

**Confidence labels used throughout**
- **[P]** confirmed in a primary source I opened in this session (statute or ordinance text, official gazette, parliamentary report or reply, government press release or budget speech, official statistics, court record).
- **[S]** secondary source I opened (scholarly work, professional or law-firm note, reputable journalism, or a news report reproducing official statistics).
- **[X]** seen only in a search-engine extract, or only in a lead-only source (advocacy group, community history site, blog); page not opened or not accessible. Treat as unverified.
- **[U]** unresolved: no source found in this session.

**Two framing corrections the report writer should know about**
1. Singapore: the Differential Premium did not *replace* the Development Charge. They ran in parallel (DC on planning permission, collected by URA; DP on lifting title restrictions on state leases, collected by SLA) and were both replaced, together with the Temporary Development Levy, by the Land Betterment Charge on 1 August 2022 [P] ([MinLaw 2021](https://mlaw.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-speeches/2021-05-10-second-reading-speech-by-2m-edwin-tong-on-land-betterment-charge-bill); [SLA](https://www.sla.gov.sg/properties/land-betterment-charge/)).
2. Hong Kong: "standard rates" for premium assessment did not start in 2023–2024. A pilot began in 2021 (old industrial buildings), was extended to New Development Areas in February 2022, and was regularised and widened in December 2023 (effective 1 April 2024) [P] ([info.gov.hk 10 Apr 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202504/10/P2025041000564p.htm); [DEVB LCQ22, 9 Nov 2022](https://devb.gov.hk/en/sdev/press/index_id_11352.html); [news.gov.hk 28 Dec 2023](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2023/12/20231228/20231228_172528_949.html)).

## 2. Hong Kong

### Takeaway
Hong Kong collects land value mainly through one-off **premiums**: on new land grants (sold by auction or tender) and on **lease modifications** and land exchanges, where the charge is the difference in land value before and after the change. It does not do this through an annual land tax. An annual **government rent** at 3% of rateable value was written into the 1984 Joint Declaration and Article 121 of the Basic Law. It applies only to leases granted or extended since 27 May 1985. Rates (5% of rateable value, made progressive for the most valuable homes from early 2025) and government rent share one rental valuation, which the Rating and Valuation Department redoes every year. Land premium has swung from about a fifth of government revenue in the late 2010s (search extracts of LegCo data) to HK$13.5 billion in 2024-25 and HK$17.5 billion in 2025-26 (about 2.5% of revenue) [P]. Since 2021 the government has offered "standard rates" that applicants can choose instead of negotiating a premium case by case.

#### Tenure origins, 1841–1997
- First land auction 14 June 1841 under Captain Charles Elliot, before British sovereignty was formalised; 34 lots; about £3,238 a year in rents; buyers had to spend $1,000 on building within six months — [X] [Gwulo](https://gwulo.com/media/27278) (community site; lead only). One scholarly table of contents (JSTOR, book not identified in this session) calls the 1841 auction "probably ultra vires" — [X] [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt24hkwh).
- 1843: government says it will grant only 75-year leases; strong protests; Parliament considers the matter in 1847 — [X] search extract of digitised texts on hkintexts.histsyn.com (page not opened; 403 on a related page). The later grant of 999-year leases for many early Victoria lots is **[U]**, not verified this session.
- New Territories leases granted before the Joint Declaration expired on **27 June 1997** and were extended by s. 6 of the New Territories Leases (Extension) Ordinance (Cap. 150) — [P] [Audit Commission Report No. 53, ch. 2, 2009](https://www.aud.gov.hk/pdf_e/e53ch02.pdf).

#### The 1984–85 settlement and the Basic Law: government rent at 3% of rateable value
- Joint Declaration Annex III [P] ([CMAB text](https://www.cmab.gov.hk/en/issues/jd5.htm)):
  - para. 2: non-renewable leases expiring before 30 June 1997 could be extended to no later than 30 June 2047 **without additional premium**, at an annual rent of **3% of rateable value**, "adjusted in step" with later changes in rateable value; certain rural holdings kept their old rent.
  - para. 3: new leases granted from entry into force to 30 June 1997 at a premium and nominal rent to 1997, then 3% of rateable value.
  - para. 4: new land grants capped at **50 hectares a year** to 30 June 1997, excluding Housing Authority public rental housing land.
  - para. 5: lease modifications at a premium "equal to the difference" in land value between old and new conditions. This is the legal statement of the "before-and-after" principle used for modification premiums.
  - para. 6: premium income, after deducting the average cost of land production, shared equally between the British Hong Kong government and the future SAR government. The British side's share went to the Capital Works Reserve Fund; the SAR's share was held in Hong Kong banks for land development and public works.
  - para. 7: a Sino-British Land Commission monitors these rules. A Foreign Office briefing noted the Commission agreed "each year so far" to exceed the 50-hectare limit — [X] search extract of digitised FCO file on [hkintexts](https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/page/1340521).
- Basic Law Art. 121 [P] ([Basic Law ch. 5](https://www.basiclaw.gov.hk/en/basiclaw/chapter5.html)): for leases granted or renewed 27 May 1985–30 June 1997 (no right of renewal) extending beyond 1997 and expiring by 30 June 2047, "the lessee is not required to pay an additional premium as from 1 July 1997, but an annual rent equivalent to 3 per cent of the rateable value of the property at that date, adjusted in step with any changes in the rateable value thereafter, shall be charged." Art. 122 freezes the old rent for rural lots held by male-line descendants of 1898 village residents. Art. 123 leaves leases expiring after 1997 to SAR law and policy.
- Government Rent (Assessment and Collection) Ordinance (Cap. 515): rent payable on (a) the extended pre-1985 New Territories leases, (b) extended non-renewable Hong Kong Island/Kowloon leases, and (c) **all leases granted since 27 May 1985**. "Government rent is charged at 3% of the rateable value of the land, which is the aggregate of the rateable values of the properties situated on the land." The basis of rateable value is the same as for rates. Rent for renewable leases is collected by the Director of Lands under the Government Leases Ordinance (Cap. 40) — [P] [Audit 2009](https://www.aud.gov.hk/pdf_e/e53ch02.pdf).
- Scale: at 1 April 2009 the Government Rent Roll held 1.63 million assessments with total rateable value of HK$199 billion — [P] [Audit 2009](https://www.aud.gov.hk/pdf_e/e53ch02.pdf). In 2014-15 the RVD collected **HK$22.3 billion in rates and HK$9.3 billion in government rent** — [P] [Audit Commission Report No. 66, ch. 1, 2016](https://www.aud.gov.hk/pdf_e/e66ch01.pdf).

#### Rates and the annual revaluation
- Rates are a property tax under the Rating Ordinance (Cap. 116), charged at a prescribed percentage of rateable value. "The rateable value of a property is the estimated annual open market rental value at a designated valuation reference date …, assuming that the property was then vacant and was to let." The prescribed percentage has been **5% since 1999-2000**. The valuation reference date has been **1 October of the preceding year since 1999**. The RVD conducts **General Revaluations annually** to prepare a new Valuation List and Government Rent Roll — [P] [Audit 2009](https://www.aud.gov.hk/pdf_e/e53ch02.pdf). Whether annual revaluation itself began in 1999 is [U].
- Progressive rates for domestic properties: 5% on the first HK$550,000 of rateable value, 8% on the next HK$250,000, 12% above. Proposed in the 2024-25 Budget; applies from the fourth quarter of 2024-25 (January–March 2025); affects about 1.9% of private domestic tenements — [X] search extracts of [info.gov.hk 28 Feb 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202402/28/P2024022800519.htm) and [info.gov.hk 23 Oct 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202410/23/P2024102300237.htm). Sources disagree on start date (1 January vs 1 April 2025); the quarterly-billing account favours January 2025.
- Rates concessions continue: 2026-27 concession of up to HK$500 per property for the first two quarters (about 3.15 million domestic properties, revenue forgone ~HK$3.1 billion; about 440,000 non-domestic properties, ~HK$400 million) — [P] [Budget speech 25 Feb 2026, para. 279](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202602/25/P2026022500188.htm).

#### Land premiums: sales and lease modifications

### Inferences
- Hong Kong has two value bases. One is a **market-rent base** (rateable value), revalued every year for rates and the 3% government rent, so it does not age. The other is a **capital land-value base** for premiums, fixed at the moment of grant or modification. Annual collection therefore tracks rents closely, while most land-value collection is lumpy and tied to the property cycle. That fits the fall in premium revenue from about a fifth of revenue to about 2.5% in 2025-26.
- The 3% government rent is a political compromise from 1984–85. It let leases run past 1997 without new premiums, but it is not a full annual land-rent system: leases granted before 27 May 1985 that remain unexpired and renewable under Cap. 40 are handled separately.
- Standard rates trade valuation accuracy for speed and certainty: a zone-and-use table set in advance and reviewed annually replaces site-specific valuation. This mirrors Singapore's table-of-rates method (below). Whether the public gives up revenue as a result is not addressed in the sources found [U].

- Official confirmation of progressive-rates dates and HK$ per square foot standard rates (Practice Notes not opened).
- Government rent revenue after 2014-15.

## 3. Singapore

### Takeaway
Singapore's state owns about 90% of land (44% in 1960) [S]. The state collects land value by **selling leases** (typically 99-year) through the Government Land Sales programme (since 1967) and by **betterment charges** on planning permission and on lifting title restrictions. These were the Development Charge (from the 1964 Planning Act amendment), the Differential Premium and the Temporary Development Levy. Since **1 August 2022** they have been one Land Betterment Charge at 70% of the increase in land value, or 100% in some covenant cases, priced from a **half-yearly table of rates** or, by election, a valuation [P]. Under the Constitution, land-sale proceeds are **past reserves**: they cannot be spent in the budget, and only up to 50% of investment returns on reserves can be [P]. The annual property tax is levied on **annual value** (estimated market rent), which IRAS reviews every year [X].

### Cited Findings

#### State ownership and acquisition
- State share of total land: 1960 44%; 1965 49%; 1970 57%; 1975 66%; 1980 70%; 1985 76%; by 2005 90%. Reclamation has added over 25% to land area since independence — [S] [Phang (SMU), presentation to Hawaii Community Development Authority, Sept 2024](https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/hcda/files/2024/08/GEN-09-04-24_ITEM-2_Professor-Sock-Yong-Phang-Presentation.pdf).
- Land Acquisition Act 1966: broad acquisition powers including "for any residential, commercial or industrial purposes"; "Landowners cannot object to decisions"; compensation appeals go to an Appeals Board, not the courts — [S] [Phang 2024](https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/hcda/files/2024/08/GEN-09-04-24_ITEM-2_Professor-Sock-Yong-Phang-Presentation.pdf). Act in force June 1967 — [X] (Wikipedia extract; lead only). Compensation at **fair market value since 2007** — [P] [MOF, 7 Nov 2022](https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/impact-from-sale-of-state-land-on-reserves-and-accounting-treatment-of-cost-of-state-land-for-public-housing/).

#### Land sales (Government Land Sales, GLS)
- "Since its establishment in 1967, the Sale of Sites programme" (later GLS) has reshaped the city centre — [P] [Centre for Liveable Cities](https://knowledgehub.clc.gov.sg/publications-library/the-government-land-sales-programme-turning-plans-into-reality). URA is the main land-sales agent; programmes are announced every six months — [X] [URA Land Sales](https://www.ura.gov.sg/Corporate/Land-Sales) (extract).
- How GLS reserve prices are set (Chief Valuer) and annual GLS proceeds: **[U]**, not found this session.

#### Budget treatment: proceeds go to reserves
- "Under the Constitution, State land and revenues from land sales form part of Singapore's Past Reserves, which are invested." "Land sales revenue is excluded as it is not available for budgetary spending by the Government." "Up to 50% of net investment returns on our Reserves can be included in the Budget for spending" (the Net Investment Returns Contribution, NIRC) — [P] [gov.sg explainer, 19 Apr 2016](https://www.gov.sg/explainers/why-are-some-revenues-not-included-in-the-budget-statement/).
- Second Minister Indranee Rajah, 7 Nov 2022: "There is no net increase in the reserves when State Land is first sold" (a physical asset is converted into a financial one). "All State Land forms part of our reserves". Land reverting at lease expiry does not add to reserves, because the reversion was already counted. HDB buys land from the government at fair market value ("If fair market value is not paid … the past reserves would be depleted"). HDB's Homeownership Programme deficit was S$3.85 billion in FY2021/22 — [P] [MOF](https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/impact-from-sale-of-state-land-on-reserves-and-accounting-treatment-of-cost-of-state-land-for-public-housing/).
- NIRC estimated at S$19.6 billion for FY2021, about 20% of the Budget — [X] [MOF newsroom extract](https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/published-government-cash-surplus-numbers-and-impact-on-government-s-fiscal-space-and-decision-on-gst-rate-increase/).
- Disputed: opposition leader Pritam Singh proposed letting the budget use some land-sale proceeds (e.g., the lower of 20% of 20-year average land sales or 20% of that year's) — [X] [Leader of the Opposition site](https://leaderoftheopposition.sg/2024/02/07/parliament-motion-on-reserves) (advocacy; lead only). The government's position is the reserves framework above [P].

#### Betterment charges: DC, DP, TDL → Land Betterment Charge (LBC)
- Development Charge introduced by a **1964 amendment** to the Planning Act, "currently 70% of increment in land value" — [S] [Phang 2024](https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/hcda/files/2024/08/GEN-09-04-24_ITEM-2_Professor-Sock-Yong-Phang-Presentation.pdf).
- Rate history: 70% originally; cut to 50% (reportedly 1985, during a recession); raised back to 70% in 2007. MND said rates "will be revised from the current 50% of the appreciation in land value to 70%" to "reinstate the DC to its original rate at 70 per cent" — [X] MND press release extract ([sla.gov.sg, now 404](https://sla.gov.sg/articles/press-releases/2007/revision-of-development-charge-dc-press-release-by-mnd)). The 1985 date is [X], source unidentified.
- Pre-2022 mechanics: URA collected DC and TDL where planning permission "results in an enhancement in land value", using a "DC Table of Rates in the Planning (Development Charges) Rules" by **geographic sector and land-use group**. SLA collected Differential Premium on waivers or variations of restrictive covenants in state titles. "The current practice is to collect DP at 100% of the enhancement of land value" for material change of use on land sold without tender. DC had no adjustment for remaining lease — [P] [MinLaw second reading speech, 10 May 2021](https://mlaw.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-speeches/2021-05-10-second-reading-speech-by-2m-edwin-tong-on-land-betterment-charge-bill).
- Why merge: DC, TDL and DP "are similar in nature, as they all cream-off in different ways the increase in value of land". LBC is "not a new charge"; principles and rates "will remain largely unchanged"; a single agency (SLA) collects — [P] [MinLaw 2021](https://mlaw.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-speeches/2021-05-10-second-reading-speech-by-2m-edwin-tong-on-land-betterment-charge-bill).
- LBC design: "the LBC rate will be set at 70% of the increase in land value" for development proposals; 100% for lifting land-use covenants and certain other cases. A **leasehold factor** applies to state leases with 99 years or less remaining. Where the taxpayer elects valuation (irrevocable) or no table rate fits, the **Chief Valuer (IRAS) or a private valuer appointed by SLA** values the enhancement. Penalty tax is 30% in some breach cases — [P] [MinLaw 2021](https://mlaw.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-speeches/2021-05-10-second-reading-speech-by-2m-edwin-tong-on-land-betterment-charge-bill); [P] [SLA](https://www.sla.gov.sg/properties/land-betterment-charge/).
- Commencement **1 August 2022**: "With effect from 1 August 2022, a new Land Betterment Charge (LBC) has replaced Differential Premium" (with DC and TDL). "The principles for computing LBC follow the previous DP and DC systems, known as the Table of Rates method." Revised tables (with sector maps A and B and a use-groups table) are published each **March and September**; editions listed from March 2022 to **September 2026** — [P] [SLA](https://www.sla.gov.sg/properties/land-betterment-charge/); [S] [Allen & Gledhill, 28 Jul 2022](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/22143/new-charge-under-land-betterment-charge-act-2021-replaces-development-charge-temporary-development-levy-and-differential-premium).
- Who decides the half-yearly rates and on what evidence (Chief Valuer advice; number of sectors and use groups): **[U]** in sources opened.

#### Property tax on annual value
- Annual value (AV) is "determined based on estimated market rentals of similar or comparable properties and not on the actual rental income received". "The AVs of all properties are subject to annual reviews by IRAS". AVs can go up or down; HDB flat AVs were revised upward 4–6% (2022) — [X] extracts of [IRAS AV page](https://iras.gov.sg/taxes/property-tax/property-owners/annual-value) and [IRAS 2022 HDB AV release](https://www.iras.gov.sg/news-events/newsroom/revision-in-annual-values-for-hdb-flats-in-2022). The page body could not be read in this session.
- Property-tax rate schedule (progressive owner-occupier and non-owner-occupier rates, 2023–2024 increases) and revenue: **[U]**.

### Inferences
- Singapore values twice. A **capital value** is captured once, through competitive tender of leases, plus a 70–100% charge on any later uplift from planning or title changes, priced from a centrally set table revised every six months. An **annual market rent** is taxed through the property tax. The half-yearly table means betterment values are refreshed far more often than in Canberra or old Amsterdam, at the cost of zone-average rather than site-specific accuracy.
- Because sale proceeds are constitutionally ring-fenced, land value reaches current spending only indirectly, through the NIRC. This separates fiscal policy from land-market swings, unlike Hong Kong and mainland China. That is the government's rationale [P]; opposition parties dispute the trade-off [X].

- Original statutory DC rate and the 1985 cut (primary source); start date of Differential Premium.
- IRAS property-tax rate tables and revenue; the history of annual value (Property Tax Act 1960).

## 4. Mainland China

### Takeaway
Urban land is state-owned and rural land collectively owned. Since the 1987 Shenzhen auction and the **12 April 1988** constitutional amendment, the state has sold time-limited **land-use rights** (土地使用权, *tǔdì shǐyòngquán*) for an up-front conveyance fee (土地出让金, *tǔdì chūràngjīn*). The **19 May 1990** State Council regulations set maximum terms of 70/50/40 years and allowed agreement, tender or auction [P]. Local governments' reliance on these fees is called "land finance" (土地财政, *tǔdì cáizhèng*). Revenue peaked around 8.4–8.7 trillion yuan in 2020–2021 and fell to **4.15 trillion yuan in 2025** (−14.7%, the fourth straight double-digit fall). It fell a further **28.6% in January–August 2026** [S]. A recurrent property tax has been piloted only in Shanghai and Chongqing (since 28 January 2011). The **23 October 2021** NPC Standing Committee authorisation for wider pilots runs for five years *from the date the State Council issues pilot measures*. I found no evidence such measures were ever issued [U].

### Cited Findings

#### Legal foundation

Lot **H409-4**, 8,588 m² of residential land next to Shenzhen Reservoir, **50-year** right; starting price **2 million yuan** in steps of 50,000; sold for **5.25 million yuan** to 深圳经济特区房地产公司 (Shenzhen SEZ Real Estate Company). 44 firms registered (9 foreign-invested); a 21-person Hong Kong "viewing group" attended — [P] [Shenzhen Municipal Government](https://www.sz.gov.cn/szstoryjp/202301/content/post_10372285.html).

- **19 May 1990**, State Council Order No. 55, 《城镇国有土地使用权出让和转让暂行条例》 (last revised 29 Nov 2020) [P] ([State Taxation Administration database](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100010/c5192953/content.html)):

#### Property-tax pilots (2011) and the 2021 authorisation
- From **28 January 2011**, Shanghai and Chongqing taxed some private housing — [S] [KPMG China Alert, Feb 2011](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2011/02/china-alert-1102-06-c.pdf).
  - **Shanghai** (whole municipality): newly bought homes (local families' additional homes; non-residents' purchases). Base = purchase price per m² (or an assessed taxable price) × **70%** × rate.   - **Chongqing** (nine main urban districts): existing and new detached houses, newly bought high-end homes, and second-plus ordinary homes bought by people without local hukou, business or job.   - Note: the national 房产税 revenue line above (5,212 亿元 in 2025) is the general building tax, mostly on non-residential property. It is not a measure of the pilots (my inference; no source separates the pilot yield) [U].

Taxable objects: residential and non-residential real estate. "土地使用权人、房屋所有权人为房地产税的纳税人" (land-use right holders and building owners are taxpayers). Legally held rural homesteads (农村宅基地) excluded. Term **five years "自国务院试点办法印发之日起算"** (from the date the State Council issues pilot measures). Report due six months before expiry — [S] [China Daily (Chinese)](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202110/23/WS6173e3e7a3107be4979f44ff.html). ### Inferences
- China's valuation point is the **sale**: an auction or tender price fixes a capitalised 40–70-year value up front, and there is no periodic revaluation of urban land for annual charges. ## 5. Canberra (Australian Capital Territory)

### Takeaway
The federal capital was built on Commonwealth land leased, never sold freehold, so that the public would capture the value created by building the city. Leases (99 years) began in 1924 with rent at 5% of the unimproved value, re-appraised at long intervals (lengthened to every 20 years by 1935) [P]. Because revaluations were so far apart, rents "lagged well behind 5 per cent of the current market value". Auctions shifted step by step to up-front premiums (1935, 1962). From **1 January 1971** the Commonwealth stopped charging land rent and **abolished periodic re-appraisals**. It recovered costs through reserve prices, raised municipal rates, and from January 1971 charged a **50% betterment** fee on changes of lease purpose [P]. Neutze called the 50% "essentially an arbitrary fraction", and the 1988 parliamentary inquiry recommended charging the full premium value instead [P]. The ACT later reintroduced optional land rent (2008) for affordability [P]; the current Lease Variation Charge and rates rules were not verified this session [U].

### Cited Findings

#### Founding intent
- Barton, Maitland, January 1901: "So far as the law of the land allows land within the federal area will not be sold. Its ownership will be retained in the Commonwealth. The land will be let for considerable terms but with periodical re-appraisement so that the revenues thus obtained will assist the cost of creating the Commonwealth Capital." (OCR corrected: "iaw" → "law", "wilt" → "will") — [P] Neutze's paper in [1988 House report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf), quoting Brennan, *Canberra in Crisis* (1971), p. 19.
- Constitution Convention and early parliamentary debates show "prevention of speculation was a principal objective" when it was decided that land should be "leased rather than sold 'for an estate of freehold'" — [P] [1988 House report, Neutze appendix](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf). The statutory source (Seat of Government (Administration) Act 1910 s 9) was not opened [U].

#### Rent, valuation and re-appraisal, 1924–1970
- "In 1924 the first Canberra leases were auctioned with bidders nominating a capital price … The successful bidder was required to pay the first year's land rent, 5 per cent of the sum bid, to obtain the lease. From 1935, however, rents were fixed at 5 per cent of the assessed value and any amount bid in excess of that value had to be paid as a cash premium. This was the first step in conversion from a rental to a premium leasehold system. From 1962 bidding was solely for the premium" — [P] [1988 House report, para. 2.16, quoting Neutze evidence](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
- Leases of 99 years; rent 5% of assessed unimproved capital value "for the initial twenty years" — [X] ACT Archives extract. Which ordinance governed the first auction is disputed: the City Area Leases Ordinance 1924 (later cited as "1924–1929") vs a 1921 ordinance — [X]; the 1935 Ordinance confirms a "City Area Leases Ordinance 1924–1929" existed [P] ([1935 Ordinance](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/ord/1935-1/19350104-47760/PDF/1935-1.PDF)).
- 1935 amendment of s 14 ("Re-appraisement of value of land"): "omitting the word 'tenth' and inserting … 'twentieth'" and "omitting … 'ten' and inserting … 'twenty'". This lengthened the re-appraisal cycle — [P] [City Area Leases Ordinance 1935](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/ord/1935-1/19350104-47760/PDF/1935-1.PDF).
- **Depression revision of values**: rents "reduced and … deemed at all times to have been reduced by Twenty per centum" from 1 July 1931. The Minister could reduce "the unimproved capital value of any parcel" for up to five years from 1 January 1933 — [P] [1935 Ordinance, s 11](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/ord/1935-1/19350104-47760/PDF/1935-1.PDF).
- 1961: rent based on unimproved value "is subject to re-appraisal during the twentieth year of the term of the lease and during each twentieth year thereafter". The UV definition was aligned with the Rates Ordinance 1926-1950 so rating and rent values would be consistent. Bidding switched from naming a "reserve value" (UV) to bidding a premium for a lease at a stated rent; restricted auctions (e.g., bona fide home builders) were introduced (Minister Gordon Freeth) — [P] [Explanatory Memorandum No. 12 of 1961](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_42717/19610713-49290/PDF/db_42717.PDF).
- Valuation ageing (Neutze, 1988): "long before 1970, most rents had lagged well behind 5 per cent of the current market value because of the long (20 year) interval between revaluations". As rising inflation pushed market yields above 5%, more equity passed to lessees. Through the 1960s the gap between 5% and debenture yields (2–3 points) "provided the benchmark for premiums expected at auctions". Rates were kept low to attract settlers and reluctant transferees — [P] [1988 House report, Neutze appendix](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).

#### Abolition of land rent (decided December 1970, effective 1 January 1971)
- "The Government has decided that as from 1 January 1971 land rent will no longer be charged in the Australian Capital Territory. Instead, persons purchasing leases will be required to pay reserve prices for all blocks offered, which will be calculated to return to the Commonwealth the cost of acquisition and development of the land offered for sale. At the same time, the level of general rates and municipal charges will be increased to levels comparable with the other Australian capitals." The aim: "removing the obligation to pay land rent, abolishing periodic re-appraisements and implementing instead the new reserve price scheme." Special-purpose leases (non-residential, non-business) granted after 1 January 1971 also free of rent. Rates Ordinance 1970: "The new unimproved value of parcels of land will be determined on 1 January 1971 based on the values existing at 1 January 1970". Rating year moved to the financial year — [P] [Explanatory Memorandum No. 45 of 1970 (City Area Leases, Leases (Special Purposes) and Rates Ordinances 1970)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_40743/19701217-46859/PDF/db_40743.PDF).
- Effect: "In 1970 land rent was effectively abolished when it was reduced to five cents payable on demand and a premium became the only payment: the conversion was complete" — [P] [1988 House report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf). (The 1970-vs-1971 discrepancy across sources is decision year vs effective date.)
- Minister Ralph Hunt's March 1971 press statement: "the system of land tenure was in no way affected by the new system of land charges…". Neutze: "a view which may have been legally correct but was economically nonsensical" — [P] [1988 House report, Neutze appendix](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
- Why: the official memorandum gives the policy (cost-recovery reserve prices plus higher rates) but no political reason [P]. An ACT Assembly debate (1991) ties it to "the Commonwealth Government's municipal budget initiatives for the ACT which saw the virtual abolition of land rent in favour of the introduction of higher municipal rates" — [X] [ACT Hansard, 17 Dec 1991, p. 6365](https://hansard.act.gov.au/hansard/1st-assembly/1991/HTML/week19/6365.htm). A claim that the Gorton government promised abolition ahead of a Canberra by-election — [X] [progress.org](https://www.progress.org/wiki/canberra-leasehold-system/) (advocacy; lead only). Brennan, *Canberra in Crisis* (1971), the standard history, was not accessed [U].

#### Betterment after 1971 and the 1988 inquiry
- From January 1971 a lessee granted a change of lease purpose (s 11A) paid **50% of the increase in site value less $1,500**. The 1970 memorandum specified "50% of the difference between the rating value for the old and new lease less $1,500, with a provision for appeal against the assessed value" — [P] [1970 EM](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_40743/19701217-46859/PDF/db_40743.PDF); [1988 House report para. 2.21](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
- Neutze: "The objective was to recover something for the public purse following abolition of land rents but to leave an incentive for lessees to undertake desirable redevelopment. The 50 per cent was essentially an arbitrary fraction, though with land rents at 5 per cent of capital value the Commonwealth's equity interest was probably less than 50 per cent prior to 1971." — [P] [1988 House report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
- 1988 committee (Joint Sub-Committee on the Canberra Leasehold System, chair J.V. Langmore) recorded the argument that the 50% levy "is quite arbitrary with no empirical or administrative basis". It worried about "the amount of revenue which is foregone" and stated that "A 50 per cent betterment amounts to a subsidy". **Recommendation 10**: replace the 50% levy "by compensation to the lessee for the value of the lease that is surrendered, including improvements, and a charge of the full premium value for the grant of a new lease together with the cost of any necessary off-site services". Case evidence includes a reduced betterment charge of $2.25 million agreed for one development — [P] [1988 House report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
- Later Lease Variation Charge (LVC): remission of up to 75% for high-environmental-performance adaptive re-use (2014 instrument). In 2020 a recovery package cut the LVC by 50% of the amount payable up to $250,000 — [X] extracts of [ACT legislation register (2014 ES)](https://legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_49430/20140410-57636/PDF/db_49430.PDF) and [2020 instrument](https://legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/di/2020-224/20200625-74701/PDF/2020-224.PDF). Canberra Times headline: Treasurer Andrew Barr "has given Canberra's property developers $25 million worth of tax breaks since 2014" — [X] [Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/6027225/andrew-barr-has-given-canberras-property-developers-25-million-worth-of-tax-breaks-since-2014/). LVC codification date, current rates and revenue: **[U]** (ACT Revenue Office page blocked).

#### Land rent revived as an option (2008)
- Land Rent Act 2008: from the Affordable Housing Action Plan announced 12 April 2007, a land rent scheme for owner-occupiers to reduce up-front costs. Initially limited to low-to-moderate income households at a discount rate. "To protect households from large rental increases due to increases in unimproved land values rent increases will be capped". Lessees may later buy the land by varying the lease to a nominal rent — [P] [Land Rent Bill 2008 Explanatory Statement (Treasurer Jon Stanhope)](https://legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_32377/20080508-36556/PDF/db_32377.PDF); [Land Rent Act 2008 (A2008-16)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2008-16/20080701-37066/PDF/2008-16.PDF).
- Rates: standard 4% and discount 2% of unimproved value (2016–2017 determinations); worked example UV $200,000 → $4,000 a year; different rules for leases under contracts from 1 October 2013 — [X] extracts of [2016 determination ES](https://legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_54248/20160627-63931/PDF/db_54248.PDF). Determination DI2013-179 under the Taxation Administration Act commenced 1 July 2013; the Land Rent Amendment Act 2013 (A2013-38) exists — [P] [DI2013-179 ES](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/es/db_48116/20130628-55755/PDF/db_48116.PDF); [A2013-38](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2013-38/20131001-56278/PDF/2013-38.PDF).

### Inferences
- Canberra is a clear case of **valuation ageing ending a ground-rent system**. A rent pegged to unimproved value but revalued only every 20 years fell behind market value. Lessees capitalised the gap into auction premiums. By 1970 the government replaced the rent with up-front prices and a higher, more frequently revalued rate. The Rates Ordinance 1970 set a fresh UV at 1 January 1971 based on 1 January 1970 values.
- After 1971 the public's share of later value increases depended on the betterment fraction (50%, later remissions) and on rates. The 1988 inquiry's push for full capture, and later remission controversies, are disputes about that fraction rather than about valuation frequency.

### Gaps
- Seat of Government (Administration) Act 1910 s 9 text; City Area Leases Ordinance 1924 original s 14 (the exact original re-appraisal schedule); date of the first auction from a primary source.
- Land-rent revenue before 1971; numbers of lessees affected; whether existing leases were converted or only new grants (the "five cents" passage suggests existing rents were also reduced, but this is not explicit) [U].
- Current LVC (codified vs valued charges, rates, revenue); ACT rates (average unimproved value) and the 2012 tax reform replacing stamp duty with rates; current Land Rent percentages and take-up.
- Bourassa, Neutze & Strong (1997), *Journal of Property Research* 14(1): 49–68, on betterment under Canberra's premium leasehold — only the abstract listing seen [X] ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:jpropr:v:14:y:1997:i:1:p:49-68)).

## 6. Amsterdam (erfpacht)

### Takeaway
About 80% of land in Amsterdam is municipally owned [P]. Since **1896** the city has issued it in ground lease (*erfpacht*) so that "de waardevermeerdering van de grond valt toe aan de gemeenschap" (the increase in land value accrues to the community) [P]. Under **continuous** lease (*voortdurende erfpacht*, AB 1915–2000) the annual ground rent (*canon*) was indexed (since 1966) but reset to current land value only at the end of each long period (*tijdvak*, 50–75 years). This produced large jumps and many court cases. The council adopted **perpetual** lease (*eeuwigdurende erfpacht*, AB 2016) on **22 June 2016**: no more end-of-period revisions, an annually indexed rent or a one-off buy-out (*afkoop*). A transition scheme (2017–2020) used **frozen 2014/2015 WOZ values and a 35% discount** [P]. Disputes have centred on the land-valuation method (the residual method's "leverage"), the canon percentage, opaque rules and deadlines. A council inquiry (2021), a consumer-authority probe, a second-chance scheme (2022–2023) and an independent advisory committee (2025) followed. The system continues in 2026 with a 2.39% canon rate [P].

### Cited Findings

#### Origins and the continuous system (1896–2016)
- 1896: council decides municipal land will as a rule be issued in erfpacht, not ownership. Stated goals: the community benefits from land-value increases caused by public investment, and the city gains influence over use and maintenance — [P] [Amsterdam council inquiry, 1 Jun 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf). Introduced under alderman Willem Treub — [X] [KNB article extract](https://www.knb.nl/stream/nieuw-erfpachtstelsel-amsterdam-revolutionaire-systeemwijziging) (now 404).
- AB 1915: from temporary to continuous erfpacht. Under continuous lease, after 50 or 75 years a new period starts and a new canon is set. AB 1937: canon can be prepaid for a period. AB 1966: canon "periodiek aan de inflatie aangepast" (periodically indexed to inflation), and the city first makes an offer for the new canon "voordat deskundigen kunnen worden ingeschakeld" (before experts can be called in). "Sinds de AB 1966 is er sprake van tijdvakken van 50 jaar"; older ABs have 75 years only in the first period — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
- At the end of a period (CHET, *canonherziening einde tijdvak*) the canon is revised on current land value, by independent experts if the parties disagree. The Amsterdam district court (5 June 2013, ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2013:CA2167) held revision on current land value permissible. The Hoge Raad (29 April 2016, ECLI:NL:HR:2016:765 and :769) held that the city may unilaterally amend the General Conditions 75 years after issue and that doing so is "niet onredelijk bezwarend" (not unreasonably onerous). In the long-running Honnebier case, the appeal court refused to hold the lessee to a defective expert report and appointed new experts — [P] as reported in [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
- 1996 centenary symposium "Een eeuw erfpacht in Amsterdam" was critical. The college's *Eindrapportage 100 jaar erfpacht; operatie Groot Onderhoud* (4 Jan 2000) reaffirmed that "De waardevermeerdering van de grond valt toe aan de gemeenschap". AB 2000 in force 1 January 2001 — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).

#### The 2014–2017 reform to perpetual erfpacht
- 2011–2013: growing criticism. 2014–2018 coalition agreement (the inquiry names VVD and D66; SP's participation is from a search extract only [X]): no more CHET; perpetual erfpacht for all individual lessees; no price adjustment for volume changes on private owner-occupied homes. VVD had long wanted lessees able to buy freehold; D66 (largest party in 2014) wanted the system changed. A 2013 option to adjust the canon only on sale was dropped — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
- Grondwaardecommissie (land-value commission), June 2015: recommended the residual method and a discount for switching, because past rent-setting had applied many discounts (25–40% depreciation) and "een aanbod zonder korting nagenoeg geen kans van slagen zal hebben" (an offer without a discount would have almost no chance) — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
- **22 June 2016**: council decision adopting AB 2016 (Gemeenteblad 2016, Afd. 3A, nr. 187/664 of 24 June 2016) — [P] [Gemeenteblad 2025, nr. 500150](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2025-500150.pdf). Perpetual erfpacht for new issues from mid-2016; transitions for existing lessees thereafter — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf). Interest-group anger at the plans and a referendum threat (2016) — [X] [AT5 headline](https://www.at5.nl/artikelen/168232/college-past-erfpachtplan-aan-belangenclub-is-woedend-en-dreigt-met-referendum).

#### How land value and the canon are calculated
- Land value for erfpacht (*erfpachtgrondwaarde*) — [P] [council inquiry 2021, ch. 3](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf):
  - **residual**: (WOZ value − rebuilding cost) − depreciation, or in practice **WOZ value × buurtstraatquote (BSQ) − depreciation**.
  - BSQ = average of at least five individual land ratios (*grondquotes* = land value ÷ WOZ value) in one neighbourhood street.
  - depreciation factor 10%, for restrictions and the existing contract.
  - minimum land price 5% of WOZ value; land value for the canon never more than half the market value.
  - WOZ is the municipal property valuation under the Dutch Valuation of Immovable Property Act (Wet WOZ).
- Canon = land value × canon percentage. AB 2016 percentage set yearly by a formula linked to 10-year government bond yields, "momenteel 2,39%". AB 2000 percentages set quarterly (bond yield plus a surcharge depending on the fixed-rent period). Perpetual canon indexed yearly — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf). Bond-yield series from Money View Research; council decision of 1 Oct 2014 on the canon percentage — [P] [Gemeenteblad 2025, nr. 500150](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2025-500150.pdf).
- 2026 values: AB 2016 canon percentage **2.39%** (decision 13 Nov 2025). Indexation coefficients for 2026: **1.021 (AB 2000), 1.031 (AB 2016)**; reduced coefficient **1.188** for AB 1966/1994 rights in "Groot Onderhoud"; 25% surcharge for excluding indexation — [P] [Gemeenteblad 2025, nr. 500150](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2025-500150.pdf); [Gemeenteblad 2025, nr. 364645](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2025-364645.pdf).

#### The transition scheme (*Overstapregeling*) and its frozen valuation date
- Favourable terms applied **1 October 2017 – 8 January 2020**: "De laagste WOZ-waarde uit 2015 (peildatum 1-1-2014) of 2016 (peildatum 1-1-2015)" (the lower of the 2015 and 2016 WOZ values) and "Een overstappremie van 35%" — [P] [Spijtoptantenregeling, Gemeenteblad 2022, nr. 525691](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2022-525691.pdf).
- The discount was 25% in the scheme. The college decided on 18 December 2018 to add 10 points (35%), retroactively. After 8 January 2020: 25% and the prior year's WOZ value. Energy-efficient homes get €20/m² off land value per 0.1 EPC point below 0.4. The deadline was moved from 1 to 8 January 2020 because the online portal threw errors and slowed — [P] [council inquiry 2021, p. 33 and fn. 81](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf). After 8 January 2020 "werd de veel hogere WOZ-waarde uit 2018 de grondslag" (the much higher 2018 WOZ value became the basis) — [S] [Binnenlands Bestuur, 10 Nov 2021](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/ruimte-en-milieu/cultuur-en-onderwijs/amsterdamse-erfpachters-kunnen-toch-gunstig-afkopen).
- Uptake: in March 2019 the lessees' foundation SEBA (Stichting Erfpachters Belang Amsterdam) said only **2,027 households** were switching (under 1%), called it "een groot fiasco" and "Het verwerkingssysteem functioneert niet", and went to court — [S] [AT5, 7 Mar 2019](https://www.at5.nl/artikelen/192181/erfpachters-stappen-naar-de-rechter-uit-onvrede-over-overstapregeling). By 21 September 2020 there were about **167,500 applications**, about 166,000 of them for the pre-8 January 2020 window. By December 2020 offers had been sent for about **48,500** rights. The erfpacht department had 150 staff for 150,000 lessees — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).

#### Criticism, oversight and second chances
- Council inquiry (1 June 2021), "Erfpacht in Amsterdam: een Gordiaanse knoop" (a Gordian knot) — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf):
  - The 2.39% canon rate is "aan de hoge kant" (on the high side) given low interest rates; the 1.5% risk premium is high compared with banks.
  - "De residuele methode leidt tot een hefboomwerking": a rise or fall in house value produces an amplified rise or fall in land value. This is acknowledged by the city but not clearly communicated.
  - Keeping the municipal working instruction secret "leidt tot wantrouwen onder erfpachters" (breeds distrust).
  - Time-limited terms made citizens feel forced to choose under uncertainty.
  - The original 1896 objectives had not been updated.
  - Total revenue from perpetual erfpacht likely below that of the old system.
- Revenue: realised erfpacht income **€282.6 million** and costs €45 million in **2019** — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
- The ACM (Authority for Consumers and Markets) examined the city's practice and found information on the financial advantages of switching possibly not clear enough for a group. On 10 November 2021 alderman Marieke van Doorninck announced a one-off second chance for the roughly 15% (about **30,000**) of lessees who had not applied — [S] [Binnenlands Bestuur, 10 Nov 2021](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/ruimte-en-milieu/cultuur-en-onderwijs/amsterdamse-erfpachters-kunnen-toch-gunstig-afkopen); [S] [AT5, 10 Nov 2021](https://www.at5.nl/artikelen/211891/laatste-kans-voor-erfpachters-om-over-te-stappen-naar-nieuwe-regeling). Formal ACM enforcement requests exist — [X] [ACM document](https://www.acm.nl/system/files/documents/handhavingsverzoek-consument-inzake-erfpachtbeleid-gemeente-amsterdam.pdf).
- **Spijtoptantenregeling** (regret-optants scheme), adopted by the college: one more application on pre-2020 financial terms (lowest 2014/2015-reference WOZ; 35% premium), deadline "uiterlijk 1 augustus 2023" — [P] [Gemeenteblad 2022, nr. 525691](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2022-525691.pdf). Consultation 24 June–19 September 2022; in force 24 November 2022; open from 28 November 2022 — [S] [court summary 2026](https://omgevingsweb.nl/jurisprudentie/eclinlrbams20268590-rechtbank-amsterdam-27-08-2026-c-13-790977/).

#### Evidence on effects (disputed/limited)
- Francke & van der Schans (ESB, 2 Oct 2019): in 72,581 Amsterdam sales (2010–2018), matched canon-paying vs bought-out homes (1,270 sales) show buyers price in on average about **30%** of the present value of future erfpacht payments: 14% in 2010–2013, **40%** in 2014–2018. "Amsterdamse kopers houden slechts rekening met veertig procent van de contante waarde van de erfpachtbetalingen." They recommend a 40% correction in WOZ valuations, recalculated yearly — [S] [ESB](https://esb.nu/kopers-in-amsterdam-houden-beperkt-rekening-met-erfpachtbetalingen/).
- Municipal view: many who did not switch "kiezen daar bewust voor" (chose that deliberately), per alderman Van Doorninck — [S] [Binnenlands Bestuur 2021](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/ruimte-en-milieu/cultuur-en-onderwijs/amsterdamse-erfpachters-kunnen-toch-gunstig-afkopen).

### Inferences
- Amsterdam shows two valuation problems in sequence. First, **long revision intervals**: a 50–75-year tijdvak with a CHET reset produced shock increases, much like Canberra's 20-year gaps. Second, a **frozen valuation date** in the transition: offers based on 2014/2015 WOZ values became steadily more valuable as prices rose, which produced the deadline rush (≈166,000 applications) and later fairness claims.
- Moving to perpetual, annually indexed canons (or buy-outs) means future land-value growth above inflation is largely no longer captured from switched lessees. The 2021 inquiry judged total revenue likely to fall. This is the reverse of the 1896 goal, though the city still sets value at the point of switching or buy-out [P inquiry; my inference on the mechanism].

Cross-cutting patterns (my synthesis from the findings above):
- **Annual market-rent bases are kept fresh; capital land-value bases age.** Hong Kong and Singapore revalue rental values every year for rates and property tax. Every system that tied an annual land charge to a capital land value revalued only rarely: Canberra every 20 years, Amsterdam every 50–75 years. Both saw rents fall behind and then politically difficult jumps or abolition.
- **The shift from rent to premium is common.** Canberra (1935–1971) moved from annual rent to up-front premium. Amsterdam (2016–) moved from periodically reset rent to indexed rent or buy-out. Hong Kong has always relied mainly on premiums, and China always on up-front fees. Up-front collection fixes the public's share at the moment of sale. Later value increases are captured only through betterment or modification charges (Hong Kong, Singapore, Canberra) or recurrent property taxes.
- **Tables replace site valuations to cut disputes.** Examples are Singapore's sector/use-group table (half-yearly), Hong Kong's standard rates (annual, by region or zone and use) and Amsterdam's street land ratio (BSQ). Each trades site accuracy for speed and predictability.

## 8. Plain-language explanations for newcomers

- **Leasehold vs freehold**: under freehold you own land indefinitely. Under public leasehold the government stays owner and grants a right to use the land for a term (e.g., 50, 70, 99 years) or perpetually, on conditions.
- **Premium**: a lump sum paid up front for a lease. It capitalises (adds up in today's money) the value of the land over the lease term, less any annual rent.
- **Ground rent / land rent / canon**: an annual payment to the public landowner. In Amsterdam it is *canon*: land value × a canon percentage, indexed for inflation.
- **Lease modification (Hong Kong) / change of lease purpose (Canberra) / lifting a restrictive covenant (Singapore)**: permission to use land more intensively or differently than the lease allowed. The government charges for the increase in land value.
- **Betterment / value capture**: charging landowners for land-value increases caused by public decisions or investment, such as rezoning or new infrastructure. Singapore's LBC (70%/100% of uplift) and Canberra's 50% charge (1971) are examples.
- **Table of rates / standard rates**: a published schedule of land values per unit of floor area by zone and use. The charge is calculated from it instead of valuing each site.
- **Rateable value (Hong Kong) / annual value (Singapore)**: the estimated yearly rent a property would fetch on the open market (Hong Kong assumes it is vacant and to let). This is the base for rates or property tax, and in Hong Kong also for government rent.
- **Unimproved value (Canberra)**: what the land alone would be worth without buildings. It is the base for Canberra's land rent and rates.
- **WOZ value (Netherlands)**: the official annual municipal market valuation of a property (land plus buildings), used for local taxes. Amsterdam derives land value from it using street-level land ratios.
- **Residual land value method**: land value = property value − cost of rebuilding the structure (− adjustments). Because the building cost is roughly fixed, a change in property value produces a proportionally larger change in land value. This is the "leverage" (*hefboomwerking*) criticised in Amsterdam.
- **Tijdvak / CHET (Amsterdam)**: the 50- or 75-year period of a continuous lease. At its end (*canonherziening einde tijdvak*, CHET) the canon is reset to current land value.
- **Buy-out (afkoop)**: paying the present value of future ground rent in one sum.
- **Land-use right (土地使用权) and conveyance fee (土地出让金, China)**: the state keeps ownership but sells time-limited use rights. The fee is paid mostly up front. "Land finance" (土地财政) means local governments' dependence on these fees.
- **Government-fund budget (政府性基金预算, China)**: a budget separate from the general public budget, for earmarked revenues. Land conveyance revenue is the bulk of local government-fund revenue.
- **Past reserves / NIRC (Singapore)**: savings built up by earlier governments, protected by the Constitution. Land and land-sale proceeds count as past reserves. Only up to 50% of investment returns (the Net Investment Returns Contribution) may be spent.
- **Reversion**: the landowner's right to get the land back when a lease ends.

## 9. Scenes and anecdotes (sourced)

1. **Shenzhen, 1 December 1987, 4:30 p.m.** Forty-four firms, nine of them foreign-invested, registered to bid for a 50-year right to 8,588 m² beside Shenzhen Reservoir. Officials and mayors from 17 cities watched, with a 21-person Hong Kong "viewing group". Bidding opened at 2 million yuan, rising in 50,000-yuan steps, until the auctioneer called "成交!" (Sold!) at 5.25 million yuan. Four months later, on 12 April 1988, the Constitution was amended to make such transfers lawful — [P] [Shenzhen Municipal Government](https://www.sz.gov.cn/szstoryjp/202301/content/post_10372285.html).
2. **Barton's promise and its undoing.** In 1901 Edmund Barton promised that Canberra's land would "be let for considerable terms but with periodical re-appraisement". By the 1960s, 20-year re-appraisals had left rents "well behind" market values. In 1970 the rent was "reduced to five cents payable on demand". The minister insisted in March 1971 that "the system of land tenure was in no way affected"; economist Max Neutze called that "economically nonsensical" — [P] [1988 House report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/reports/1988/1988_pp409.pdf).
3. **Canberra in the Depression.** A 1935 ordinance cut every lessee's rent by "Twenty per centum", backdated to 1 July 1931. It let the Minister write down the "unimproved capital value" of leased blocks for up to five years from 1933, a rare case of a government revising land valuations downward by decree — [P] [City Area Leases Ordinance 1935](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/ord/1935-1/19350104-47760/PDF/1935-1.PDF).
4. **Amsterdam, New Year 2020.** With favourable transition terms (2014/2015 WOZ values, 35% discount) due to expire on 1 January 2020, the online "Overstapportaal" threw errors and slowed. The city extended the deadline by a week to 8 January. About 166,000 applications arrived for the cheap window. Eleven months later offers had gone out for only about 48,500 rights, from a department of 150 officials serving about 150,000 lessees — [P] [council inquiry 2021](https://www.gemeente.nu/content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Raadsonderzoek-erfpachtbeleid-Amsterdam.pdf).
5. **A sale hanging on a valuation rule (Amsterdam, 2026).** A couple sold their flat on 22 May 2026 on condition that the city confirm by 14 August that they qualified for 2020-era "regret-optant" terms. The city did not confirm. In a kort geding (summary proceedings) on 27 August 2026 the judge ruled they should have "aan de bel … trekken" (raised the alarm) years earlier, and rejected the claim — [S] [court summary](https://omgevingsweb.nl/jurisprudentie/eclinlrbams20268590-rechtbank-amsterdam-27-08-2026-c-13-790977/).
6. **Hong Kong, 1841 (unverified).** Captain Elliot auctioned 34 lots before the colony legally existed. Buyers had to build $1,000 worth within six months and hoped, in vain, for perpetual leases — [X] [Gwulo](https://gwulo.com/media/27278); lead only.
