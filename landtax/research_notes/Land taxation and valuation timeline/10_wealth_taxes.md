# Net wealth taxes and related inheritance and property taxes: valuation, rise and retreat (to 4 November 2026)

Scope note for the report writer. These notes cover annual net wealth taxes, plus inheritance and property taxes where they share the valuation story. Coverage runs to 4 November 2026. The through-line is valuation: how assets (above all land and buildings) were valued, by whom, how often, and what happened when values aged or were challenged.

Confidence labels used throughout:
- **[P]** = confirmed in a primary source (statute or official gazette, court judgment or court press release, tax authority, parliament or government document, official audit body, or an intergovernmental or official analytical report such as the OECD or California's Legislative Analyst).
- **[S-sch]** = secondary, scholarly (peer-reviewed article, academic working paper, or expert commission).
- **[S]** = secondary only (journalism, professional or law-firm commentary).
- **[U]** = unresolved (sources conflict, or the claim comes only from a lead such as Wikipedia or a search-engine summary and could not be checked).

## 1. Dated timeline (for merging into the global chronology)

### Takeaway
Annual net wealth taxes spread across Europe from the 1840s to the 1970s. They reached 12 OECD countries in 1990, fell to 4 in 2017, and in 2026 survive as general taxes only in Norway, Spain and the Swiss cantons. France keeps a real-estate-only version (IFI), and Colombia and Argentina tax wealth outside the OECD core. The German collapse (1995–97) and the later German inheritance-tax and property-tax rulings all turned on stale real-estate values. That makes valuation the clearest bridge to the land-tax story.

- The OECD's 12-to-4 count can be rebuilt from its own repeal dates. The 1990 group was Austria, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Iceland, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland: the 4 survivors plus the 8 named repealers. From the stated dates the count runs roughly: 12 (1990) → 11 (after Austria, 1994) → 9 (1997) → 8 (2001) → 5 (2006) → 4 (2007) → 3 in practice (2008–09, Spain zeroed) → 4 (Iceland, 2010) → 5 (Spain, 2011) → 4 (2015–17). After 2018 there are 3 general taxes (Norway, Spain, Switzerland) plus France's real-estate-only IFI. This is my own count from OECD-stated dates. OECD Figure 1.1 may use slightly different counting conventions.
- German valuation decisions recur about every decade (1995, 2006, 2014, 2018), always on the same theme: assessed values that drifted from market values. This makes Germany the main case linking wealth taxes to land valuation.

### Gaps
- Exact dates of Prussian and Reich-era German wealth taxes before 1952, and of the original 1910/1911 Swedish ordinance, were not checked in primary sources.
- The date of the G20 Ministerial Declaration on International Tax Cooperation (July 2024) is given only by French Wikipedia (26 July 2024) [U].

## 2. Overall arc: introductions, the OECD count, survivors in 2026

### Takeaway
Wealth taxes were old (Swiss cantons from 1840, Norway 1892, Denmark 1903, Sweden 1911) and widespread in Europe by 1990. Most were repealed between 1994 and 2007, with governments citing efficiency, capital flight, administrative cost relative to small revenue, and narrow, avoidable bases. Valuation problems drove several of these: hard-to-value assets were exempted, and real estate was valued below market. In November 2026, general annual net wealth taxes survive in Norway, Spain and the Swiss cantons. France taxes only real-estate wealth (IFI), and Colombia and Argentina keep wealth taxes outside the OECD core.

### Cited Findings
- "While 12 countries had net wealth taxes in 1990, there were only four OECD countries that still levied recurrent taxes on individuals' net wealth in 2017" [P] — [OECD 2018, Executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/ctp/tax-policy/role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-OECD-summary.pdf)
- Repeals: "Austria (in 1994), Denmark (in 1997), Germany (in 1997), the Netherlands (in 2001), Finland, Iceland, Luxembourg (all three in 2006) and Sweden (in 2007)". In 2008 Spain zeroed liabilities with a 100% credit. Iceland (2010–2014) and Spain (2011) reinstated wealth taxes "as temporary fiscal consolidation measures" [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 16–18](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Reasons given for repeal: "efficiency costs and the risks of capital flight"; that wealth taxes "often failed to meet their redistributive goals as a result of their narrow tax bases as well as tax avoidance and evasion"; and "high administrative and compliance costs, in particular compared to their limited revenues (i.e. high cost-yield ratio)" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 17](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Valuation as a driver of erosion: "Partly as a consequence of valuation issues, many of these hard-to-value assets have been exempted from wealth taxes, eroding the tax base, distorting the choice of savings vehicles and creating opportunities for tax avoidance" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 69](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Revenue was low: in 2016, from 0.2% of GDP (Spain) to 1.0% (Switzerland), and from 0.5% (France) to 3.7% (Switzerland) of total tax revenue. Norway's low yield despite high rates "may in part be because of the very favourable valuation rules that apply to primary residences" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 18](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- A later scholarly survey: "About a dozen OECD countries had wealth taxes in the recent past, but only three still have them". Behavioural responses depend heavily on how broad the base is and how well it is enforced [S-sch] — [Scheuer & Slemrod 2021, JEP 35(1): 207–30](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.35.1.207)
- The Wealth Tax Commission cites Perret (2020) as finding "a mix of tax design and political reasons that led to the abandonment [of] wealth taxes in nine of the twelve OECD countries which had them in 1990. Chief among the design concerns was equality of treatment across asset classes" [S-sch] — [Advani, Chamberlain & Summers 2020, p. 90](https://www.wealthandpolicy.com/wp/WealthTaxFinalReport.pdf)
- Survivors in 2026, checked in 2026 primary sources:
  - Norway: 1.0% above NOK 1.9m and 1.1% above NOK 21.5m in 2026 [P] — [Skatteetaten 2026](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesskatt/?year=2026)
  - Spain: Ley 19/1991 in force, plus the ITSGF extended indefinitely [P] — [BOE Ley 19/1991](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392); [BOE Ley 38/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22684)
  - France: the IFI applies to real-estate wealth above €1.3m on 1 January 2026 [P] — [service-public.gouv.fr, verified 6 Mar 2026](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F563)
  - Colombia: permanent tax since Ley 2277/2022 [P] — [DIAN](https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/ley_2277_2022.htm)
  - Argentina: Bienes Personales with legislated rates for 2025–2027 [P] — [InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/365/texact.htm)
- Netherlands: since 2001 a presumptive tax on capital income (Box 3) "functions in practice like a net wealth tax" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 76](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Policy advice that bears on land taxation: the OECD recommends "Aligning the tax base with asset market values" and suggests the base "could be limited to a fixed percentage of that market value (e.g. 80-85%) to prevent valuation disputes". It also suggests that "valuations used for the residential property tax or the inheritance tax can be used for net wealth tax purposes" [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 69, 86–87](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- In this account, wealth taxes retreated because three features combined: real estate kept at below-market or stale values, exemptions for business and unlisted assets, and small revenues. Each case below shows a different mix.
- Switzerland is the exception the literature points to: it has low thresholds, a broad base, and housing valued below market. It continues to raise about 1% of GDP (2016).

### Gaps
- No 2026 primary source was checked confirming that all 26 Swiss cantons still levy wealth tax. The most recent confirmations here are OECD (2018), the Wealth Tax Commission (2020) and FT/Swissinfo (2022). No abolition was found.
- Italy (taxes on foreign real estate and financial assets), Belgium (securities-account tax) and Uruguay are not covered (search budget).

## 3. Germany: the 1995 rulings, the end of the wealth tax, and later valuation rulings

### Takeaway
Germany's wealth tax ended because of land valuation. Real estate was taxed at "unit values" (Einheitswerte) frozen at 1 January 1964 value relationships (plus a flat 40% uplift), while securities and cash were taxed at current values. In 1995 the Federal Constitutional Court held that one tax rate could not be applied to both. Parliament never passed a new law, so the tax has not been levied since 1 January 1997. The same frozen-value problem later sank the inheritance-tax valuation rules (1995, 2006) and the land tax (2018).

### Cited Findings
**Tax profile (Vermögensteuer, to 1996)**
- Period: 1952–1997 per the OECD [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.1](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). Threshold in the last year: €61,355 per single taxpayer (converted at the 2002 euro rate) [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.2](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- How real estate was valued, and by whom: the tax offices fixed unit values for each economic unit. "stützen sich sämtliche für Grundvermögen festgestellten Einheitswerte auf die Wertverhältnisse des Jahres 1964" (all unit values fixed for real property rest on the value relationships of the year 1964). These values were already out of date when first applied in 1974, and were raised by 40% (§ 121a BewG), so the base was 140% of the 1964 value. Before the 1965 reform, 1935 values had been used. The law required a general revaluation every six years, but none happened after 1964 because the law fixing the next date was never enacted. Other assets were taken at current market-related values (Gegenwartswerte) [P] — [BVerfG 2 BvL 37/91, 22 Jun 1995, via DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)
- The size of the gap: the Court reports the Federal Finance Court's finding that unit values were "um ein Mehrfaches niedriger als die gemeinen Werte" (several times lower than the market values) of other assets. The 1995 judgment gives no single percentage [P] — [BVerfG 2 BvL 37/91 via DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)

**1995 wealth-tax ruling (2 BvL 37/91, Second Senate, BVerfGE 93, 121)**
- Holding: § 10 Nr. 1 VStG (the rate provision) was incompatible with Art. 3(1) GG (equality) "jedenfalls seit dem Veranlagungszeitraum 1983 in allen seinen seitherigen Fassungen" (at least since the 1983 assessment period, in every version since), because it applied one rate to unit-valued real estate and to assets at current values [P] — [DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)
- Deadline: "Der Gesetzgeber ist verpflichtet, eine Neuregelung spätestens bis zum 31. Dezember 1996 zu treffen." (The legislature must enact new rules by 31 December 1996 at the latest.) "Längstens bis zu diesem Zeitpunkt ist das bisherige Recht weiterhin anwendbar." (The old law may apply until then at the longest.) [P] — [DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)
- The "half-sharing" limit (Leitsatz 3): the wealth tax may be added to other taxes on yield only if the total burden stays "in der Nähe einer hälftigen Teilung zwischen privater und öffentlicher Hand" (near an even split between private and public hands). The decision text does not use the word "Halbteilungsgrundsatz", which later commentators coined [P] — [DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)

**Aftermath: suspension, not repeal**
- In 1996 a government bill to repeal the Wealth Tax Act (BT-Drs. 13/4839, 11 June 1996) and rival reform bills were tabled, but none found a majority. Kube's view: "§ 10 Nr. 1 VStG ist damit zum 31.12.1996 unwirksam geworden" (became ineffective on 31 December 1996), and with it the whole Act. On the widely held view that follows, the Länder would not be barred from enacting their own wealth taxes, because the Bund never passed a repeal or blocking law [P: written expert statement to the Bundestag Finance Committee, hearing of 19 April 2021] — [Kube 2021, pp. 2–3](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/833422/a776a6209e9c97f66c820a0ae8fad354/04-Kube.pdf)

**1995 inheritance-tax ruling (2 BvR 552/91, BVerfGE 93, 165)**
- Tenor: § 12(1) and (2) ErbStG "ist jedenfalls seit 1987 in allen seinen seitherigen Fassungen ... mit Artikel 3 Absatz 1 des Grundgesetzes unvereinbar" (incompatible with equality since at least 1987, in every version since), because it uses for real property "den seit 1964/74 der Wertentwicklung nicht mehr angepaßten Einheitswert" (unit values not adjusted to value changes since 1964/74) and for other assets "den Gegenwartswert" (current value). New rules due by 31 December 1996 [P] — [BVerfGE 93, 165 via DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093165.html)
- Reasoning: unit-valued property is taxed "in den Vergangenheitswerten von 1964" (at past values of 1964), producing "deutliche Wertverzerrungen und Belastungsungleichheiten" (clear value distortions and unequal burdens) [P] — [DFR](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093165.html)

**2006 inheritance-tax valuation ruling (1 BvL 10/02, First Senate, decided 7 November 2006, published 31 January 2007)**
- § 19(1) ErbStG (uniform rates on values computed in different ways) is unconstitutional. For built-up real estate, a simplified income method with a fixed multiplier of 12.5 produced values averaging about 50% of market value, but ranging "zwischen weniger als 20 % und über 100 % des gemeinen Werts" (from under 20% to over 100% of market value). Undeveloped-land values were frozen at 1 January 1996. Business assets and unlisted shares were taken at tax-balance-sheet values, generally well below market. New law due by 31 December 2008 [P] — [BVerfG press release 11/2007](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2007/bvg07-011.html)

**2014 inheritance-tax business-relief ruling (1 BvL 21/12, 17 December 2014)**
- The exemptions for business assets (§§ 13a, 13b ErbStG) are partly disproportionate: "Die Privilegierung betrieblichen Vermögens ist jedoch unverhältnismäßig" in particular respects. These were relief for large firms without any test of need; exempting firms with up to 20 employees from the payroll condition; and allowing up to 50% "administrative" (non-operating) assets. Relief as such is in principle compatible with equality. "Der Gesetzgeber muss bis 30. Juni 2016 eine Neuregelung treffen" (new law due by 30 June 2016) [P] — [BVerfG press release 116/2014](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2014/bvg14-116.html)

**2018 land-tax ruling (Grundsteuer, 1 BvL 11/14 and others, 10 April 2018): the land-tax echo of 1995**
- "Das Festhalten des Gesetzgebers an dem Hauptfeststellungszeitpunkt von 1964 führt zu gravierenden und umfassenden Ungleichbehandlungen bei der Bewertung von Grundvermögen, für die es keine ausreichende Rechtfertigung gibt." (Clinging to the 1964 valuation date causes serious and widespread unequal treatment in valuing real property, with no sufficient justification.) Administrative simplicity cannot justify it. New law due by 31 December 2019, and old rules usable for five years after that, but no later than 31 December 2024 [P] — [BVerfG press release 21/2018](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html)
- The Court spelled out the logic: a uniform lag would be harmless, but "Es gibt indes keine Anhaltspunkte dafür, dass die ... Wertverzerrungen sich in einer gleichmäßigen Relation zum Verkehrswert bewegten" (there is no indication that the distortions moved in a uniform ratio to market value) [P] — [BVerfG 21/2018](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html)

### Inferences
- The 1995 ruling did not hold that a wealth tax is unconstitutional. It held that taxing stale real-estate values and current financial values at one rate is. Parliament then let the tax lapse rather than revalue all German real estate. The 2018 ruling shows that the underlying land valuation stayed unreformed for another two decades.
- German revaluation failed through a 1970 statutory postponement "until a special law" that never came. This is a concrete example of the general problem that periodic reassessment is easy to defer politically.

### Gaps
- Revenue in the last full year (1996): an FDP parliamentary document reportedly cites "rund 9 Milliarden Mark" (about DM 9bn). That is a party statement, not checked against Destatis or BMF statistics [U].
- Rates in 1995–96 (commonly given as 1% for individuals and 0.6% for corporations) were not checked in a primary source [U].
- The 2009 and 2016 inheritance-tax reform laws were not checked in BGBl.

## 4. Sweden: wealth tax 1911–2007, inheritance and gift tax abolished 2004, state property tax replaced 2008

### Takeaway
Sweden's wealth tax valued real estate at the official tax-assessed value (taxeringsvärde, set at about 75% of market value) and listed shares at 75–80%. Unlisted shares were taxed lightly from the 1970s and exempt from 1991. Rising assessed home values in the late 1990s pulled ordinary homeowners into the tax, and thresholds were raised to compensate. When abolishing it from 2007, the government cited its "non-uniform valuation rules", loopholes, perceived arbitrariness and an estimated SEK 500bn held abroad. Sweden had already abolished inheritance and gift tax (December 2004, under a Social Democratic government), and in 2008 it replaced the state property tax with a capped municipal fee.

### Cited Findings
**Wealth tax: what was taxed, valuation, rates, revenue**
- Valuation: "Real estate was valued at the so-called tax-assessed value (taxeringsvärdet), which was supposed to be roughly 75 percent of the market value." Listed stocks were taken at 80% from 1997 to 2006, 75% from 1978 to 1996 and 100% before 1978. "From 1978 unlisted shares (on the so-called O-list, an informal listing) and other OTC-shares were assessed at only 30 percent of their quoted or book value." Forest accounts were taken at half value [S-sch] — [Du Rietz & Henrekson, IFN WP 1000, p. 3](https://www.ifn.se/media/apndv2ws/wp1000.pdf)
- 2000–2006 rules: a 0% rate below a threshold (SEK 900,000 in 2000) and 1.5% above it. "Real estate, including tenant-owned apartments, was taxed at 75 percent of the market value." Shares on the main A-list were taxed at 80%, O-list shares at 0%, and majority owners holding over 25% were exempt. Pension savings, art and jewellery were exempt [S-sch] — [Seim 2017, AEJ: Economic Policy 9(4): 395–421, pp. 398–400](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf)
- Payers and revenue: in 2000 about 7.7% of the population paid, and the tax raised 1.0% of total tax revenue. In 2006, 3.0% paid and it raised 0.7%. Thresholds in 2006 were SEK 1.5m (singles) and SEK 3.0m (couples) [S-sch] — [Seim 2017, Table 1](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf). Revenue "never exceeded 0.4 percent of GDP in the postwar period and amounted to 0.16 percent of GDP in 2006" [S-sch] — [Du Rietz & Henrekson, abstract](https://www.ifn.se/media/apndv2ws/wp1000.pdf)
- Valuation driving the thresholds: "The large increases in the wealth-tax threshold in the early 2000s are the result of substantial increases in real-estate tax liabilities at the end of the 1990s, following a renewal of the government's procedure for computing the taxable value of housing" [S-sch] — [Seim 2017, p. 400](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf)
- Rates over time: the top rate was cut from 4% to 3% in 1984, to 2.5% in 1991 and to 1.5% in 1992 [S-sch] — [Du Rietz & Henrekson, p. 12](https://www.ifn.se/media/apndv2ws/wp1000.pdf)
- OECD threshold data for Sweden (2007) give €166,214 single and €221,619 couple [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.2](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). The couple figure does not match Seim's SEK 3.0m for 2005–06 [U].

**Abolition (proposition 2007/08:26, "Slopad förmögenhetsskatt m.m.", signed 18 October 2007)**
- "Förmögenhetsskatten slopas från och med beskattningsåret 2007." (The wealth tax is abolished from tax year 2007.) The Act (1997:323) applies for the last time to tax year 2006, assessed in 2007 [P] — [Prop. 2007/08:26](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:26)
- Reasons stated:
  - Assets: "tillgångar motsvarande 500 miljarder kronor kan vara placerade utanför Sveriges gränser" (assets of SEK 500bn may be held outside Sweden; a Skatteverket estimate).
  - Valuation: "Förmögenhetsskattens oenhetliga värderingsregler och många undantag skapar kryphål" (the wealth tax's non-uniform valuation rules and many exceptions create loopholes).
  - Perception: "Förmögenhetsskatten har därför kommit att uppfattas som både godtycklig och orättvis" (it has come to be seen as both arbitrary and unfair).
  - Homeowners: "Samtidigt har många småhusägare kommit att betala förmögenhetsskatt på grund av snabbt stigande taxeringsvärden" (meanwhile many small-house owners have come to pay wealth tax because of rapidly rising tax-assessed values) [P] — [Prop. 2007/08:26](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:26)

**Inheritance and gift tax abolished (decided 16 December 2004)**
- The Riksdag decided on 16 December 2004 (proposition 2004/05:25, committee report 2004/05:SkU18). No tax applies to deaths or gifts after end-2004.
- The recorded vote was 171 for the committee's proposal (S 131, V 26, MP 13, one independent) against 138 for the opposition's Reservation 1 (M 50, FP 41, KD 27, C 20). This was a vote between the committee text and the reservation, not a simple yes/no on abolition.
- The stated rationale on the page: easing generational transfers of family businesses [P] — [Riksdagen, 2004/05:SkU18](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/betankande/slopad-arvsskatt-och-gavoskatt-prop.-20040525_gs01sku18)

**State property tax replaced by a capped municipal fee (2008 budget, prop. 2007/08:1)**
- Stated reason: "Nuvarande fastighetsskatt är orättvis, saknar folkligt stöd och legitimitet" (the current property tax is unfair and lacks popular support and legitimacy) [P] — [Prop. 2007/08:1](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:1)
- New fee: "en kommunal avgift på 6 000 kronor per småhus, dock högst 0,75 procent av taxeringsvärdet" (a municipal fee of SEK 6,000 per house, but at most 0.75% of taxeringsvärde), and SEK 1,200 per apartment in blocks, at most 0.4%. The same proposal raised the capital-gains tax on private homes from 20% to 22% [P] — [Prop. 2007/08:1](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:1)
- Cap over time (houses): SEK 6,000 in 2008, SEK 7,812 in 2018, SEK 10,074 in 2025 and SEK 10,425 in 2026 (cap base SEK 1,390,000 at 0.75%). The fee for apartments in blocks in 2026 is 0.3%, capped at SEK 1,784. Land under a leased-land house paid the state fastighetsskatt at 1% in 2008 [P] — [Skatteverket, "Kommunal fastighetsavgift kalenderåren 2008 och 2018–2026", 11 Nov 2025](https://skatteverket.se/download/18.16f588619a39f2c14aa57/1762862283047/kommunal-fastighetsavgift-kalenderaren-2008-och-2018-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Sweden the property assessment system fed the wealth tax directly. When assessed values were renewed and rose (late 1990s), many more homeowners crossed the threshold. The policy reaction was first higher thresholds and then, in 2007–08, abolition of the wealth tax and capping of the property levy, with "rapidly rising assessed values" given as one reason. The municipal fee's cap breaks the link between assessed value and tax for most houses: above the cap base, a higher valuation no longer raises the bill.
- The 2004 abolition passed under a Social Democratic government with left-party support, and the 2007 wealth-tax abolition under a centre-right one. Both main blocs took part in dismantling Swedish capital taxes on property and transfers.

### Gaps
- Revenue of the wealth tax in kronor for 2006 and the number of payers were not found in the proposition text read (first 100,000 characters).
- The full arguments in prop. 2007/08:1 section 5.4.1 (for example, links to ability to pay) were not read.
- Exact SFS numbers of the repealing acts were not checked: the search summary gave SFS 2007:545 and 2004:1341, both [U].

## 5. France: IGF (1982), ISF (1989–2017), IFI (2018–) and the 2025 "taxe Zucman" fight

### Takeaway
France's wealth tax was created (1982), abolished (1986), re-created (1989) and then narrowed in 2018 to real estate only (IFI). Under both ISF and IFI, real estate is self-declared at market value (valeur vénale) as of 1 January, with a 30% abatement on the main residence. The Cour des comptes found in 2024 that self-declared values risk "under-revaluation" when prices rise, controls are rare, and the €1.3m threshold has not moved since 2018. In 2025 a 2% minimum tax on fortunes above €100m (taxe Zucman) passed the Assemblée (20 February 2025), failed in the Sénat (12 June 2025) and was rejected again in the 2026 budget (31 October 2025). The finance law of 19 February 2026 instead created a 20% tax on luxury assets held through holding companies.

### Cited Findings
**History and design**
- IGF from 1982, abolished 1986, re-introduced as ISF in 1989 [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.1](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). The ISF was replaced by the IFI "with effect from 1 January 2018" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 94](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- ISF rates and threshold: the threshold rose in 2012 from €800,000 to €1,300,000, a change the OECD links to rising "asset values – most notably housing prices". In 2011 the top rate was cut from 1.80% to 0.50% and in 2012 raised again to 1.50%. A cap ("bouclier fiscal") limited total French and foreign taxes to 75% of income [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 81, 87–88](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- ISF valuation in 2017:
  - Main residences: France offered "tax allowances on the value of main residences, equal to 30%".
  - Market value vs taxe foncière: market value was used for the wealth tax, while the recurrent property tax (taxe foncière) is based on cadastral rental values.
  - Listed shares: last price or 30-day average, at the taxpayer's choice.
  - Unlisted companies: three methods combined (revalued net assets, a return-based value, a productivity value).
  - Valuation date: 1 January [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 83, 85–86](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- ISF revenue in 2016: 0.22% of GDP and 0.48% of total taxes [P] — [OECD 2018, Fig. 1.3](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). The ISF reached about 350,000 taxpayers, per Senate rapporteur Emmanuel Capus [S] — [Public Sénat, 12 Jun 2025](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/economie/taxe-zucman-le-senat-rejette-la-creation-dun-nouvel-impot-sur-les-ultra-riches)
- Migration: "In 2014, 780 taxpayers subject to the net wealth tax left France, while 300 returns were registered". The OECD adds it is "difficult to determine the extent to which decisions to move are motivated by tax factors" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 66](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)

**IFI in 2026: what is taxed and how real estate is valued**
- Liability arises when net taxable real-estate wealth is "supérieure à 1 300 000 € au 1er janvier 2026" (above €1.3m on 1 January 2026).
  - Each asset is declared "à sa valeur vénale réelle" (at its true market value).
  - The main residence "fait l'objet d'un abattement de 30 % sur sa valeur au 1er janvier 2026" (receives a 30% abatement on its 1 January value).
  - Debts existing on 1 January are deductible if justified.
  - Shares in property-holding companies count for "la valeur représentant les biens immobiliers détenus par la société" (the value representing the real estate the company holds), with exclusions for stakes under 10% [P] — [service-public.gouv.fr, verified 6 Mar 2026](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F563)
- Cour des comptes (published 25 January 2024):
  - Revenue and payers: the IFI raised €1.8bn in 2022, with revenue growing about 14.2% a year on average since 2018. About 164,000 households received an IFI notice in 2022, and the number of payers rose 23% from 2018 to 2022.
  - Threshold: it "est resté inchangé depuis sa création" (has stayed unchanged since creation) while property prices rose.
  - Valuation: taxpayers value their own property, which "demeure cependant un exercice délicat" (remains a delicate exercise). The Court proposes that the administration supply local price-trend data to limit "sous-revalorisation" (under-revaluation) when prices rise.
  - Controls: these remain infrequent, and the administration lacks information on property held through sociétés civiles immobilières.
  - Migration and assets: since 2018 returns of emigrants exceed departures, though no causal link to the IFI can be established, and no shift of wealth into financial assets was observed.
  - Cost: collection costs 2.66% of revenue (2022) against 1.05% for income tax, which "apparaît cependant élevé" (appears high) [P] — [Cour des comptes, "L'impôt sur la fortune immobilière"](https://www.ccomptes.fr/fr/publications/limpot-sur-la-fortune-immobiliere)
- French Wikipedia gives €5.067bn for ISF in 2017 and €1.9bn (2018) to €2.739bn (2024) for IFI, without sources. Its 2022 figure (€2.353bn) differs from the Cour des comptes' €1.8bn. Treat the Wikipedia figures only as leads [U] — [fr.wikipedia, IFI](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imp%C3%B4t_sur_la_fortune_immobili%C3%A8re)

**The 2025 "taxe Zucman" fight and its outcome**
- The bill (by deputies Éva Sas and Clémentine Autain) set a minimum tax of at least 2% on fortunes above €100m, adding private and professional wealth together and keeping liability for five years after a move abroad.
  - 20 February 2025: adopted by the Assemblée nationale.
  - 12 June 2025: rejected by the Sénat, 188 against and 129 for.
  - The rapporteur estimated about 1,800 taxpayers. Supporters estimated revenue of up to €20bn.
  - Opponents (rapporteur Capus, minister Amélie de Montchalin) called it confiscatory and raised constitutional fragility and tax exile [S] — [Public Sénat, 12 Jun 2025](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/economie/taxe-zucman-le-senat-rejette-la-creation-dun-nouvel-impot-sur-les-ultra-riches)
- 31 October 2025 (2026 budget, first reading): the Assemblée rejected the Sas amendment for a 2% minimum tax on worldwide net wealth above €100m (about 1,800 people). JSS reports 168 for and 231 against, and says fallback versions at 1% and 0.5% also failed. Montchalin argued "jamais la France n'a taxé des biens professionnels" (France has never taxed professional assets) [S] — [JSS, 1 Nov 2025](https://jss.fr/post/projet-taxe-zucman-largement-rejete-assemblee-nationale)
  - Search summaries attributed to other outlets give 228 against and 172 for, and mention a Socialist "taxe Zucman light" (3% above €10m, excluding innovative and family firms) also rejected. I did not fetch these pages, so the counts conflict [U] — [legifiscal.fr (not fetched)](https://www.legifiscal.fr/actualites-fiscales/4302-plf-2026-taxe-zucman-rejetee-assemblee-nationale.html)
- Outcome in the 2026 finance law:
  - No consensus was reached in Parliament; a special law (loi spéciale n° 2025-1316 du 26 décembre 2025) bridged the gap. The finance law is Loi n° 2026-103 du 19 février 2026.
  - The contribution différentielle sur les hauts revenus (CDHR) is extended until the deficit falls below 3% of GDP [S] — [Laplace, 28 Feb 2026](https://laplace-groupe.com/publications/actualite/la-loi-de-finances-pour-2026-enfin-promulguee-les-changements)
- The holdings tax (article 7 of the 2026 finance law, new CGI art. 235 ter C):
  - Rate and scope: 20% a year on the market value of "sumptuary" assets held by holding companies. These are companies with assets of at least €5m, passive income above 50%, and control by a natural person (at least 50%).
  - Assets covered: hunting and fishing assets; non-professional vehicles, yachts and aircraft; jewellery and precious metals; horses; wines and spirits; and housing reserved for the owner's or family's enjoyment.
  - Timing: it applies to financial years closed from 31 December 2026, and assets subject to it are exempt from IFI from 1 January 2027.
  - Origin: the government's initial version (filed 14 October 2025) taxed all non-professional assets including cash at 2%. The "amendement Juvin" (31 October 2025) narrowed it to sumptuary assets at 20%, and the Sénat (December 2025) reinstated that text [S] — [village-justice.com, Eve d'Onorio di Méo, 13 Feb 2026, updated 27 Apr 2026](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/nouvelle-taxe-holding-sur-les-actifs-somptuaires-detenus-par-les-societes,56172.html)

### Inferences
- Moving from ISF to IFI concentrated wealth taxation on the most visible and immobile asset, real estate, while keeping self-assessment of its value. The Cour des comptes' warning about under-revaluation in rising markets, and its note that the threshold was frozen, are the same valuation-lag problems seen in Germany and Sweden, here arising through self-declaration rather than official assessment.
- In the 2025–26 debate, objections centred on including professional and unlisted business assets, the category hardest to value.

### Gaps
- The IFI rate schedule (commonly 0.5–1.5%) and the décote were not checked in a primary source; the service-public page read did not show them.

- Garbinti et al.'s study of French wealth-tax elasticities and the France Stratégie evaluation of the 2018 reform were not accessed (search budget).

## 6. Norway: formuesskatt, valuation discounts, the 2022 increase, emigration and the 2026 housing model

### Takeaway
Norway has levied a national wealth tax since 1892. It values assets at fractions of market value: primary homes at 25% of a statistically estimated value (70% above a high-value threshold), secondary homes at 100%, and shares at 80% (2026). In 2022 the government raised rates and cut the discount on business assets, followed by a widely reported wave of wealthy emigrants, mostly to Switzerland. In 2026 a new SSB model for home values raised more revenue than expected, so the government raised the high-value threshold from NOK 10m to 14m. This shows valuation models directly reshaping the tax.

### Cited Findings
**Tax profile**
- National tax since 1892, with a municipal and a national part [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.1 (p. 76) and p. 78](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Rates over time:
  - The rate fell from 1.1% in 2013 to 0.85%, with higher thresholds, "to promote Norwegian ownership and investment in business assets". The threshold reached NOK 1.48m (single) in 2017 [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 81, 87](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
  - Pages fetched with year parameters (year labels not shown in the extracted text, so treat the year mapping as likely but unconfirmed):
    - `?year=2021`: threshold NOK 1.5m, 0.7% municipal plus 0.15% state (0.85% total).
    - `?year=2023`: threshold NOK 1.7m, 0.7% municipal plus 0.3% state up to NOK 20m and 0.4% above (1.0% and 1.1% total) [P/U] — [Skatteetaten 2021](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesskatt/?year=2021); [Skatteetaten 2023](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesskatt/?year=2023)
  - 2026: threshold NOK 1.9m (doubled for couples), 0.35% municipal plus 0.65% state, and 0.75% state above NOK 21.5m [P] — [Skatteetaten 2026](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesskatt/?year=2026). Confirmed by the Finance Ministry: "1,0 pst. ... over et bunnfradrag på 1,9 mill. kroner i 2026 ... over 21,5 mill. kroner ... 1,1 pst." [P] — [Answer to question 1957, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.stortinget.no/globalassets/pdf/dokumentserien/2025-2026/dok15-202526-1957-vedlegg.pdf)
- 2026 valuation discounts (verdsettingsrabatter) as displayed:
  - Primary home: 75% discount up to the high-value threshold, and a 30% discount (that is, 70% of value) above NOK 14m.
  - Secondary home: 0%.
  - "Shares, including commercial property": 20%.
  - Operating assets (driftsmidler): 30%.
  - Discounts apply only to directly owned assets, and debt allocated to discounted assets is reduced proportionally [P] — [Skatteetaten 2026](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesskatt/?year=2026)
- Homes, 2026: "25 % av boligverdien opp til kr. 14 000 000 og deretter 70 % av den overskytende boligverdien" (25% of the home's value up to NOK 14m, then 70% of the excess). Secondary homes count at 100%.
  - Who values and how: Skatteetaten estimates the home value (boligverdi) from SSB statistics on sales, using location, floor area, year built and dwelling type.
  - Safety valve: owners can document a lower market value, because "Formuesverdier som overstiger disse grensene, kan kreves nedjustert" (wealth-tax values above these limits can be reduced on request) [P] — [Skatteetaten, formuesverdi bolig 2026](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesverdi-bolig/?year=2026)
- Unlisted firms are effectively valued at book values, which "may be significantly lower than market values (as is the case in Norway, for instance)", giving "an incentive to invest in non-listed firms relative to listed firms" [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 63–64](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Revenue in 2016: 0.43% of GDP and 1.13% of total taxes [P] — [OECD 2018, Fig. 1.3](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)

**The 2026 housing-model episode (valuation in action)**
- The Finance Minister writes that "Flere formuesobjekter verdsettes imidlertid til dels betydelig lavere enn markedsverdi" (several assets are valued in part substantially below market value), because of formal discounts and because some assets are hard to value.
  - Following a 2021 Storting request, the government adopted SSB's updated housing model for the 2026 wealth tax. "Et viktig premiss for regjeringen var at staten ikke skulle tjene på omleggingen" (a key premise was that the state should not profit from the change), so the basic allowance was raised.
  - When SSB's January 2026 estimates showed the model would raise more than assumed, the government proposed (27 February 2026) raising the threshold for high valuation of primary homes from NOK 10m to 14m.
  - About 109,500 people would pay over NOK 200 less from that change alone. With all 2026 wealth-tax changes, about 459,000 pay less and 196,000 more [P] — [Answer to question 1957, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.stortinget.no/globalassets/pdf/dokumentserien/2025-2026/dok15-202526-1957-vedlegg.pdf)
- The combined revenue effect is a NOK 280m (accrual) reduction against the reference. The estimate ignores that more owners may use the safety valve to lower their estimated values [P] — [Answer to question 1769, 9 Mar 2026](https://www.stortinget.no/globalassets/pdf/dokumentserien/2025-2026/dok15-202526-1769-vedlegg.pdf)
- On 12 February 2026 the rule still read "25 pst. ... opp til 10 mill. kroner, og 70 pst. ... over 10 mill. kroner" [P] — [Answer to question 1405, 12 Feb 2026](https://www.stortinget.no/globalassets/pdf/dokumentserien/2025-2026/dok15-202526-1405-vedlegg.pdf). Skatteetaten's 2026 page now shows NOK 14m [P] — [Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesverdi-bolig/?year=2026)

**2022 changes and emigration**
- In 2022 the centre-left government "raised the wealth tax level, raised the dividend tax, and scaled back the reduction on business assets". The tax was levied above NOK 1.7m at up to 1.1%.
  - At least 30 billionaires and millionaires left for Switzerland and elsewhere in 2022 (according to public filings to the population registry), with a combined fortune of NOK 29bn and NOK 550m in tax paid. Dagens Næringsliv counted the 2022 exodus as exceeding the previous 13 years combined.
  - Kjell Inge Røkke moved to Lugano in September 2022. An exit tax was planned.
  - A state secretary defended the tax as needed for the welfare state [S] — [Financial Times via Swissinfo, 16 Dec 2022](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/rich-norwegians-flee-to-low-tax-switzerland-as-wealth-levy-bites)
- A government commission report, NOU 2026:9 ("Veien mot et bedre og mer forutsigbart skattesystem"), reportedly presented on 24 June 2026, includes a chapter on the wealth tax. regjeringen.no blocked access, so its content is unverified [U] — [NOU 2026:9 chapter link (blocked)](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/nou-2026-9/id3167167/?ch=7)

### Inferences
- Norway shows that a wealth tax can survive with low thresholds when politically sensitive assets (primary homes, business shares) are valued at deep discounts. The trade-off is that any change in the valuation model (as in 2026) or in the discounts (as in 2022) is effectively a change in the tax and draws a political response.
- Who values homes in Norway is the tax agency, using a national statistical model rather than individual appraisal. This is a different route from Sweden (official assessment rolls) and France (self-declaration).

### Gaps
- Official (SSB or Skatteetaten) statistics on numbers of wealth-tax payers, revenue in kroner, and emigration of wealthy taxpayers after 2022 were not obtained; SSB URLs tried returned 404.
- The exact 2022 and 2023 discounts for shares and expensive homes were not confirmed in a primary source.
- Peer-reviewed Norwegian studies (for example on saving responses and firm effects) were not reached because of the search budget.
- The exit-tax rules (2024) were not verified.

## 7. Spain: Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio, regional variation, and the 2022 solidarity tax

### Takeaway
Spain values real estate at the highest of three figures: cadastral value, any value the administration has set for other taxes, or the purchase price. Unlisted shares are valued at book value from the last audited balance sheet, and qualifying family-business shares are exempt. Regions set thresholds and rates, and some reduced the tax to near zero. In 2022 the state added a "solidarity" tax on net wealth above €3m to recapture tax foregone in those regions. Courts upheld it in 2023, and it has been extended until regional financing is reformed. It remains in force per the BOE consolidated text read on 4 November 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Tax profile (Ley 19/1991, de 6 de junio, in force from 1 January 1992)**
- Real estate (art. 10.Uno, as amended by Ley 11/2021): "Por el mayor valor de los tres siguientes: El valor catastral, el determinado o comprobado por la Administración a efectos de otros tributos o el precio, contraprestación o valor de la adquisición." (At the highest of: the cadastral value, the value determined or checked by the administration for other taxes, or the purchase price or acquisition value.) [P] — [BOE, Ley 19/1991](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- Unlisted shares (art. 16): "por el valor teórico resultante del último balance aprobado" (at the book value from the last approved balance sheet), if audited with a favourable opinion. Otherwise the highest of nominal value, book value, or the value from capitalising average profits of the last three years at 20% [P] — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- Listed shares: average trading value of the last quarter [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 86](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Exemptions (art. 4):
  - Business assets used in the owner's main activity are exempt, and so are shares in entities that do not mainly manage movable or real-estate wealth, if ownership and management conditions are met.
  - The main home ("vivienda habitual") is exempt "hasta un importe máximo de 300.000 euros" (up to €300,000) [P] — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- Allowance and rates:
  - Allowance: €700,000 unless the region sets its own (art. 28).
  - Default state scale: 0.2% to 3.5%, with the top rate above €10,695,996.06 (art. 30.2). Regions may approve their own scales (art. 30.1).
  - Cap (art. 31): wealth tax plus income tax may not exceed 60% of the income-tax base, but the reduction cannot exceed 80% of the wealth tax [P] — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- Suspension and return: Ley 4/2008 (23 December 2008) effectively zeroed the tax. RDL 13/2011 (16 September 2011) reinstated it temporarily, with yearly extensions through budget laws. Ley 11/2020 (30 December 2020) repealed the sunset from 2021 [P] — [BOE annotations](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392); [OECD 2018, pp. 16–18](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Before 2008, regional thresholds ranged from €108,182 to €150,000. After 2011 the central threshold was €700,000 and the main-home exemption was "almost doubled". Top regional rates in 2017 included 3.03% in Andalucía, 3% in Murcia and 2.75% in Cataluña [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 81, 87](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Revenue in 2016: 0.18% of GDP and 0.54% of total taxes [P] — [OECD 2018, Fig. 1.3](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)

**The family-business exemption and avoidance**
- The 1994 exemption covered owner-managers holding at least 15% (20% with family) who drew over 50% of income from the business. In 2003 the stake requirement fell to 5%. Alvaredo and Saez (2009) found it "progressively and substantially eroded the wealth tax base", with strong shifting by business owners. Durán-Cabré et al. (2017) found that after 2011 "taxpayers transfer part of their wealth in real estate, bank accounts and non-exempted business holdings to exempted business holdings" [P: OECD summarising S-sch] — [OECD 2018, p. 91](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)

**Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas (ITSGF)**
- Created by art. 3 of Ley 38/2022 (27 December 2022) as a state tax that is not ceded to the regions and complements the wealth tax, on net wealth above €3,000,000.
  - Rates: 0% on the first €3m, 1.7% to €5,347,998.03, 2.1% to €10,695,996.06, and 3.5% above. Regional wealth tax actually paid is deducted, and the 60% cap with a maximum 80% reduction applies.
  - It was originally valid for two tax years [P] — [BOE, Ley 38/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22684)
- Purposes (preamble): first, revenue, "una muestra de solidaridad de las grandes fortunas" (a show of solidarity by large fortunes). Second, "La segunda finalidad es armonizadora, con el objetivo de disminuir las diferencias en el gravamen del patrimonio en las distintas CC.AA., especialmente para que la carga tributaria de los contribuyentes residentes en aquellas CC.AA. que han desfiscalizado total o parcialmente el Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio no difiera sustancialmente..." (to harmonise, reducing differences in wealth taxation between regions, especially so that taxpayers in regions that have wholly or partly removed the wealth tax do not face a substantially different burden) [P] — [BOE, Ley 38/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22684)
- Status: extended by RDL 8/2023 (DA 5.2) "en tanto no se produzca la revisión de la tributación patrimonial en el contexto de la reforma del sistema de financiación autonómica". The BOE consolidated text, read on 4 November 2026 with its last update published 21 December 2024 (on other articles), shows art. 3 still in force [P] — [BOE consolidated](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22684)
- Court challenges dismissed: STC 149/2023 (7 November 2023), STC 170/2023 and 171/2023 (22 November 2023), and STC 189/2023 and 190/2023 (12 December 2023; 190 "en relación al art. 3") [P] — [BOE analysis](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22684)

### Inferences
- Spain's "highest of three values" rule for real estate works as a floor that ties the wealth tax to other valuations: the cadastre, and values set for transfer or inheritance taxes. Unlisted companies, by contrast, are valued at book value, which is often far below market (see the OECD and Wealth Tax Commission on book values). Together with the family-business exemption, this makes holding assets in a qualifying company the main avoidance route, as the empirical studies cited by the OECD find.
- The ITSGF is a direct response to tax competition between regions over wealth taxation, which is unusual in the literature. It is enacted as a federal-level floor rather than a new valuation regime: it reuses the wealth-tax base and valuation rules.

### Gaps
- Revenue of the wealth tax and the ITSGF for 2022–2025 (Agencia Tributaria statistics) was not obtained.
- Which regions apply 100% relief in 2026 (for example Madrid and Andalucía) was not verified in BOE or regional gazettes.
- Whether the 2021 amendment of art. 10 (adding "determinado") was meant to bring in the new catastral "valor de referencia" was not verified [U].

## 8. Switzerland: cantonal wealth taxes, below-market real-estate values, and the 30 November 2025 JUSO vote

### Takeaway
All Swiss cantons tax net wealth, with low thresholds and rates that vary by canton (top rates 0.13%–1.0% in 2012). Real estate is typically valued well below market, at about 60% of market value or a roughly 40% discount on main homes. The tax raised about 1% of GDP in 2016, the most in the OECD. On 30 November 2025, voters rejected a JUSO initiative for a 50% federal inheritance tax on estates above CHF 50m (21.72% yes, no canton in favour). Two months earlier they had approved a constitutional basis for cantonal taxes on second homes, linked to abolishing the tax on imputed rent.

### Cited Findings
**Tax profile**
- Introduced by the cantons between 1840 and 1970 [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.1](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). There is no federal wealth tax, and cantons and municipalities set rates [S] — [FT via Swissinfo, 2022](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/rich-norwegians-flee-to-low-tax-switzerland-as-wealth-levy-bites)
- Thresholds (2014) ranged from CHF 25,000 (Obwalden) to CHF 200,000 (Ticino). In Bern, 30% of all taxpayers and 41% of married households had a wealth-tax liability over 2001–2011. The OECD suggests low thresholds may partly reflect the absence of a capital-gains tax and limited property taxes [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 80–81](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Top rates in 2012 "ranging from 0.13% to 1.0%" across cantons, with a downward trend in central cantons. Seven of 26 cantons had caps on total tax [P] — [OECD 2018, pp. 86–88](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Real-estate valuation: "In Switzerland, as a general rule, housing is taxed at 60% of its market value" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 84](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). "Even Switzerland, which is remarkable for its broadly-based annual wealth tax, nevertheless provides a 40% valuation discount on main residences" [S-sch] — [Wealth Tax Commission 2020, p. 51](https://www.wealthandpolicy.com/wp/WealthTaxFinalReport.pdf)
- Revenue in 2016: 1.03% of GDP and 3.70% of total taxes. Switzerland "has always stood out as an exception" [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 18](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Behavioural evidence (Brülhart, Gruber, Krapf & Schmidheiny): a 0.1 percentage-point higher wealth-tax rate goes with 3.4% lower reported wealth across cantons [P: OECD summarising S-sch] — [OECD 2018, p. 62](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf). On mobility the readings conflict:
  - OECD (2018), reading the 2017 version: responses are "driven by changes in wealth holdings rather than mobility".
  - Wealth Tax Commission (2020), citing the 2019 version: internal migration makes up "one-third of the behavioural response in Switzerland" [U] — [WTC 2020, p. 90](https://www.wealthandpolicy.com/wp/WealthTaxFinalReport.pdf)

**30 November 2025: JUSO "Initiative für eine Zukunft"**
- Official title: Volksinitiative «Für eine soziale Klimapolitik – steuerlich gerecht finanziert (Initiative für eine Zukunft)». The authors were JUSO, with 109,988 signatures, and both chambers of Parliament recommended rejection.
  - Result: rejected with 21.72% yes and 0 of 23 cantonal votes. Turnout 42.95% [P: official results as compiled by Swissvotes, University of Bern] — [Swissvotes 681](https://swissvotes.ch/vote/681.00)
  - The Chancellery-funded post-vote survey also reports 21.7% yes and turnout of about 43% [P] — [VOX summary, gfs.bern, Jan 2026](https://vox.gfsbern.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/de_zusammenfassung_vox_november_2025_def2.pdf)
- Content: a federal inheritance and gift tax of 50% on amounts above CHF 50m, with the first CHF 50m tax-free and revenue for climate policy. Existing cantonal inheritance taxes would be unaffected [S] — [Homburger](https://homburger.ch/en/insights/introduction-of-a-federal-inheritance-tax-rejected) (content as given in search summaries; page not fetched)
- Why it lost (VOX): "Die Vorlage zur Erbschaftssteuer wurde insgesamt als zu extrem wahrgenommen" (the inheritance-tax proposal was seen overall as too extreme). "Besonders wirksam waren Argumente zur Gefährdung von Familienunternehmen und zum möglichen Wegzug Vermögender" (the most effective arguments were the threat to family firms and the possible departure of the wealthy). Only 53% of SP sympathisers voted yes, against 69% of Greens and 4–9% of centre-right parties [P] — [VOX summary](https://vox.gfsbern.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/de_zusammenfassung_vox_november_2025_def2.pdf)

**28 September 2025: property-tax decree**
- The "Bundesbeschluss über die kantonalen Liegenschaftssteuern auf Zweitliegenschaften" (keywords: Eigenmietwert, Zweitwohnungen) was accepted with 57.73% yes and 16.5 of 23 cantonal votes, on turnout of 49.50% [P: as compiled] — [Swissvotes 678](https://swissvotes.ch/vote/678.00)

### Inferences
- The Swiss case suggests that a broad wealth tax can raise meaningful revenue while valuing real estate well below market. This trades valuation precision for acceptance across a middle class that pays the tax. Departures from market value create incentives to hold business assets or hold assets through businesses, as the Wealth Tax Commission notes (see section 11).
- The JUSO vote focused on transfer taxation of family firms. Their value is fixed once at death, unlike the annual wealth tax's recurring valuation, but in the campaign it was the size of the levy that drove the opposing arguments, not how assets would be valued.

## 9. Denmark (abolished 1997), Colombia, Argentina, Uruguay

### Takeaway
Denmark had one of the highest wealth-tax rates (2.2%) until 1989 and abolished the tax in 1996–97. The best evidence on long-run wealth responses comes from that episode. Colombia made its long series of temporary wealth taxes permanent in 2022: it applies from 72,000 UVT, excludes the first 12,000 UVT of the main home, and takes unlisted shares at tax cost capped by intrinsic value. Argentina's Bienes Personales (1991–) values real estate at inflation-indexed cost, with the provincial fiscal valuation as a floor, and is legislated to fall to 0.25% in 2027. Uruguay could not be covered.

### Cited Findings
**Denmark**
- Formueskat, 1903–1997 [P] — [OECD 2018, Table 4.1](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- Rates and threshold: the threshold sat around the 97th–98th percentile of household wealth. The marginal rate was 2.2% until the late 1980s, cut to 1% between 1989 and 1991, with the couples' threshold doubled. The tax was abolished in 1996–97. A ceiling capped total personal taxes, set at 78% of income at the time of the 1989 reform [S-sch] — [Jakobsen, Jakobsen, Kleven & Zucman, NBER WP 24371 (2018), pp. 1, 9–11](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24371/w24371.pdf); published as *QJE* 135(1), 2020
- Base and reporting: the base included cash, deposits, bonds, equities, housing, large durables and business assets, net of debts, excluding pensions. Banks and financial institutions reported many items, but cash, durables, unlisted equities, non-corporate business assets and foreign assets were self-reported [S-sch] — [Jakobsen et al., p. 9](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24371/w24371.pdf)
- Effects: taxable wealth rose about 30% after 8 years for the very wealthy after the 1989 cuts (ceiling design) and about 10% for the moderately wealthy. Simulated long-run effects were about 70% after 30 years for the super-wealthy and about 20% for the moderately wealthy. Bunching at the threshold was small, about 0.3% of wealth. The authors caution that the super-wealthy have "particularly effective avoidance and evasion vehicles" [S-sch] — [Jakobsen et al., pp. 2–3, 14](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24371/w24371.pdf)

**Colombia (Ley 2277 de 2022, 13 December 2022, Diario Oficial 52.247)**
- Taxable event: "El impuesto al patrimonio se genera por la posesión del mismo al primero (1) de enero de cada año" (the tax arises from holding wealth on 1 January each year) for net wealth of at least 72,000 UVT. It covers resident individuals and some non-residents and foreign entities [P] — [DIAN normograma](https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/ley_2277_2022.htm)
- Main home: "se excluyen las primeras doce mil (12.000) UVT del valor patrimonial" (the first 12,000 UVT of the home's value are excluded), for the dwelling where the person mainly lives only.
- Unlisted shares: valued at tax cost ("costo fiscal"), but "Si el valor calculado conforme a la anterior regla es mayor al valor intrínseco de las acciones, se tomará este último" (if that value exceeds the shares' intrinsic value, the latter is used).
- Rates: 0.5% from 72,000 to 122,000 UVT, 1.0% from 122,000 to 239,000 UVT, and 1.5% above. The 1.5% bracket "sólo aplicará de manera temporal durante los años 2023, 2024, 2025 y 2026" (applies only temporarily in 2023–2026) [P] — [DIAN normograma](https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/ley_2277_2022.htm)
- Enforcement evidence: "roughly two-fifths of the wealthiest 0.01 percent evade taxes, and these evaders conceal about one-third of their wealth offshore". Stronger enforcement (a voluntary-disclosure programme, the Panama Papers) significantly affected compliance and revenue [S-sch] — [Londoño-Vélez & Ávila-Mahecha 2021, AER: Insights 3(2): 131–48](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20200319)

**Argentina (Impuesto sobre los Bienes Personales, Ley 23.966 Title VI, passed 1 August 1991)**
- Real-estate valuation (art. 22.a): acquisition cost indexed for inflation, less 2% a year depreciation on buildings. The value "no podrá ser inferior al de la base imponible ... fijada a los efectos del pago de los impuestos inmobiliarios o tributos similares" (may not be lower than the base fixed for provincial real-estate taxes), or the fiscal value at 31 December [P] — [InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/365/texact.htm)
- Threshold and home exemption (art. 24, as replaced by Ley 27.743, Boletín Oficial 8 July 2024, effective from fiscal year 2023): non-taxable minimum ARS 100,000,000. A home ("casa-habitación") worth up to ARS 350,000,000 is not taxed. Amounts are indexed each year to the CPI [P] — [InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/365/texact.htm)
- Rates (art. 25, per Ley 27.743): fiscal year 2025, 0.50%–1.00%; 2026, 0.50%–0.75%; 2027, a flat 0.25%. Residence replaced domicile as the connecting criterion from 2019 (Ley 27.541) [P] — [InfoLEG](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/365/texact.htm)

### Inferences
- Both Latin American cases anchor real estate to administrative values: in Argentina the provincial fiscal valuation acts as a floor, and Colombia uses tax-cost rules. Both treat unlisted shares at historical or book-type values. This is the same "below-market" pattern as in Europe.
- The Danish and Colombian studies are the most cited evidence that responses at the very top are large, in Denmark through accumulation, avoidance or evasion and in Colombia through offshore concealment.

### Gaps
- Uruguay's Impuesto al Patrimonio (rates, use of Catastro "valor real") could not be checked: the IMPO site reset the connection and no other address was available after the search budget ran out.
- Colombia's pre-2022 temporary wealth taxes (2002 onward) and any 2025–26 emergency-decree changes were not verified.
- Argentina's 2020 "Aporte Solidario y Extraordinario" (Ley 27.605) and its 2024 special advance-payment regime (REIBP) were not verified.
- How Denmark valued real estate for its wealth tax (public assessments) was not confirmed.

## 10. The G20 billionaire-tax discussion (2024–) and 2026 ballot measures

### Takeaway
In June 2024 Gabriel Zucman's report to Brazil's G20 presidency proposed a coordinated minimum tax equal to 2% of billionaires' wealth, about 3,000 people, raising $200–250bn a year. G20 leaders in November 2024 committed only to "seek to engage cooperatively to ensure that ultra-high-net-worth individuals are effectively taxed". The national follow-ups examined here all stalled: the French taxe Zucman (2025), the Swiss JUSO inheritance tax (2025) and California's Proposition 40 (one-time 5% on billionaires, real estate excluded), which was trailing on 4 November 2026 with counting ongoing.

### Cited Findings
**Zucman report (25 June 2024)**
- "individuals with more than $1 billion in wealth would be required to pay a minimum amount of tax annually, equal to 2% of their wealth". Countries could implement this through "a presumptive income tax, an income tax on a broad notion of income, or a wealth tax".
- It would raise "$200-$250 billion per year globally from about 3,000 taxpayers". Extending it to centi-millionaires would add $100–140bn. Billionaires' current effective tax rate is equivalent to 0.3% of wealth, and their average real pre-tax return was 7.5% a year over four decades [P: commissioned report] — [Zucman 2024, p. 1](https://www.taxobservatory.eu/www-site/uploads/2024/06/report-g20.pdf)
- Valuation: "about half of the wealth of global billionaires is in shares of publicly listed companies, which are straightforward to value. The other half consists mostly of shares in private companies."
  - Private businesses would be valued with multiples from comparable listed firms (to profits, assets and sales), or from transactions in private shares. Art would use insured values.
  - To avoid an incentive to stay private, the report suggests valuing listed companies "like private firms" [P] — [Zucman 2024, pp. 33–34](https://www.taxobservatory.eu/www-site/uploads/2024/06/report-g20.pdf)

**G20 Rio Leaders' Declaration (18 November 2024)**
- Para. 19: "Progressive taxation is one of the key tools to reduce domestic inequalities, strengthen fiscal sustainability...". "We endorse the Rio de Janeiro G20 Ministerial Declaration on International Tax Cooperation."
- Para. 20: "With full respect to tax sovereignty, we will seek to engage cooperatively to ensure that ultra-high-net-worth individuals are effectively taxed." [P] — [G20 Rio Leaders' Declaration](https://www.g20.utoronto.ca/2024/241118-declaration.html)

**California Proposition 40 (2026 Billionaire Tax Act), vote of 3 November 2026**
- LAO (11 December 2025):
  - Rate and timing: billionaires living in California on 1 January 2026 would pay a one-time state tax of 5% of net worth, due in 2027, with an option to spread payments over five years at extra cost.
  - Exclusions: "Real estate, pensions, and retirement accounts would be excluded from the tax."
  - Revenue: "probably would add up to tens of billions of dollars spread over several years". There would likely be an ongoing loss of state income tax of "hundreds of millions of dollars or more per year" if billionaires leave, and administration costs of "tens of millions of dollars per year" [P] — [LAO, A.G. File No. 25-0024 Amdt #1](https://www.lao.ca.gov/ballot/2025/250481.pdf)
- Valuation rules (law-firm summary):
  - Real estate: directly held real property, or property held through a revocable trust, is excluded, but real estate held through an LLC or other entity is captured.
  - Listed and private assets: listed assets at market price on 31 December 2026. Private businesses are presumed worth book value plus 7.5 times three-year average book profits, which a certified appraisal can rebut. A financing round in the prior two years sets a floor.
  - Other assets: insured value is a floor for tangible personal property, and appraisals are required for assets over $1m.
  - Rate and payment: a flat 5% on the whole net worth, phased in between $1.0bn and $1.1bn. Five annual instalments carry a 7.5% deferral charge [S] — [BDO, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/californias-billionaire-tax-proposal-would-allow-sweeping-one-time-taxation-based-on-net-worth)
- Campaign and early count:
  - Sponsor: SEIU-UHW. The measure would affect about 200 billionaires, with sponsors estimating about $100bn over five years.
  - Competing measures: Props 41 and 42 were placed by opponents, and either would nullify Prop 40 if it won more votes. Prop 42 would bar taxes on financial assets, private businesses and other wealth.
  - Spending: opponents spent $316.7m, including Sergey Brin's $102m.
  - Early count: with 54% counted early on 4 November, CalMatters reported Prop 40 trailing, citing an AP figure of 56% (the article does not make clear whether this is the no share). Prop 42 was leading at about 55%. A search summary attributed a 44.4% yes vs 55.6% no count with 60% counted to another outlet; it was not fetched [S; U for exact shares] — [CalMatters, 3 Nov 2026, updated 4 Nov](https://calmatters.org/health/2026/11/california-election-2026-prop-40-41-42/)
- KQED reported Prop 40 "well below a majority Tuesday night" and "too early to call". It put opponents' fundraising at over $330m and the yes campaign at about $33m [S] — [KQED, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.kqed.org/news/12107038/california-billionaire-tax-trails-in-early-results)

### Inferences
- California's design avoided the land-valuation problem by excluding directly held real estate, which California already taxes through its property tax, and put the valuation burden on private companies through a formula with an appraisal option. That is the same asset class the Zucman report and the Wealth Tax Commission identify as the core difficulty.

## 11. Across cases: how real estate was valued compared with securities and private businesses, and the evidence on valuation-driven avoidance

### Takeaway
In almost every case real estate entered the wealth-tax base below market value. This came about through stale official assessments (Germany 1964 values), assessed values set at about 75% of market (Sweden), cadastral floors (Spain, Argentina), statistical models with discounts (Norway 25%), self-declaration with abatements (France 30%), or cantonal practice (Switzerland about 60%). Listed securities were generally taken at or near market value. Unlisted businesses were valued at book value or by formula, or exempted. The resulting unequal treatment was the legal reason for Germany's suspension and the main channel of avoidance documented in Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

- Larger responses:
  - Spain: shifting into exempt business holdings [P via OECD; S-sch] — [OECD 2018, p. 91](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
  - Switzerland: 3.4% less wealth per 0.1pp of tax [P via OECD]

  - Colombia: offshore concealment by about two-fifths of the top 0.01% [S-sch] — [Londoño-Vélez & Ávila-Mahecha](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20200319)

  - Netherlands: a 1% higher wealth tax leads to 0.10–0.17% lower savings (Zoutman 2015) [P via OECD] — [OECD 2018, p. 62](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
  - France: since 2018, returns exceed departures, with no causal claim, and no shift into financial assets observed [P] — [Cour des comptes 2024](https://www.ccomptes.fr/fr/publications/limpot-sur-la-fortune-immobiliere)
- Scheuer & Slemrod: responses "sometimes" large, "but not always", depending on base breadth and enforcement [S-sch] — [JEP 2021](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.35.1.207)

### Inferences
- For the land-tax chronology: wealth taxes inherited the valuation infrastructure of land and property taxes (German unit values, Swedish assessment rolls, Spanish cadastre, Argentine provincial valuations). Their fate therefore depended on how current those land valuations were. Where valuation was stale or discounted, real estate was favoured relative to financial assets (Germany). Where valuations were updated sharply, the tax suddenly reached ordinary homeowners (Sweden in the late 1990s, Norway's 2026 model). Both outcomes produced legal or political pressure.

### Gaps
- No systematic cross-country data were found on assessment ratios (assessed or market value) for wealth-tax real estate beyond the OECD's general statements.

## 12. Plain-language explainers

### Takeaway
Short definitions a newcomer needs to follow the cases.

### Cited Findings
- **Net wealth tax**: a recurring (usually annual) tax on the value of a person's assets minus debts. It differs from a property tax, which falls only on real estate and usually allows no debt deduction, and from a one-off capital levy [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 16](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- **Unit value (Einheitswert)**: a German official tax value for land and buildings, fixed at a general valuation date (Hauptfeststellung) and meant to be renewed every six years. In practice it was frozen at 1964 (West) values [P] — [BVerfG 21/2018](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html)
- **Taxeringsvärde**: the Swedish tax-assessed value of a property, set at about 75% of market value. It is the base for the property fee and was the base for the wealth tax [S-sch] — [Du Rietz & Henrekson](https://www.ifn.se/media/apndv2ws/wp1000.pdf); [Prop. 2007/08:1](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:1)
- **Valor catastral**: the Spanish cadastral value recorded by the land registry for tax purposes. Under the wealth tax it is one of three candidate values, and the highest is used [P] — [BOE art. 10](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- **Valeur vénale (réelle)**: French for market value, the price a willing buyer would pay. For IFI it is self-declared as of 1 January [P] — [service-public](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F563)
- **Valuation discount (verdsettingsrabatt)**: a rule that counts only a fraction of an asset's estimated value, for example 25% for a Norwegian primary home [P] — [Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/formuesverdi-bolig/?year=2026)
- **Book value (valor teórico, costo fiscal)**: a company's accounting net assets. It usually omits goodwill and unrealised gains, so it often understates what the company would sell for [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 86](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf)
- **Third-party reporting**: banks and brokers report balances and security values directly to the tax authority. Self-reported items (cash, cars, unlisted shares) are where underreporting concentrates [S-sch] — [Jakobsen et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24371/w24371.pdf); [Seim](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf)
- **Bunching and elasticity of taxable wealth**: if many people report wealth just below a tax threshold ("bunching"), that shows a response. The elasticity measures how much reported wealth changes when the after-tax return changes [S-sch] — [Seim](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf)
- **Tax cap (bouclier fiscal, límite conjunto)**: a ceiling on wealth tax plus income tax as a share of income, for example 75% of income in France (ISF) and 60% of the income-tax base in Spain, with at least 20% of the wealth tax always due in Spain [P] — [OECD 2018, p. 88](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/the-role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-oecd_g1g89919/9789264290303-en.pdf); [BOE art. 31](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1991-14392)
- **Minimum tax (impôt plancher, "Zucman tax")**: a top-up so that total taxes paid reach at least a set share of wealth, for example 2%. Someone already paying that much owes nothing extra [P] — [Zucman 2024](https://www.taxobservatory.eu/www-site/uploads/2024/06/report-g20.pdf)
- **One-off and annual wealth tax**: a one-off tax values assets once, at a past date, so it cannot be dodged by later behaviour. An annual tax needs repeated valuations [S-sch] — [WTC 2020](https://www.wealthandpolicy.com/wp/WealthTaxFinalReport.pdf)
- **Imputed rental value (Eigenmietwert)**: Swiss taxation of the notional rent an owner-occupier "pays" to themselves. Voters approved a related constitutional change in September 2025 [P] — [Swissvotes 678](https://swissvotes.ch/vote/678.00)

### Inferences
- None beyond the definitions.

### Gaps
- None material.

## 13. Scenes and anecdotes (well sourced)

### Takeaway
Seven concrete scenes that show the valuation story.

### Cited Findings
1. **The revaluation that never came (Germany, 1970–2018).** In 1970 Parliament decided the next general valuation of land after 1964 would be set "by special law". Forty-eight years later the Constitutional Court noted drily, "Ein solches Gesetz ist bis heute nicht verabschiedet worden" (no such law has been passed to this day). By then the frozen values had already brought down the wealth tax in 1995 [P] — [BVerfG 21/2018](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2018/bvg18-021.html); [BVerfG 1995](https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv093121.html)
2. **A tax that died by deadline (Germany, 31 December 1996).** No law repealed the German wealth tax. A 1996 repeal bill and its rivals all failed, the court's deadline passed, and the Act has been inapplicable since 1 January 1997 while staying on the statute book [P] — [Kube 2021](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/833422/a776a6209e9c97f66c820a0ae8fad354/04-Kube.pdf)
3. **"Under 20% to over 100%" (Germany, 2006).** The Court found that the shortcut used to value built-up property for inheritance tax gave values averaging about half of market value. For individual houses it could produce anything from under a fifth of market value to more than the full price [P] — [BVerfG press release 11/2007](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2007/bvg07-011.html)
4. **Homeowners pulled in by rising assessments (Sweden, 1990s–2007).** After a renewed housing-valuation procedure in the late 1990s, Sweden repeatedly raised its wealth-tax thresholds. When abolishing the tax, the government noted that "många småhusägare" (many small-house owners) were paying it "på grund av snabbt stigande taxeringsvärden" (because of rapidly rising tax-assessed values) [S-sch/P] — [Seim 2017](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/seimAEJ17wealth.pdf); [Prop. 2007/08:26](https://lagen.nu/prop/2007/08:26)
5. **"Paying taxes on money I don't have" (Norway, 2022).** As Norway raised its wealth tax, crypto founder Fredrik Haga (31) prepared to register his move to Zug: "It's about paying taxes on money I don't have." Kjell Inge Røkke, once Norway's richest man, had moved to Lugano in September 2022 [S] — [FT via Swissinfo, 16 Dec 2022](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/rich-norwegians-flee-to-low-tax-switzerland-as-wealth-levy-bites)
6. **A new model, a surprise surplus (Norway, 2026).** Norway's government promised a revenue-neutral switch to a new statistical model for valuing homes. When SSB's January 2026 numbers showed it would raise more than planned, the government lifted the "expensive home" threshold from NOK 10m to 14m to hand the money back, cutting tax for about 109,500 people [P] — [Answer to question 1957](https://www.stortinget.no/globalassets/pdf/dokumentserien/2025-2026/dok15-202526-1957-vedlegg.pdf)
7. **Yachts, racehorses and wine cellars (France, 2026).** France rejected the 2% taxe Zucman, and the government's own 2% tax on holding companies' idle assets shrank in Parliament. What emerged is a 20% annual tax on luxuries parked in holding companies, including yachts, jets, jewellery, horses, wines and spirits, and family homes held for private use [S] — [village-justice.com](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/nouvelle-taxe-holding-sur-les-actifs-somptuaires-detenus-par-les-societes,56172.html)
- Additional detail for scene 7: the Sénat had voted the Zucman bill down 188 to 129 on 12 June 2025 [S] — [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/economie/taxe-zucman-le-senat-rejette-la-creation-dun-nouvel-impot-sur-les-ultra-riches)

### Inferences
- Scenes 1–4 and 6 are the strongest bridge to a land-tax history, because each turns on official real-estate valuations.

### Gaps
- None.
