# STATE — landtax (learning thread)

**Project:** a history of land taxation written to teach it, organised by era, with valuation as the
through-line: what was measured, who measured it, how often, and what happened when values aged, froze or
were challenged. Property taxes, public leasehold and wealth taxes are woven in where they fit. On
2026-11-04 Stella asked to be taught the history first; a blog post ("a timeline of sorts", with a short
disclaimer at the top) may follow. Not a paper.
**State as of:** 2026-11-04.

## Where things stand

- **Research:** ten passes done. Notes are in `research_notes/Land taxation and valuation timeline/` (files 01
  to 10, about 137,000 words). Each file has a dated timeline, Takeaway / Cited Findings / Inferences / Gaps for
  each question, a glossary, scenes and an Access log. Claims are labelled primary, secondary or unresolved.
  - 01 Britain · 02 Australia and New Zealand · 03 Denmark, Estonia and the Nordics · 04 Germany and Austria ·
    05 US and Canada · 06 Japan, South Korea, Taiwan · 07 public land ownership (Hong Kong, Singapore, China,
    Canberra, Amsterdam) · 08 pre-modern and the Global South (Jamaica, Kenya, South Africa) · 09 valuation
    methods and the age of European valuations · 10 wealth taxes
- **Report:** `reports/Land taxation and valuation timeline.md`, titled "Land taxes rise, freeze and return"
  (about 23,000 words). Overview; seven eras; how land is taxed today; a 104-event timeline; a design-choices
  map of 35 cases in place of a conclusion; a table of valuation ages for 30 jurisdictions; glossary; open
  questions.
- **Her decisions so far:** blog, not a pitch to Works in Progress; sections by era, not geography; scope
  beyond the English-speaking world; wealth taxes woven in where appropriate, possibly cut later; no verdict.
  Not yet decided: the ending, interviews, charts. The Watling–Doucet exchange is not referenced in any piece.

## Known gaps, and why

- The shared web-search budget (200 per turn across all agents) ran out in every pass, and many official sites
  returned 403 or 503 to the fetch tool. The environment's network policy blocked two hosts: `mail.nzlii.org`
  and `dynamic-rsnz.natlib.govt.nz`. No gap was filled from a weaker source. Every gap is listed in the notes'
  Access logs and in the report's last section.
- Largest open items:
  - which Pennsylvania municipalities still levy split rates;
  - the Korean Constitutional Court decisions (known only from newspaper reports);
  - the text of Kenya's National Rating Act 2024;
  - early Australian state land-tax dates and when the exemptions began;
  - why New Zealand abolished its land tax in 1990;
  - certified results of the 3 November 2026 votes (Florida Amendment 3, California Prop 40);
  - Huang's and Habib's histories, not reached;
  - the Uthwatt Report, and the exact start and end dates of the 1947 and 1976 British taxes.
- Some downloaded source texts sit in the session scratchpad (`val09/docs/`, `nordic_r/`), which is not kept.
  The notes cite the URLs, so they can be fetched again.
