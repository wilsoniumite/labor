Model B against the US record, 1950–2024

Model B is the paper's pinning model fitted to the US record with the setup chosen in the explorer on 6 November. The goods wage is read over everything but shelter. Women enter work until 1990. The required return is set at 2%. The basket substitutes at constant utility, and that utility dips between 1970 and 1990. There is no cap on participation. The report checks B four ways: against the five series it was fitted to, against facts it never saw, by opening up each year's equilibrium, and by moving what was set or chosen.

What the report finds

  1. B fits all five series with eight fitted numbers, and every one of thirteen starts lands on the same fit (misfit 2.81). Its largest misses are the wage over shelter after 2010, about a fifth below the record by 2024, and θV before 1985.
  2. Every piece of the setup earns its place under the spec's rule: removing it must raise the misfit by at least 20%. Women's entry is worth 133%, the basket 197%, the utility's rise to 1970 58% and its dip 29%. The cap adds nothing (1.5%) and stays out. Pinning 1950 costs nothing. Setting the return at 2% costs 2.5%. The one cut that removes a fitted number, tying the machine chain's hours to the clock, costs 71%. B is at its simplest.
  3. A post-war technology wave from 1948 cuts the misfit to 2.00 for two more numbers and halves the clock's pace to 6.8% a year. It passes the same rule.
  4. Against facts it never saw, B's machine chain is too large. The chain takes 38% of all pay in 2023, against 18% measured. Replacement is 35% of gross output, against 16%. Machine services make up half of a machine's cost (50%), more than depreciation and all of the net operating surplus together (38%). The waterline rises 1.4-fold from 1999 to 2023, against 7.6-fold measured.
  5. B's land is too quiet. The coverage ratio rises 6.7-fold in the record and falls by a fifth in the model. Housing's share of spending rises by 44%; the model's site share by 9%.
  6. Inside, from about 1970 the task line sits on the machine chain's ceiling, and land's share of a machine's cost falls from 0.40 to 0.0003. From then on the fast clock (land per unit of task capacity, down 12.5% a year) no longer moves the task line. The slow one does (hours per unit of task capacity, down 0.9% a year).
  7. B describes the record but does not forecast it. Fitted to 1950–1995 alone it fits those years closely (1.27) and misses 1996–2024 by 22.6. Every series is load-bearing: left out of the fit, none is predicted within three tolerances.
  8. Most set values barely matter. The return at 1% or 3%, the utility's knots five years either way, and women's entry ending in 1980 or 2000 all leave the misfit within 7%. The basket's elasticity matters most: Cobb–Douglas fits 9% better, and the elasticity read with B's own goods price fits 25% worse.
  9. Carried forward with its drivers, B's labour share falls from 0.45 in 2024 to 0.38 by 2050 and the task line rises from 0.874 to 0.905. With the clocks stopped both hold. No year to 2050 loses its equilibrium.

The model as set

The paper's pinning model (pinning/code/pinning_model.py), solved in each year from 1950 to 2024 with that year's adults and depreciation. Eight numbers are fitted to the record. Everything else is set before the fit, from data or by choice.

The fit

The misfit counts each series' deviations in tolerances: 5% for the two wages, the band's half-width for labour's share (0.01 where the record has no band), 0.015 for prime-age employment and 0.02 for θV. A series' misfit is its mean squared deviation. The total is the mean over the five, so 1 means the model sits one tolerance from the record on average.

B's largest misses are the wage over shelter after 2010 (83 in 2024 against the record's 105, 1950 = 100) and θV before 1985 (0.43 in 1967 against 0.50). The 1948 wave closes most of the early θV gap and half of the shelter gap, and gives up a little on the goods wage and employment.

Facts the fit never saw

None of these entered the misfit. Where the model has a counterpart, it is drawn against the record. Several come from the machine-chain measurements of paths/results/chains.json (BEA input–output tables, 1997–2023), which calibration (c) reads in its target year.

Carried forward

The fitted drivers continued from 2025 to 2050. The clocks keep their paces. Adults grow as in 2014–2024 (0.8% a year), depreciation stays at its 2015–2024 mean and the cost of working at its 2024 level. The basket's utility is either held at its 2024 value, which is B's own rule after its last knot, or rises as it did over 1990–2024 (0.6% a year). A third run stops both clocks in 2025.

Labour's share falls from 0.45 to 0.38 by 2050 and the task line rises from 0.874 to 0.905, whichever way the utility goes. A rising utility takes the prime-age rate down to 0.74. With the clocks stopped, labour's share and the task line hold. Because the task line sits on the ceiling, automation advances at the slow clock's pace, and no year to 2050 loses its equilibrium.

Stress tests

Each test refits B from its best fit. A set value that moves far is stepped there, refitting at each step (code/stress_B.py; 51 fits).

Out of sample

Fitted to 1950–1995 alone, B fits those years closely (misfit 1.27) and misses 1996–2024 by 22.6, mostly on the wage over shelter and labour's share. Fitted to 1950–1985 it misses 1986–2024 by 11.6. With the utility held after 1990, so that nothing after 1990 enters but adults and depreciation, the miss is 31.9. The shorter records fit the clock at 15.5–17% a year.

Each series left out

Each series dropped from the misfit and B refitted on the other four. None is predicted within three tolerances on average. Each series carries information the others do not.

Set values moved

The return, the utility's knots, the end of women's entry and the schedule's floor barely move the misfit. The floor g0 moves the task line's level (0.91 or 0.83 in 2024), so the record does not pin that level. The basket's elasticity matters most. At 1, Cobb–Douglas, the misfit falls to 2.54. At 0.64 it rises to 3.51; that is what the share regression gives when shelter's price is read against everything but shelter, as B reads goods.

How sharply the record pins the clock

The clock's pace held at each value and the rest refitted. The best is 12.5% a year, and anything from 10% to 16% stays within 10% of the best misfit.

Simpler?

The spec's rule (section 6): a piece stays if removing it raises the misfit by at least 20%, or if without it a targeted series leaves its band. Each piece of B taken out in turn, and two simplifications tried:

Every piece stays and the cap stays out. Of the cuts tried, none that removes a fitted number keeps the fit: on this rule B is at its simplest. Taking the basket's utility through the real wage in every year instead of at four knots fits as well (2.88) with no knot years to choose, but it leans harder on the wage data.

Where this leaves B

B fits the five series with eight numbers, and the fit holds when the set values move. The trouble is in what the fit implies. To match the wages and labour's share, the fit drives the economy onto the machine chain's ceiling, where land leaves a machine's cost. The chain then takes about twice its measured share of pay and of output. The waterline, which should read the clock independently of the targets, moves a fifth as far as measured. The record's land, meanwhile, grows in value far faster than anything in B.

What to try next, in order:

  1. Make the machine chain's size a target: its share of all pay, 1997–2023, from the input–output tables. Or set land's share of a machine's cost as calibration (c) does. Either is the direct test of whether the ceiling regime is the record's or a corner the fit found.
  2. A second good, durables apart from other goods. The durables wage rises 6.2-fold against 2.0-fold for everything but shelter, and one good cannot carry both.
  3. Read the basket's elasticity and the goods wage on one price basis. B pairs an elasticity read against durables (0.875) with a goods wage read over everything but shelter (0.64 on that basis).
  4. Keep the 1948 wave as the leading candidate addition. It improves three series, halves the clock, and passes the 20% rule.
