# Long-run protocol: review of the first draft (2026-10-08)

Three adversarial reviewers read the first draft of `longrun/PROTOCOL.md` (theory fidelity, design loopholes,
data feasibility from metadata only). The revision was paused before it ran: these findings are still to be
worked into the protocol.

## Reviewer 1 (theory, design or data lens)

- **[major]** (longrun/PROTOCOL.md s.2 'What the distinctive test is distinctive against'; s.5 LR-K2 'No-coresidence account: DiD >= 0'; s.5 Verdicts 'favours the floor mechanism against the no-coresidence account') DiD on raw slopes is not scale-free, so DiD < 0 does not separate the floor from a no-coresidence account in which high-F countries simply respond less. Take any account where the high-F young's response is a proportional attenuation lambda < 1 of a convex response: the symmetric family-insurance model the critic names in derivations headline (3), or a lower participation density at the margin where the 25-34 rate sits at a different level. That gives DiD = (lambda - 1)(gamma3 - gamma1) < 0 with gamma3^H > 0. The no-coresidence account therefore predicts DiD of about 0 only under equal scaling, not 'DiD >= 0' as the protocol writes. The derivations themselves say the discriminating quantity is the sign of the high-F slope at high q, not the interaction.
  - *Fix:* Name the attenuation and insurance foil in s.2 beside Kaplan and Ermisch. Change the foil line to 'DiD of about 0 under equal scaling; any proportional attenuation at high F also gives DiD < 0'. Add a scale-free companion with no verdict (gamma3^H/gamma1^H against gamma3/gamma1, or DiD after dividing each group's slopes by its bottom-tercile slope). Make the model-level reading conditional: a supported DiD reads 'favours the floor against the no-coresidence account' only if gamma3^H is not significantly positive. Otherwise it reads 'consistent with the floor or with attenuation'.
- **[major]** (s.2 bullet 2 ('should fall ... to zero or below'); s.5 LR-K2 'Reported with no verdict: ... The model says <= 0' and 'is the critic's full refutation condition') The model gives slope_top <= 0 only for a population made up entirely of floor-types. A high-F country mixes floor-types (share phi) with no-floor types. In regime (iii) its aggregate slope is phi*0 + (1-phi)*slope_N > 0 when work can be done from home. The critic's own floor_regimes_v2.py output shows this: slope_F = 0 and slope_N = +2.5 at q = 0.44-0.47. A top-half F_L country with, say, 30-60% of 25-34s at home is predicted a smaller but POSITIVE gamma3^H. The paper's linear reduced form gives a negative floor slope (-h_w) only if work needs one's own flat. So 'the model says <= 0' overstates the paper, and a significantly positive gamma3^H on its own is not a refutation.
  - *Fix:* Restate the prediction as follows: gamma3^H falls relative to gamma1^H and lies below the low-F top-tercile slope. It is <= 0 only if floor-types dominate F_L or jobs require one's own dwelling. Keep gamma3^H as a reported quantity, without the sentence that it 'says <= 0'. Quote the critic's refutation as the conjunction it is: positive, no smaller than at low F, and no drop.
- **[major]** (s.2 'The regime claim, written down now'; s.5 Verdicts 'against the floor mechanism: LR-K2 contradicted'; joint reading table) A contradicted DiD (DiD > 0) is the model's own prediction in several cases. (a) The high-F countries' top tercile is still in regime (ii): there the floor-types' slope h/(s0-qh) is steeper and rising, so b2 > 0. (b) The low-F countries' top tercile is in regime (iv): no-floor types at a state floor have slope -h/(w-qh) < 0. (c) Parents rent, so that 'the host's capacity to share inherits the host's rent bill' (paper l.383). The protocol pre-commits against (a) and states (c) as unmeasured. It never writes the low-F half of the regime claim, and it never states the LR-K1 assumption that the no-floor types' floor is not rent-indexed (no out-of-work housing allowance). Derivations (4g) flags exactly this for DE, NL and FR, which are low-F members here. The high-F group in practice is CL, CO, CR, MX and IL, with short and late OECD RPI histories. Nothing in derivations.md places their top tercile past q_enc^F. The critic's rationale, that q crosses q_enc inside a 75-year history, was argued for the South and JP, which drop out. Reading 'contradicted' as 'against the floor mechanism' without naming these auxiliaries overstates what the data can refute.
  - *Fix:* Write the two-sided regime claim in s.2. (i) Each high-F country's top q tercile lies past q_enc^F. (ii) In each low-F country, the no-floor young are not at a floor (state or tolerated) in any tercile, and no rent-indexed out-of-work housing allowance applies. (iii) The parents' generation owns outright. Name the countries the claim covers as of L2. Phrase 'contradicted' as 'against the floor mechanism together with its stated regime placement and auxiliaries (i)-(iii)', still with no post-look reinterpretation allowed. Disclose in s.2 that the critic's 75-year-crossing rationale does not carry to the short Latin American and Israeli histories.
- **[major]** (s.5 'The piecewise basis' (terciles and knots from x_ct across all eligible years) vs L6/L7 ('a splice is a fixed effect', no ratio-linking)) Terciles rank log q levels across q segments. Under L7, levels across a source switch, documented redesign or JST rescaling are deliberately unlinked: each segment's index base is arbitrary. So whether a year falls in the 'high-q part of the country's history' depends on unlinked bases, not on q. The segment dummies absorb only additive shifts in A. With the nonlinear P1-P3 basis, a level shift in x moves observations across knots and changes the slopes, and no dummy undoes that. D2-L recognises the problem (m_cs within country x q-segment); LR-K2 does not. Cases: US at the 2021 rung switch, JP's CPI flow switch, the JST R6 2016-2020 extension and rescaled stretches, and every CPI break.
  - *Fix:* Compute terciles and knots within a single q segment per country: the longest q segment in the country's unseen window, chosen from availability metadata before outcomes are fetched. Drop LR-K2 observations whose lagged q lies in other segments, or give each q segment its own terciles and knots (state which). Apply the same rule in LR-Y0 and the U-K2 study.
- **[major]** (s.5 LR-K2 primary; L12 / robustness row 'strict rent control dropped') LR-RC: under binding control CPI-q prices sitting tenants, and the young's response is about 0. Derivations' LR-K2 assumption is that q tracks the market rent 'in the years used', and (4e) says to restrict to decontrolled years or code the control regime before outcomes. In this sample the long-history countries (US, CA, DE, NL, FR) are low-F. Their bottom terciles fall in the control era (US before the early 1950s, DE 1960-71, FR and NL long-running), where the predicted slope is about 0. Their top terciles fall in market years, where it is positive. The low-F top-minus-bottom difference is then positive by measurement, not by convexity. The short-history high-F group has no control era. DiD < 0 can arise mechanically, in the predicted direction. The protocol treats this only as a no-verdict robustness run.
  - *Fix:* Add a mechanical qualifier 'rests on control-era years', fired when dropping the Kholodilin strict-control country-years flips DiD's sign or pushes its own-direction p above 0.10. Alternatively, make the decontrolled-years sample the primary. Note that Kholodilin's second-generation controls also attenuate CPI-q, and say why only first-generation years are dropped.
- **[major]** (s.5 LR-K2 'Mechanical qualifiers' (H x decade) vs derivations LR-K2 Assumptions and (4f); s.10 departures) The critic makes 'no F-specific trends in schooling, conscription or women's entry, or F x decade effects included' a condition of the LR-K2 sign. The protocol demotes F x decade effects to a qualifier and does not list this as a departure in s.10. With the primary pooling sexes and within-country terciles close to time periods (q trends up), the women's-entry trend loads onto the tercile slopes. That trend is later in the Latin American high-F group, and the bias runs against the model in the high-F top tercile.
  - *Fix:* Either put H x decade effects in the primary, as the critic specifies, or list the demotion in s.10 with its reason (identification when terciles track time) and keep the qualifier. In s.2, state that the primary sign is conditional on no floor-group-specific trends.
- **[minor]** (s.5 'The piecewise basis' (eligible years need A2534, U_ct and x_ct); L14 order) Her decision fixes the terciles 'computed from q alone'. The ranking set here is years where A2534 and U_ct are also available. That is outcome metadata, and L3's OBS_STATUS E/F/M exclusions on the outcome arrive only with the outcome values. So the set is either not q-only or not knowable before outcomes are fetched. This is not listed as a departure.
  - *Fix:* Define the ranking set as every year with x_ct available (L3 flags of q applied) inside the country's unseen observation window, within the chosen q segment. State that outcome-missing years later drop from the regression without moving the knots.
- **[minor]** (s.2 'What the distinctive test is distinctive against' (Kaplan 2012; Ermisch)) The required statement is present and accurate in substance: LR-K2 does not discriminate against life-cycle models with a below-market parental room. The wording 'the same sign path, shifted in location' overstates the critic's evidence. In floor_regimes_v2.py the coresidence ('std') model has no b2 > 0 regime (ii), and its floor-type slope is negative (-0.47 to -0.06) where the paper's is positive. What coincides is the DiD sign. The statement also does not repeat that D1 and D2 are equally non-distinctive against these models.
  - *Fix:* Write 'produce the same DiD sign (a fall in the high-F slope as q rises), with the turning point at a different q', and add one clause that nothing in D1-L or D2-L discriminates against them either.
- **[minor]** (s.5 LR-C1 'Reading'; L9/L10 (which censuses are LR-C1 outcomes)) (a) The critic's refutation is conditional on 'where parents own outright'. The protocol's reading drops the condition while tenure is deliberately unmeasured, so a null cannot carry the stated refutation. (b) L9 calls later 1955-1975 samples LR-C1 outcomes, but L10 builds Extract B from samples after 1975. The critic says coresidence should FALL in 1950-75 where q was low or controlled and marriage early. Pre-1976 census points would put a regime of opposite sign into a linear b > 0 test.
  - *Fix:* Restore the condition in the reading ('with clean q, and assuming outright-owning parents, which is unmeasured'). Restrict LR-C1 outcomes to censuses of 1976 and later, and remove the pre-1976 sentence from L9.
- **[minor]** (s.5 LR-Y0 and the reading table) In long-history countries the bottom q tercile is largely the rent-control era, where LR-RC predicts about 0, and young men near saturation are also predicted at about 0. An inconclusive LR-Y0 with a small MDE would be read 'no support' even though the model predicts a null there.
  - *Fix:* Add a reading note for LR-Y0: inconclusive reads 'no support' only on the decontrolled-years robustness. Otherwise it is 'consistent with LR-RC attenuation'.
- **[minor]** (L4 outcome; s.10 departures (derivations (7), LR-W1; design review's agricultural-share split)) Activity rates count unpaid family workers as active. That work is the paper's land-access exit recorded as participation, and it is concentrated in exactly the high-F Latin American group. The critic's secondary outcome (employee-to-population ratio) and the design review's no-verdict agricultural-share split were dropped, and neither drop is listed as a departure.
  - *Fix:* List the drop in s.10 with its reason (no single-source employee series by five-year age). Add a sentence to s.2 that in agrarian or informal settings the outcome partly measures exit.
- **[minor]** (L9 (F_L from the earliest 1955-1975 census); s.2) For AT, CA, CH, DE, IE and GR the earliest sample is 1970-71, inside the panel's observation years, so F_L is not predetermined for earlier years. Under LR-C1 and LR-F1, 1960s-70s coresidence is itself a response to the early q path and to marriage timing, which is the endogeneity the critic raised against 2003-08 F. The binary split may also align with history length (long North Atlantic histories against short Latin American ones).
  - *Fix:* State this in s.2 as a disclosed limit of the binding floor measure. Report, with no verdict, LR-K2 restricted to observation years after each country's F_L census year.
- **[minor]** (s.5 LR-K2 primary (A2534, sexes equal-weighted) vs LR-F1) The equal-weighted primary is her binding decision. Even so, the critic says parental F is the wrong moderator for young women before about 1975 (their floor was the marital household), so the primary includes early cells where the model does not sign the women's half. Section 2 says this only in a robustness footnote.
  - *Fix:* Add one sentence to s.2 stating that the primary pools early women for whom the parental floor is not the operative floor (LR-F1), and that the men-only run (no verdict) is the theory-clean version.

## Reviewer 2 (theory, design or data lens)

- **[blocker]** (longrun/PROTOCOL.md s5 'The piecewise basis' + L6/L7 (terciles and knots ranked across q segments)) Terciles and knots k1_c, k2_c come from ranking x = log q_{t-1} over all of a country's eligible years, across q segments. Segment dummies absorb a level shift in a linear term. They do not absorb it in the kinked basis P1-P3, so 'a splice is a fixed effect' fails exactly where LR-K2 and LR-Y0 need it. Concrete cases: the US, AU and JP switch from JST to RPI/CPI (base 2015) for q years from 2021, which gives obs 2022-2025. JPN also switches CPI flows. JST R6 has its 2016-2020 extension method, and DE has its 1991 territory seam. In each case the arbitrary relative index base decides whether the post-seam years fall in the top or the bottom tercile, and where the knots sit. That is ratio-linking by accident, and it hits the longest, partly seen series (the US).
  - *Fix:* Write the rule now: terciles and knots are computed within country × q-segment. Alternatively, LR-K2 and LR-Y0 use only each country's longest q segment, and other segments are dropped from these two tests (testability of at least 9 eligible years counted per segment). Under either rule, no tercile spans a seam. Add a check that P1-P3 are invariant to rescaling q within any segment.
- **[blocker]** (s5 LR-K2 (DiD) and its qualifiers; s10 ('series-permutation placebo kept for D2-L')) q drifts close to monotonically in time, so within-country q terciles are close to calendar-time terciles. LR-K2's DiD then compares the curvature of participation over time between floor groups that are observed in different windows. Low F: US from 1960, CA from 1976, and DE, FR and NL in 1970/75/87-1996. High F: in practice CL, CO, CR and MX, from 1991/1996/2001 to 2025. Known S-curves (women's entry, accelerating early in the low-F windows and saturating late in the LatAm windows) and the expansion of schooling can produce DiD < 0, the supported direction, with no floor at all. This is D2's quadratic-trend flaw reborn. The design review's guard against it (the series-permutation placebo and the 'indistinguishable from trend curvature' qualifier) was moved to D2-L, which now carries no verdict, so the one verdict test has no such guard. H × decade FE do not absorb curvature within a decade.
  - *Fix:* Add pre-registered mechanical qualifiers to LR-K2. (a) 'indistinguishable from time curvature': the same regression with terciles of calendar year (eligible years ranked by year) in place of q terciles, plus the series-permutation placebo (each country gets a coverage-matched other country's q path). The qualifier fires if the observed t lies inside the placebo's central 80%, or if the time-tercile DiD is at least as large in the predicted direction. (b) 'rests on floor-group trends' uses H × year FE (identified wherever both groups are observed) or country-specific quadratic trends, not H × decade. Report the men-only run beside the primary, since LR-F1 says women's floor differs before 1975.
- **[major]** (L7 last sentence ('A break found after the freeze counts as plumbing only if its documentation predates the look')) Almost all historical documentation predates the look. Between the L-freeze and the LE-freeze, analysts see explore-half values (and the US deep study), so they can look for documentation of breaks wherever the data jump and add segments as 'plumbing'. segments.csv therefore stays open after values are seen, which is the forking path the blind compilation was meant to close. Breaks in confirm-half countries can be added the same way, before the look, with the explore half's lessons in hand.
  - *Fix:* segments.csv is frozen at the L-freeze for the primary. A break found later enters only a pre-specified no-verdict run, 'with post-freeze segments'. If it must enter the primary, it counts only if a fresh blind agent, given no values, finds it in a documentation re-read covering every country (both halves) before the LE-freeze. That agent's complete output is applied, not a selection from it, and every such addition is logged as a deviation.
- **[major]** (s8 Bug policy ('that P-L test's primary falls back to the confirm half')) For LR-K2, the confirm half holds about 5 countries, below the testability floor of 8. Declaring a deviation after seeing the explore half's LR-K2 leaning against the model therefore turns the model-level test into 'not testable' or 'uninformative'. The fallback rule becomes an escape hatch from a looming contradiction.
  - *Fix:* For LR-K2 (and any test whose confirm half would fail testability), the frozen specification stays the primary whatever deviation occurs. The deviated version is reported as exploratory beside it. The fallback to the confirm half applies only where the confirm half itself passes the frozen testability rule.
- **[major]** (s5 LR-K2 'No-coresidence account: DiD ≥ 0' and the verdict on DiD alone) The no-coresidence model predicts a convex, rising response in each group (slope_top − slope_bottom > 0 within both). It does not fix the sign of the difference between groups whose q-to-income positions differ: LatAm against North America and the Continent. A DiD < 0 can therefore come entirely from stronger convexity in the low-F group, with γ3^H − γ1^H > 0 and slope_top(high F) significantly positive. Under the current text that would read 'favours the floor mechanism' although the model's own high-F prediction (slope falls to ≤ 0) failed. The rival signs the within-high-F change (γ3^H − γ1^H > 0) unambiguously, and that is the discriminating quantity in the critic's headline (3).
  - *Fix:* Correct the foil statement to say the rival's DiD is unsigned across groups but its within-group top-minus-bottom difference is > 0. Keep DiD as the tested quantity (her decision). Add a mechanical qualifier: 'carried by low-floor convexity' when γ3^H − γ1^H ≥ 0, or when slope_top(high F) has one-sided p < 0.05 for > 0. A supported LR-K2 carrying that qualifier is never reported as 'favours the floor mechanism' without it.
- **[major]** (s5 Size correction, the reading table and the European benchmarks) The reading table can be steered. (1) The skeleton simulation needs 'synthetic q paths for the terciles', but their DGP is not fixed, and the within-tercile variance of q is the main driver of the MDE. (2) Taking 'the larger of ρ = 0.5 and 0.9' inflates the long-run MDE and pushes an inconclusive result toward 'uninformative', the reading her stated prior prefers. (3) The long-run MDEs are simulated, while the benchmarks are inference.mde(se) from real European regressions. The benchmarks are also in raw coefficient units on differently scaled regressors and outcomes: rent over all items against rent over goods (C-1 vs LR-C1), and the ILOSTAT attendance share against edat_lfse_19 (C-6 vs C-6L). The 'at or below the benchmark' comparison is not like for like.
  - *Fix:* Fix the synthetic-q DGP now, for example coverage-matched draws of the seen European HICP q paths, or a random walk with drift and SD fixed in the text. Compute every MDE, the European benchmarks included, by the same skeleton procedure with the same ρ (either a single stated value or the ρ estimated from the European look's residuals). Express the comparison in a scale-free unit: the MDE per within-segment SD of the residualised tested regressor, in outcome-SD units, or as power against a common standardised effect. Commit the simulation code and its seed with the tag.
- **[major]** (s5 European benchmark for LR-K2/LR-Y0 ('run, before the freeze, on the frozen European panel')) The LR-K2 and LR-Y0 specifications are run on seen, outcome-bearing European 2009-2025 data before the L-freeze. Nothing prevents iterating the tercile rule, the H split, the basis, controls or qualifiers until the European run looks a particular way, and then freezing that specification for the long run. That is a forking path through real data.
  - *Fix:* Commit and hash longrun/code/specs.py (LR-K2, LR-Y0, qualifiers) and this protocol text first. Then run the European benchmark once from that commit and log its hash and output. Any later change to the LR-K2 or LR-Y0 code is a recorded pre-freeze amendment, and both benchmark runs are reported.
- **[major]** (L3/L14 and Testability (eligibility, terciles and H 'from availability metadata')) OBS_STATUS (E/F/M/B) is an observation-level attribute. SDMX availability constraints do not return it, and JST and ILOSTAT have no values-free availability metadata. So eligible years, terciles, testability and the F_L median split can change once values (and flags) arrive, and the protocol does not say what happens then. Could a country drop below 9 years, could H be re-split if a frozen IPUMS sample proves unusable, could knots be recomputed? Each choice would be made after values are seen.
  - *Fix:* State now: (a) the L2 country lists, the eligible-year lists and the knots are frozen, and cells later found flagged become missing in the regression without changing knots or membership; (b) H is computed by the median rule over the frozen L2 list, and a country whose frozen sample proves unusable drops while H is recomputed mechanically over the rest (or H is held, but say which); (c) coverage masks for JST and ILOSTAT come from a values-dropping script (as in the scouts' cov.py), disclosed and committed.
- **[major]** (L9 'as listed on the IPUMS-I sample page on the day of the extract'; L4 timeline) The extract is made after the freeze, and the sample set is whatever IPUMS lists that day. A newly released sample, say an earlier census, would change a country's 'earliest' census, F_L, H and the testability count after the freeze. Which sample defines F_L for each country must be fixed before values exist.
  - *Fix:* Freeze the list of IPUMS-I sample IDs at L2, from the public sample list and codebooks (metadata), with the RELATE detailed-code scheme of each sample recorded. The extract requests exactly those IDs, and samples released later are ignored. A frozen sample that turns out to lack RELATE or weights drops its country by the rule in the previous finding.
- **[major]** (L13 weighting; s3 coverage (US 1960-2025 vs NL 1987-96); s4 forced placement) 'Countries weighted equally within each observation' in an unbalanced annual panel means the US (about 64 years) carries roughly three to six times the weight of NL (10), FR (22) or DE (27). It dominates the low-F slopes and so the DiD. The US is the partly seen country whose q path is the paper's own fork. The LOO qualifier only labels the result; a verdict that is in effect a US-versus-LatAm contrast would still stand. The phrase itself is ambiguous.
  - *Fix:* Define the weighting exactly. Either use country-equal weights (1/n_c per country-year) in the primary, or keep observation weights and publish before the look each country's share of the identifying variation of DiD, taken from the skeleton. Add the rule that if dropping the US moves DiD's own-direction p above 0.10, the model-level verdict reads 'rests on the US, a partly seen country', not just the generic qualifier.
- **[major]** (s1 tier 2 (E-L), s4 'What each half may be used for') E-L is restricted to series in the manifest but not to the unseen cells of L1. An E-L tested on the confirm half could use European confirm countries' 1997-2025 years, which were seen at the European look. The addendum also does not restate that an E-L that varies LR-K2 never changes LR-K2's or the model-level verdict. With three E-L and a model-level verdict resting on one test, an E-L variant of LR-K2 (men only, H × year FE) supported on about 5 confirm countries invites a rescue reading.
  - *Fix:* State that every E-L sample is restricted to the L1/L2 unseen cells. Restate the European rule: an E-L that varies a P-L test is labelled as such, never alters that test's or the model-level verdict, and appears only in the E-L table.
- **[minor]** (L9 F_L predeterminedness and comparability) F_L comes from censuses between 1960 (US, NL, MX, CL) and 1972 (IL), with CA 1971 and DE 1970. Coresidence was trending in the 1960s, so the high/low split partly reflects census year, which correlates with region. F_L is not predetermined for the DE 1970 and US 1960 observation years. The West German 1970 sample moderates unified Germany's 1991-96 observations, against the 'must cover the panel's territory' rule. In the frozen list, most countries also have a 1968-1973 sample.
  - *Fix:* Restrict F tests to observation years after the F_L census year, or report it. Restrict DE to observation years up to 1990 in F tests, or explicitly allow the territorial mismatch. Make 'F_L from the sample nearest 1970' one of the no-verdict robustness runs, replacing one run to respect the cap of eight.
- **[minor]** (L9 RELATE definition) 'All detailed codes under general code 3' takes in combined codes that some samples use (child/child-in-law, child/child-in-law/grandchild). These inflate F_L in exactly the extended-household samples (likely LatAm) that set H. Child of the head also misses young adults who head a household with a parent present, and married children living with parents-in-law. The measurement heterogeneity is correlated with the floor split.
  - *Fix:* At L2, from the codebooks, list the detailed codes each frozen sample uses and fix the rule for the combined codes, for example excluding them, or counting them with the MOMLOC/POPLOC run as the check. Report the share of 25-34s in combined codes per sample.
- **[minor]** (s5 LR-K2 qualifiers, LOO and RI) Several qualifier mechanics are unspecified and could be settled after values. Which p (WCR, RI or their max) triggers 'p above 0.10'? Are H × decade cells with no high-F observations (all of 1960-1986) dropped? In LOO and the qualifier runs, is H held fixed or recomputed by the median rule? Leave-one-stratum-out is undefined if H is in effect the Latin America stratum. Exact enumeration in the new RI is checked against inference.ri, which falls back to random draws for 10 units in one stratum (10! > 10000), so the check is only approximate.
  - *Fix:* Specify: the qualifiers use max(p_WCR, p_RI); collinear H × time cells are dropped by rule; H is held fixed in LOO; and leave-one-stratum-out reports 'not estimable' when a group falls below 2 countries. State the check tolerance for the RI comparison.
- **[minor]** (s5 Size correction) Several points are unclear. Does the size check apply to max(p_WCR, p_RI) or to each p separately? Does it cover the opposite direction, which decides 'contradicted'? How does a corrected threshold combine with Holm? Sizes between 0.05 and 0.075 go uncorrected.
  - *Fix:* Simulate the size of the verdict statistic actually used (max p), in both directions. Apply the corrected threshold in both directions. State that Holm uses the corrected α for the family (with K having size 1, the corrected α itself).
- **[minor]** (L14 / s9 L5 (vintages at the look) and s1 time holdout) Three points are open. At the look, the confirm run refetches all countries, but the protocol does not say whether explore countries' terciles come from the explore vintage (hashed) or the new one; the new one would be a recomputation after outcomes were seen. The 2026-2028 time-holdout rerun would re-rank q with new years and move the knots of countries whose outcomes are all seen. Finally, the JST and IPUMS files hold confirm-country q, unemp and wage values once fetched or extracted.
  - *Fix:* State that explore countries' knots are the hashed explore-opening knots, and that only confirm countries' knots are computed at the look. For the time holdout, knots are frozen at the look and new years take the frozen basis. Require a row-level guard on the JST and IPUMS readers.
- **[minor]** (L12 cohort-size control (UN WPP) vs L3) L3 excludes modelled series (UN modelled series named), but the cohort-size qualifier's control comes from UN WPP estimates, which are modelled, interpolated and back-projected. That contradiction leaves room to argue after the look that the 'rests on cohort size' qualifier should not count.
  - *Fix:* State explicitly that WPP is admitted as an exception for the design covariate and the cohort-size control only, with the reason, so the qualifier cannot be disowned afterwards.
- **[minor]** (L9/L10 LR-C1 observations) L9 calls a country's later samples in 1955-1975 LR-C1 outcome values, while L10's Extract B covers only post-1975 censuses. It is unclear whether the in-window samples, and the F_L sample itself, enter LR-C1. That changes LR-C1's sample and its testability count.
  - *Fix:* State which censuses enter LR-C1: for example, every frozen sample in the unseen cells with x available, the F_L sample included or excluded. Make Extract A plus B match that list.
