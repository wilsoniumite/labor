# Exploration: aging retirement

Explore half only (16 countries), 2026-10-08, after the P freeze. Written from the explorer's structured return
(the harness did not let the explorer write this file). Exploratory: every estimate here was chosen along a path
of many tries (52 specifications by this explorer).

**Scope as the explorer stated it.** Aging and retirement (explore half: BE BG CH EE FI FR HR HU IE IT LU LV MT PL PT SE; at most 16 clusters). The tool refused the write to findings.md (subagents may not write report files), so the findings are only in this output and in results/explore_aging_retirement.json. Scripts s00-s14 in explore/aging_retirement run in order. s03_panel.py builds panel.pkl, which the later scripts read; I deleted that file afterwards, so rerun s03 before the others.

## Findings

### Activity at older ages rose strongly in every explore country, mostly after 2009. At 60-64 the rise was about as large for men as for women. (solid)

**Evidence.** Unweighted means over the countries with data in every year shown (lfsa_argan, nationals). Women 60-64: 26.0 (2005), 29.3 (2009), 46.6 (2019), 57.5 (2025), n=12. Men 60-64: 37.9, 41.0, 58.0, 68.1. Women 55-59: 52.7 to 79.7 (n=13). Men 55-59: 67.1 to 84.8. Women 65-69: 10.0 to 20.6 (n=10). Men 65-69: 16.1 to 25.9 (n=12). Largest rises at 60-64: HU men 21.2 (2005) to 75.6 (2025), HU women 9.8 to 46.5, BE women 9.8 to 44.5, LV women 33.8 to 76.6, FI women 32.2 to 74.7. Exceptions: PL women 60-64 only 13.6 to 27.9 (PL men 26.2 to 65.1); LU men 60-64 fell from 22.1 (2009) to 13.7 (2019); PT men 65-69 fell from 35.5 (2005) to 24.9 (2015). Figure fig1_older_activity.png; script s02_levels.py.

**Model reading.** Consistent with a fall in the exit payment m_e at 55-64: higher pension ages and closed early-exit routes, so Delta = m_w - m_e rises toward zero. This is the standard Gruber-Wise reading and does not set the model apart.

### The rise is convergence: older activity rose most where it started lowest. (solid)

**Evidence.** Across countries, the 2009-19 change in A5564 is strongly negative in its 2009 level: correlation -0.73 for 2009-19 and -0.83 for 2009-24. With stratum fixed effects the slope is -0.33 points per point of the 2009 level (se 0.086, one-sided WCR p=0.0055, n=15; LU has no 2019 value, G*=8.0). The largest leave-one-out p is 0.050 (dropping PL). Laggards in 2009 (HU 32.9, MT 31.1, PL 33.2, IT 36.5, BE 36.6, FR 39.9) gained 17-28 points; leaders (SE 74.7, CH 71.0) gained 6-7. Files: fig2_rise_xsec.png, s07_rise_xsec.py, s13_candidates.py.

**Model reading.** The exit routes that reforms closed were widest where exit was most common. This fits a model of pension reform as a change in m_e, but it is equally the standard account, and part of it may be bounded-rate dynamics.

### Retirement is not a category in lfsa_igar (current vintage), whose reasons cover only inactive people who want work. The closest measure of retirement-type exit is lfsa_igaww NSEEK_NWANT (inactive, not seeking and not wanting work), which fell sharply at 55-64. The 2012 ad hoc module splits the countries into those where people left work at pension eligibility and those where they left for health. (suggestive)

**Evidence.** lfsa_igar reasons at 55-64 sum to 1-8% of the population, against 17-40% inactive. NSEEK_NWANT as % of the 55-64 population, 2006 to 2024: HU 61.4 to 24.8, IT 62.3 to 33.3, PL 64.1 to 36.8, FR 57.4 to 33.1, SE 25.1 to 13.2. BE jumps in 2024 because of a classification change. lfso_12reasnot (ages 50-69, 2012), share who left at pension eligibility: MT 87%, BG 80%, HU 66%, PL 58%, LU 50%, FR 49%, IT 41%. Own health as large or larger than eligibility: EE 38.5 (vs 12.7), PT 37.0 (vs 13.6), FI 30.6 (vs 34.2), HR 30.7 (vs 36.8), LV 26.5 (vs 21.9). 'Favourable financial arrangements' is highest in IE 16.8, SE 12.8, BE 11.9 and LV 10.2, and 1-4% in HU, PL, IT, FI and FR. Files: s01_igar.py, s12_retirement_reasons.py, inactive_5564_want.csv. None of these series is in the manifest; they are context only.

**Model reading.** Where eligibility drives exit, retirement is exit financed by an exit-conditioned payment (m_e). 'Financial arrangements' is the item closest to the non-labour income A. It is largest in IE, SE and BE, which are not the high outright-ownership countries.

### Within countries, older participation shows no response to rents or house prices in either direction. The net-long sign (owners work less when q rises) appears only weakly, for women 55-59. (suggestive)

**Evidence.** Country and year fixed effects, U, C3 breaks. A5564 on logq_l1: -5.0 (se 5.9). A5564 on log(HPI/CP00)_{t-1}: -0.6 (se 4.1). Across sex x age bands (55-59, 60-64) and the two q measures, all eight estimates lie between -14 and +4.5, with no two-sided p below 0.12. Women 55-59: -7.0 (se 5.7) on q and -4.5 (se 3.3) on HPI. The long window (lfsa_urgan U, 1997-2025) gives A5564 on q of -5.2 (se 5.9). After the fixed effects, IE carries 42% and EE 24% of the residual variance of log q, so G*=3.3. Scripts: s04_housing_old.py, s08_longwindow.py, s09_aging_prices.py.

**Model reading.** No support for the Appendix B net-long income effect at older ages. The derivations' wage pass-through term (pensions fixed in goods while q moves with the goods wage) pushes the other way and could offset it. The data cannot separate the two.

### Where outright ownership at 55-64 is high, older participation responds more positively to house prices. This is the opposite of the net-long prediction, and it is fragile. (weak)

**Evidence.** Outcome: the 55-64 minus 25-54 activity gap. Regressor: log(HPI/CP00)_{t-1} x standardised outright-owner share 55-64 (2015-17); geo and year FE, U, breaks. Estimate +6.2 (se 2.3), one-sided p for + of 0.026, G*=4.1. For women 60-64 alone: +7.5 (se 2.4). Fragility: dropping SE gives +5.9 with two-sided p 0.445; country trends give +3.5 (p 0.15); CEE x year effects give +6.4 (p 0.21). Slopes estimated country by country show no ordering by ownership. Cross-country, ownership correlates +0.45 with the 2009-19 rise, but ownership is high where activity started low, so this is the convergence again. Files: s05_robust_own.py, s10_old_minus_prime.py, s11_own_slopes.py, fig4_own_slopes.png.

**Model reading.** Both the model's Appendix B and the standard hedging account predict a negative interaction (more net long, less work when housing wealth rises). The explore half shows the opposite sign, but it rests on SE and on the convergence of the high-ownership CEE countries.

### Population aging does not go with faster automation-type shifts. Countries that aged faster lost fewer routine jobs, and realised aging there mostly measures migration. (suggestive)

**Evidence.** C-5's own cross-section on the explore half: -0.57 (se 19.9, n=15; HU missing). Realised aging (change in pop 56+/21-55, 2011-19) with stratum FE: +10.7 (se 10.2, one-sided p 0.16, n=16) with HU, +17.4 (p 0.018) without HU. The MDE on 15 clusters is about 27. LU, MT, SE and CH barely aged and lost 4-7 points of ISCO 4+7+8 share; BG, HR, LV and PT aged fastest and lost 0.6-3 points. Panel 2011-25, routine share on the old ratio: +12.5 (se 6.9) without U, +0.8 (se 5.4) with U, -6.2 (se 13.5) with country trends. log q on the old ratio: -0.52 (se 0.30), so 'aging raises q' is not seen. Coresidence 25-34 on the old ratio: +32.9 (se 11.7, p 0.15, G* 2.9). Files: s06_aging_routine.py, s09_aging_prices.py, s14_e2_hu.py, fig3_aging_routine.png.

**Model reading.** Prop. margin(ii) predicts a negative sign (less labour supply, more automation). The explore half shows a zero or positive one, in line with migration-driven structural change, and the design is far too underpowered to see the predicted effect. No E is proposed.

## Data problems

- lfsa_igar (current vintage, 11 reason codes) has no retirement category. Its reasons are asked only of inactive people who want work, so its 55-64 cells sum to 1-8% of the population. Retirement-type exit can only be proxied by lfsa_igaww NSEEK_NWANT, or by the one-year 2012 ad hoc module lfso_12reasnot. Neither is in the manifest.
- Plumbing issue in the frozen pipeline: HU's 2011 demo_pjan reports Y_OPEN as 85+, and single ages 85-99 are missing. build.aging requires every single age up to 99, so C-5's aging_pred is NaN for HU and HU silently drops out of C-5. A confirm country coded the same way would drop too. An open group starting above 56 contains everything the ratio needs, so this is a §7 plumbing fix, and the orchestrator's call.
- The manifest's act frame has no 65-69 or 70-74 bands, so no E can use participation above 64. The 65+ descriptives use the extra argan series only.
- Missing values: LU 60-64 nationals in 2025 (and women in 2009), so LU drops from the 2009-19 convergence cross-section. MT has no 2025 older values. IT and PL have no house-price values in 2009-10. CH has no I15 HPI values (the pipeline uses I25).
- In the explore half, within-country variation in q (rent over non-energy goods) is concentrated in IE (42% of residual variance) and EE (24%). Every q regression on older outcomes has G* of about 3.3.
- lfsa_igaww BE has a classification jump in 2024: the not-wanting share of the inactive goes from 44.7% to 96.8%.
- The une_rt_a window means the frozen U control starts in 2009 for 14 of 16 countries, so within-country regressions with U effectively run 2009-2025 (FR from 2003, SE from 2005).

## Candidate E-hypotheses and their vetting

### E-AR1 — dropped by the vetter (score 3/10)

Activity at 55-64 rose more between 2009 and 2019 in countries where it was lower in 2009 (convergence of older participation).

- Model link: Conditionality appendix: participation depends on Delta = m_w - m_e. Reforms that close exit-conditioned routes (pension eligibility, early retirement) raise Delta most where exit was widest. A standard model (Gruber-Wise, life-cycle) predicts the same, so this is a consistency check, not a distinctive test. Part of the effect may be bounded-rate dynamics.
- Varies: none
- Explore estimate: beta = -0.329, CR1 se = 0.086, t = -3.83, one-sided WCR p = 0.0055 (B=9999), n = 15, G = 15 (LU missing), G* = 8.0. Largest leave-one-out p = 0.050 (drop PL).
- Variants tried: 4
- Vetter's problems: Reproduces exactly (beta -0.329, se 0.086, one-sided WCR p 0.0055, G=15, G*=8.0). The worst leave-one-out p is 0.050 (drop PL), so it passes the protocol's LOO qualifier. The fragility is on the other side: a country that was LEFT OUT. LU is missing only because one 2009 cell (F 60-64 NAT, 11.1) is flagged 'u'. LU is a clear counterexample, low and flat (34.0 in 2011, 35.7 in 2019). Putting LU in at its 2011 level gives beta -0.245, p 0.033. Windows that start in 2011 or 2012 include LU and fail: 2011-19 gives -0.120, p 0.17; 2012-19 gives -0.113, p 0.18. Without LU they give -0.22, p 0.06. So the explore significance partly rests on which small country loses a cell to C4. The confirm half's small countries can gain or lose countries the same way.; Forking paths are understated. '4 variants' counts only the final regressions. s07 printed a 4-outcome by 7-regressor correlation table (28 correlations: own, dhpi, dq, aging_pred and three initial levels) and plotted three candidate regressors. 'Initial level' was then picked as the strongest. The theme as a whole ran 52 specifications plus 63 LOO reruns. Effective variants for this candidate are about 30, not 4. The 2009-2019 window has an a-priori reason (frozen panels start in 2009; pre-COVID), and other windows agree (2006-19 -0.39, 2008-19 -0.35, 2005-15 -0.31, 2009-24 -0.49). So the window itself is not the fork. The regressor choice is.; Mechanical / functional form. The test is 'persistence slope < 1': beta = slope(A19 on A09) - 1, and the no-FE slope is 0.688. With a bounded rate, beta = 0 (equal rises in points everywhere) is not what a neutral world predicts. If every country closes the same proportional share of its inactivity gap, beta is negative automatically. In that form there is no convergence: dlog(100-A) on A09 gives b -0.002, p 0.18. The logit form still converges (-0.28, p 0.009). The 'finding' therefore depends on the scale chosen. Sigma-convergence is also weak in 2014-2019 (sd 12.3 to 12.6).; It is not the retirement margin. Decompose dA5564(09-19) into cohort replacement [A4554(09) - A5564(09)] and within-cohort change [A5564(19) - A4554(09)]. Regressed on A5564(09): cohort replacement -0.634 (p 0.0006); within-cohort exit +0.305 (wrong sign; one-sided p for '-' is 0.97). The whole convergence is cohorts with higher participation (largely women) ageing into 55-64. Within-cohort exit, where reforms closing exit-conditioned routes should act, does not converge; if anything it diverges. That undercuts the stated model link (Delta = m_w - m_e rising most where exit was widest). Caveat: a same-year age-gap version, d(A5564 - A4554) on gap09, does converge (-0.40, p 0.001), but that gap also mixes in cohort differences. It is one more fork, not a fix.; Survey breaks cannot be handled. 11 of the 16 explore countries carry a 'b' flag on these cells within 2009-2019: BE 2011/2017, BG 2010/2011/2019, CH 2010, FR 2014, HU 2018, IE 2017, IT 2018, LU 2009/2015, PL 2010/2019, PT 2011, SE 2018. C3 has nothing to act on in a two-point cross-section. Dropping those countries leaves G=5 (b +0.25), which is uninformative. Mitigating: all ten one-year changes from 2010 to 2019 correlate negatively with the 2009 level (-0.15 to -0.68), so this is not one break year. Still, a break just after 2009 acts as measurement error in x that enters y with the opposite sign. That biases beta down.; Model link is weak and not distinctive. The paper's model does not predict convergence. Convergence needs an auxiliary claim about where pension reforms landed, which the model does not supply (P7 was dropped for want of a pension table). The same sign follows from Gruber-Wise, life-cycle models, cohort growth in female participation, coordinated EU-wide policy targets, and plain bounded-rate dynamics. The prime-age placebo also converges: A2554 2009-19 gives -0.56 (p 0.06). A confirm-half pass is very likely under the standard account and would not discriminate. A fail would not refute the model either, since the model is silent on where reforms landed. Low evidential value per Holm slot.; Not a rescue of a failed P. No P concerns the 55-64 level. P7 was dropped before data, and C-2 (young minus old on q) is a different object. It is computable from the frozen manifest (act: lfsa_argan M/F 55-59, 60-64, NAT).
- Vetter's power note: The explore MDE on 15 clusters is 0.23 points per point (one-sided 5%, 80% power, t with 14 df, explore se 0.086, stratum FE). The explore estimate of -0.33 is above that, so nominal power is about 0.9. That power is fragile. With LU in at its 2011 level, se rises to 0.118 (MDE 0.31) and the estimate falls to -0.245, which puts power near 0.6. Across defensible windows the estimate ranges from -0.11 (2012-19) to -0.49 (2009-24). The confirm half has a singleton stratum, which costs a degree of freedom. It may also lose small countries to u-flagged cells, so G could fall below 15. The bigger issue is not power but informativeness: under a proportional-gap or cohort-replacement null the test is likely to pass anyway. My vet ran 33 specifications (21 + 5 + 7, explore half only; scripts in C:\Users\s6068C\GitHub\laborformal\participation\explore\_vet\ear1_vet.py, ear1_vet2.py, ear1_vet3.py).

### E-AR2 — dropped by the vetter (score 3/10)

The activity gap between 55-64 and 25-54 rises with house prices relative to the CPI more where a larger share of 55-64-year-olds own outright. This is the opposite of the net-long prediction.

- Model link: Both the model's Appendix B (net-long older owners gain A when q rises, so they work less) and the standard hedging account predict a NEGATIVE interaction. The explore half shows a positive one. Registered as a direct check of the net-long reading, with the sign the explore data suggest; a confirmation would count against the net-long auxiliary, not against the floor mechanism.
- Varies: none (C-1's secondary uses the same ownership moderator; C-2 uses an old-young gap with log q)
- Explore estimate: beta = +6.18, CR1 se = 2.28, t = 2.72, one-sided WCR p = 0.026 (B=9999), n = 223, G = 16, G* = 4.1. Rests on one country: dropping SE gives +5.9 with two-sided p 0.445. Country trends give +3.5 (two-sided p 0.15); CEE x year effects give +6.4 (p 0.21).
- Variants tried: 36
- Vetter's problems: The sign was chosen after seeing the result. s10_old_minus_prime.py states its prediction as '< 0' (net-long older owners). The explorer ran it two-sided, got +6.18 with two-sided p = 0.053, and registered the observed sign. The quoted one-sided p = 0.026 is that two-sided result halved after the fact.; The outcome was also chosen after seeing results. s04 first ran the ownership interaction on levels: A5564 x hpi gave +6.7 (two-sided p 0.12), AW6064 +7.5 (p 0.019), AM6064 +8.9 (p 0.18), and the rent versions 11.7 / 5.5 / 16.8. s05 then tested robustness on the levels, and only after that did s10 switch to the old-minus-prime gap. That is 36 logged variants across s04, s05, s10 and s11, with 2 q measures x 4 outcomes in the interaction alone. The selected cell is the second-best of about 8 interaction cells. Given that search, a two-sided p of 0.05 is what noise would produce.; The prime-age term adds nothing. Prime-age activity on the interaction gives +0.69 (one-sided p 0.62 in the predicted negative direction). A5564 alone gives +6.67, the same estimate as the gap. The 'differencing out common country-year shocks' argument therefore does no work, and the hypothesis is really about older activity levels, which are dominated by pension reforms (derivations.md lists 'pension reforms ... controlled for 55-64' as an untestable requirement).; It rests on one country (my vet, ownership re-standardised on each 15-country sample, one-sided WCR). Dropping SE gives b = 5.24, t = 0.97, p = 0.215, while G* rises from 4.1 to 7.3: SE is the high-leverage cluster. Dropping both low-ownership countries (SE and CH) gives b = 3.48, p = 0.23. The other 14 leave-one-out runs keep p at or below 0.065. Under PROTOCOL section 5 even a confirm-half pass could carry 'rests on one country' if the confirm half has a similar low-ownership, high-house-price-growth anchor.; The lead placebo fails. On the matched sample (n = 163, 2006-2023) the lagged interaction alone is +6.59 (p 0.025). With the lead interaction (log(HPI/CPI) at t+1 x ownership) added, the lag falls to +0.71 (p 0.45) and the lead takes +6.87 (p 0.088). The lead alone is +6.52. Future house prices 'predict' today's gap as well as past prices do. This looks like a differential trend (CEE convergence: real house-price growth alongside pension-reform-driven rises in older activity), not a response to prices. The two interactions are collinear (raw correlation 0.83), so this is not decisive, but nothing favours the timed reading.; Not robust to the protocol's own standard robustness runs. Excluding 2008-2013: +3.18 (p 0.16). Country-specific linear trends: +3.48 (p 0.072). CEE x year effects: +6.4 (two-sided p 0.21). Non-CEE countries only (G = 10): +4.0 (p 0.22). The moderator is effectively 'CEE plus a few against CH and SE': CEE outright ownership runs 81-92%, CH 5%, SE 14%. With a CEE dummy as the moderator: +7.7 (p 0.098).; Not house-price-specific. The rent-q version (log CP041/IGD_NNRG x ownership) is also positive (+10.6, p 0.064). That undercuts the reading that the effect runs through capitalised housing wealth.; Model link: a positive interaction contradicts both the paper's Appendix B net-long reading and the standard hedging account (Disney-Gathergood type wealth effects). The wage pass-through term in KEY (e) is also smaller for outright owners, so every channel in derivations.md predicts a negative or zero interaction. A confirmed positive sign would therefore count against the net-long auxiliary and against standard models equally; it does not discriminate between them, and derivations.md already rules the net-long sign 'not a test of this model'. Its information value for the paper is low.; The moderator is not predetermined. ilc_lvps05 OWN_NL 55-64 is averaged over 2015-17, inside the sample window (2006-2025). It is computable from the manifest (the tenure query), so this is not fatal, but the same weakness is already flagged for C-1's secondary.; Power claim misstated, and in the confirm half power is likely worse. See power_note.
- Vetter's power note: The explore-half CR1 se is 2.28. On 15 clusters (t with 14 df) that gives an MDE of (1.761 + 0.868) x 2.28 = 6.0, which is what the explorer reported. But the explorer's 'about 50% power' is wrong at alpha 0.05: if the true effect equalled 6.18, power at 0.05 would be about 80%. 50% applies only at Holm's strictest step with 5 E (alpha 0.01, t crit 2.62). The 6.18 is the best of 36 variants and is inflated. The robustness runs cluster at 3.2-3.5 (excluding 2008-13, country trends, dropping SE and CH). At a true effect of about 3.3, power is roughly 30% at 0.05 and about 10% at 0.01. G* is about 4, so the wild bootstrap is conservative relative to the t approximation, which lowers realised power further. The confirm-half se depends on whether the confirm half has a low-ownership country with large real house-price swings (SE's role here), and that cannot be checked before the look. Realistic chance of a supported verdict: 10-30%. A null would be uninformative, and a pass would likely carry 'rests on one country'.
