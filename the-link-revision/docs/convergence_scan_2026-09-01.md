# Convergence scan — who else is arriving at this theory (2026-09-01)

Four parallel web sweeps (formal economics; land/housing/Georgist; AI-forecasting
gray literature; input-output/physical-constraints), ~77 searches and ~40 page
fetches total. Every item below was verified against a live page or search
snippet during the scan — nothing is cited from model memory. Caveats at the
bottom. Ratings are against the paper's actual mechanism — the two closures
(machine-cost recursion → terminal rents; exit priced off the same rents), the
fork, and the Georgist pair — not against "AI and wages" generally.

## Verdict

The **endpoint** is getting crowded fast: "wages get pinned by machine costs,
the value migrates to whatever stays scarce, and the fix is fiscal" is now
stated independently by Restrepo, Korinek–Lockwood, Trammell, the Epoch/
Mechanize cluster, and a wave of serious essays. The **routes** remain
unoccupied: all four sweeps independently confirm that nobody closes the
machine rental through its own production recursion into non-produced-input
rents, nobody prices the outside option off those same rents inside a wage
model, nobody computes the durables-vs-shelter fork, nobody states the clean
non-produced-rent-tax + uniform-transfer pair formally, and no full-chain
labor-content time series runs past ~2016.

## Tier 1 — closest neighbors (position against these explicitly)

1. **Restrepo, "We Won't Be Missed: Work and Growth in the AGI World"**
   (NBER WP 34423, Oct 2025; chapter in the NBER *Economics of Transformative
   AI* volume). Post-AGI, wages converge to the opportunity cost of the
   compute needed to reproduce human work; labor share → 0; residual
   non-compute income accrues to "land and other scarce factors."
   *The single closest formal paper: it is w = c·γ(x*) with c priced at
   compute's opportunity cost. Our Contribution 1 is what happens underneath
   his c — the recursion that resolves the machine's own replacement cost into
   terminal rents. The wage-side engagement paragraph should name this paper.*
   https://www.nber.org/papers/w34423

2. **Korinek & Lockwood, "Public Finance in the Age of AI: A Primer"**
   (NBER WP 34873, Feb 2026; Brookings version; same NBER volume). As AI
   erodes the labor-income base, taxing rents on fixed, non-produced factors —
   unimproved land named explicitly, alongside spectrum, orbital slots,
   rare-earth deposits, unique datasets — becomes central, on the standard
   no-efficiency-cost grounds; their menu also includes produced-input taxes
   (robots, compute, tokens), sovereign wealth funds, windfall clauses.
   *The closest fiscal-side convergence, from the optimal-tax direction. Ours
   is sharper (pure rents on non-produced factors only + uniform transfer,
   exact in the limit); theirs is broader and hedged. §9/App C should cite it.*
   https://www.nber.org/papers/w34873

3. **Trammell & Korinek, "Economic Growth under Transformative AI"**
   (Annual Review of Economics 2026; NBER WP 31815). Survey: under full
   automation wages "may rise or fall, depending on the returns to scale, the
   importance of natural resources, tastes, and the direction of technical
   change." *The survey that names our slot as an open contingency without
   filling it — the natural framing citation for the contribution claim.*
   https://www.nber.org/papers/w31815

4. **Trammell & Patel, "Capital in the 22nd Century"** (Substack long-form,
   Dec 2025; widely discussed). Full automation breaks the mechanism that
   forced wages up; a global progressive **capital** tax is "essentially the
   only way" to check inequality. Concedes natural-resource taxation "avoids
   both costs" but dismisses it because today's resource share is ~5%.
   *The direct interlocutor on the tax base. Our recursion is precisely the
   argument that the ~5% is endogenous — the non-produced share of replacement
   cost grows as λ falls — i.e., the reply to their dismissal.*
   https://philiptrammell.substack.com/p/capital-in-the-22nd-century

5. **Barnett, "AGI Could Drive Wages Below Subsistence Level"** (Epoch AI
   Gradient Updates, Jan 2025). Cobb-Douglas with land: AGI labor scales,
   fixed factors (land, energy) bind, marginal product of labor falls below
   subsistence; ~1/3 probability within 20 years. Companion: **Barnett,
   Besiroglu & Erdil, "Life after work"** (Mechanize, Oct 2025) — wages
   collapse under full automation; humans prosper via investment returns,
   property rents, transfers. *The gray-literature statement of the wage-side
   consequence; no recursion, no exit price, no incidence of which property.*
   https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/agi-could-drive-wages-below-subsistence-level
   https://www.mechanize.work/blog/life-after-work/

6. **Penrose, "Housing Costs, Parental Resources, and Declining Male Labor
   Force Participation"** (SSRN 6429759, Mar 2026). The parental home as a
   consumption floor pricing the return to work; rising rents (supply-
   constraint instrumented) push low-earning men into coresidence and out of
   participation; decomposes six decades of male LFP decline.
   *The closest anything comes to Contribution 2 — and it is literally the
   dependency floor's "parent's spare room," quantified. Ammunition for §5–§6
   (cite), and a flag that the exit-pricing territory is being approached
   empirically.* https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6429759

7. **Hirano & Toda, "Unbalanced Growth and Land Overvaluation"** (PNAS 122(14),
   Apr 2025). OLG model: unbalanced technical progress + limited factor
   substitutability makes land prices grow faster than rents and GDP — growth
   absorbed into land values, with a bubble component (prices outrun even
   rents). *Formal macro convergence on land-as-the-sink, from the asset-
   pricing side; no wage margin. Worth citing beside Rognlie/Knoll et al.*
   https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2423295122

## Tier 2 — converging on pieces

- **Korinek & Suh** (NBER w32255, already cited in the paper) — "bottlenecks
  to growth from irreproducible scarce factors may exacerbate the decline in
  wages"; Korinek's policy sequels w32980 (2024), w30172 (2022, machine-cost
  subsistence benchmark).
- **NBER volume chapter c15298, "The Economics of Superabundant AI: Autonomy,
  Scarcity, and the Future of Work"** — rents shift to the scarce complement
  ("slots"/opportunities) when compute is abundant. **Attribution conflict:
  one sweep reports Garicano, another Ide & Talamàs — verify authorship
  before citing.** https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c15298/c15298.pdf
- **Growiec (CEPR 2024) & Growiec–Prettner (arXiv 2503.07341)** — hardware/
  software framework; machines self-replicating on both margins make labor
  inessential and crush the labor share. The cleanest machines-making-machines
  macro; no rent resolution.
- **Caballero, "Speculative Growth and the AI 'Bubble'"** (MIT WP 2026) —
  deployment-capacity constraint earns quasi-rents (our shorter-horizon
  terminal inputs, in asset-pricing form).
- **Catalini, Hui & Wu, "Some Simple Economics of AGI"** (arXiv 2602.20946) —
  rents migrate to the non-automatable bottleneck (verification).
- **Papadogiannis, "Scarcity in an Age of AI Abundance"** (SSRN) — rents
  migrate to five persistent scarcity layers incl. space, energy, fabs,
  jurisdictions; conceptual only.
- **Drago & Laine, "The Intelligence Curse"** (essay series, 2025) + **Laine,
  "By default, capital will matter more than ever after AGI"** (LessWrong,
  Dec 2024) + **Kulveit, Duvenaud et al., "Gradual Disempowerment"** (arXiv
  2501.16946) — the political economy of the same endpoint: labor loses
  leverage, states turn rentier. No factor-price model.
- **Krier, "The Cyborg Era"** (essay, Jan 2026) — names both halves in prose:
  scarce natural inputs bid up until ordinary life is unaffordable, and
  taxing those bottlenecked inputs as the remedy.
- **Doucet, "Is AI like Petroleum, or like Pens?"** (Center for Land
  Economics, Aug 2026) — competition commoditizes labs and hardware, gains
  are "sucked into the price of land," LVT as capture. The verbal version of
  the recursion's conclusion.
- **Cowen, "How AI Will Make Land More Valuable"** (Bloomberg, 2023);
  **Ozimek** (The Dispatch, Aug 2026) — mainstream fixed-factor revaluation
  in public prose.
- **Hanson** ("AI is Acceleration," Feb 2026 + standing em-economy work) —
  wages fall to machine subsistence; humans live off assets.
- Fiscal-adjacent with the wrong base (equity/tokens/profits, not rents):
  **Nayebi** (arXiv 2505.18687, rent-funded-UBI threshold), **Irwin–Wu–Barez
  token taxes** (arXiv 2603.04555), **Windfall Trust / Yelizarova** (2025),
  **Sanders AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act** (June 2026), **O'Keefe et al.
  Windfall Clause** (2020), **Altman, "Moore's Law for Everything"** (2021 —
  companies + land, uniform transfer; the closest published fiscal design).
- **Convergence Analysis, "Funding Government in the Age of AI"** — ranks LVT
  most promising post-TAI revenue base.
- Comparative-advantage debate: **Noah Smith** (2024/2026) vs **Newman**
  ("I Don't See How Comparative Advantage Applies…") and **Tabarrok** — the
  hinge of the public debate is whether scarce inputs price humans out of
  their own subsistence bundle; Smith's own concession paragraph states the
  energy-bid-up case.

## Ammunition (supports to consider citing, not threats)

- **Heldring, Robinson & Vollmer** (NBER w29772, 2024 rev., R&R ReStud) —
  causal evidence English enclosure raised yields/inequality and released
  labor to industrializing regions. Direct support for §5's enclosure reading.
- **Acolin & Wachter** (Real Estate Economics 2026) — affordability the
  largest market driver of 2000–2021 coresidence rise.
- **Rotta & Kumar** (SCED 2024) — vertically integrated labor-content
  measurement from world IO tables (stops 2014): the D-Q construction's
  nearest published relative; lineage citation for the effort empirics.
- Datacenter/energy/chip scarcity empirics for §11's balance-sheet claim:
  **Alvarez–Argente–Chow–Van Patten** (NBER w35194, datacenters and local
  land/grid demand); **Kay–Reaser–Taylor** (Dallas Fed 2606: wholesale power
  +3–5% already, ~+50% possible by 2028); **IMF "Power Hungry"** (WP/25/081);
  **LBNL "Queued Up 2025"** (~2,290 GW waiting, 55-month median);
  **CNAS chip-fab bottleneck report** (May 2026); Lincoln Institute "Data
  Drain"; CBRE-type rent data (datacenter rents +9% 2025, 1.6% vacancy).
  Investor notes on model-weight commoditization corroborate "weights are
  ideas, the durable items are sites/power/water."
- Fork-adjacent measurement: **FRED Blog** deflator-choice piece (2023);
  **BIS WP 1179** (producer- vs consumer-deflated wages); **Cost-of-Thriving
  debate** (Cass vs Winship–Horpedahl); **Fudge** UK housing-wedge (SSRN).
- **Bouscasse et al.** already in the refs; nothing newer displaces it.

## Rivals and counter-evidence to engage

- **Watten, Bistline & Blanford** (arXiv 2606.19777) — IV evidence datacenters
  *lowered* retail electricity rates 2015–2024 by spreading fixed grid costs
  (their caveat: future supply constraints could reverse). The sharpest
  counter-evidence for the energy-as-terminal-input-over-horizons claim;
  worth a sentence.
- **Klein & Thompson, "Abundance"** (2025) and **Caplan, "Build, Baby,
  Build"** (2024) — the same fork observable, attributed to elective
  regulatory scarcity, curable by building. The paper's zoning-rival
  paragraph now has named, prominent opponents.
- **Watling, "The Failure of the Land Value Tax"** (Works in Progress, 2025;
  + Doucet's reply) — the administrability objection the fiscal section will
  face.
- Bottleneck-floor optimists: **Jones "weak links"** (NBER w34779);
  **Bara** (arXiv 2509.24466 — unautomatable sensorimotor tasks as fixed
  factors giving a positive labor-share floor); **Aghion–Jones–Jones**
  updates (2025/2026 versions circulating) — Baumol-style floors via residual
  human tasks rather than non-produced inputs.
- **Acemoglu & Johnson, "Learning from Ricardo and Thompson"** (ARE 2024) —
  institutional reading of the machinery question; different remedy space.

## The unoccupied ground (all four sweeps agree)

1. **The machine-cost recursion.** No paper endogenizes the machine rental
   via an input-output/Sraffian unit-cost recursion inside a task model and
   resolves it into non-produced-input rents. Nearest: Restrepo's compute
   opportunity cost; Dutta's Leontief–Sraffa unification (arXiv 2506.13936,
   no automation content). No Sraffian-journal AI work at all, 2023–2026.
2. **The exit price.** No formal model prices the outside option off
   housing/energy rents. Nearest: Penrose (participation empirics), Smith's
   concession paragraph.
3. **The fork as constructed.** Nobody computes wages deflated by durables
   vs shelter; the neighbors mix housing with health/education or use whole
   indices.
4. **The clean Georgist pair.** Not stated formally in the AI literature;
   Korinek–Lockwood is the hedged, menu-form neighbor.
5. **The full-chain labor-content series through the 2020s.** Published
   vertically-integrated measurement stops ~2014–2016; the D-F/D-Q
   construction appears to have no direct competitor.
