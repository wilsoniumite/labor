# SCF summary-extract variables kept in the tidy files

Files: `scf2022_summary.csv.gz` (SCF 2022, 22,975 records = 4,595 families x 5 implicates) and `scf2025_summary.csv.gz` (SCF 2025, 21,800 records = 4,360 families x 5 implicates). Values are written exactly as published (text copied, no float round trip); `IMPLICATE` is the only added column.

Generated by `consumption/code/acquire_fed.py` from the vendored raw files. Definitions below are close paraphrases of the Federal Reserve's summary-extract program `bulletin.macro.txt` (raw/fed/scf/, the 'SAS macro - Variable Definitions' linked from the SCF 2022 page; this is the version re-posted with the 2025 survey on 2026-10-09). Under each definition the macro's own comment and defining statements are quoted with line numbers, each tagged with whether it applies to the 2022 and 2025 public data (the %IF chain is evaluated for YEAR=2022/2025, PUBLIC=YES, REAL=YES, ADJINC=YES; '?' would mark an unparsed condition). X-variable texts are in `codebk2022.txt` / `codebk2025.txt`.

## Conventions that apply to every variable

* **Dollars.** Every dollar variable in BOTH files is in **2025 dollars**. The posted extracts are the macro's REAL=YES run with CPIBASE=4789 (September 2025 R-CPI-U-RS x 100). For 2022 every dollar value was multiplied by CPIADJ = 4789/4376 = 1.094378 (4376 = September 2022 R-CPI-U-RS x 100); for 2025 CPIADJ = 1. To restate the 2022 file in 2022 dollars (the 2023 Bulletin's units) divide by 1.094378. Dollar values of exactly -1 or -2 are left unadjusted by the macro.
* **Income timing.** Income variables refer to the calendar year before the survey (2021 for the 2022 survey, 2024 for 2025) and are first moved to survey-year dollars by CPILAG (2022: 4315/3992 = 1.080912; 2025: 4750/4625 = 1.027027), then by CPIADJ. Nominal 2021 income = 2022-file value / (1.080912 x 1.094378).
* **Weights and implicates.** `WGT = X42001/5`. Summed over ALL records (all five implicates) it gives the number of families (131.31 million in 2022, 134.96 million in 2025); each implicate sums to one fifth. The full public data's X42001 sums to five times the number of families. Weighted statistics are computed on all records pooled with WGT (this reproduces the Fed's public-data tables exactly); standard errors need the replicate weights and the multiple-imputation formula (not done here).
* **Percentile categories** (INCCAT, NINCCAT, ...) are weighted percentiles over all records pooled, so a family's category can differ across its implicates.
* **Unit of observation** is the primary economic unit (PEU): the economically dominant single person or couple and everyone in the household financially interdependent with them (codebook, 'Unit of analysis').
* **Known publisher-side defect:** in the 2022 file `NHNFIN` is multiplied by CPIADJ twice (it is listed twice in the macro's `ARRAY VALUE`), so NHNFIN = (NFIN - HOUSES) x 1.0943784 on every record. Use NFIN - HOUSES. The 2025 file is unaffected (CPIADJ = 1).
* **2025-only items.** VACATION, TOTSPENDNH, MPSPEND, MPSAVE, MPDEBT were first asked in 2025; the 2022 extract carries them as zero placeholders, so they are dropped from `scf2022_summary.csv.gz` and kept in `scf2025_summary.csv.gz`.

## Definitions with the macro text

### YY1

*id; unit: id.* Public-data family (case) identifier; identical across the family's five implicate records.

Macro: `%IF (&PUBLIC NE YES OR &YEAR EQ 1989) %THEN %DO; %LET ID=X1; %LET IID=XX1; %END; %ELSE %DO; %LET ID=Y1; %LET IID=YY1; %END;` (public data use Y1/YY1).

### Y1

*id; unit: id.* Public-data record identifier = 10*YY1 + implicate number (1-5).

Macro: `%IF (&PUBLIC NE YES OR &YEAR EQ 1989) %THEN %DO; %LET ID=X1; %LET IID=XX1; %END; %ELSE %DO; %LET ID=Y1; %LET IID=YY1; %END;` (public data use Y1/YY1).

### IMPLICATE

*id; unit: id.* Implicate number 1-5, computed here as Y1 - 10*YY1 (not a published column).

Not in the macro: added by acquire_fed.py as Y1 - 10*YY1.

### WGT

*id; unit: families (weight).* Analysis weight as published: WGT = X42001/5 (macro: 'divide weight by 5 so totals estimated on the 5 implicates jointly are correct'). Summed over all records (all five implicates) it gives the number of families; each implicate sums to one fifth of that. The weight differs across a family's implicates.

Macro comment: “divide weight by 5 so totals estimated on the 5 implicates * jointly are correct”
