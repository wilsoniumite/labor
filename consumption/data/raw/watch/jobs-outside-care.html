Private jobs outside care

Normal Direct evidence

Private employers outside health care and social assistance added 192 thousand jobs over the twelve months to Sep 2026. Health care and social assistance added 520 thousand jobs, and government jobs fell by 216 thousand.

The change over twelve months is lower than 70% of the readings since 1991.

US recessions Periods when this signal was on

What it measures

US private jobs added over twelve months outside health care and social assistance.

Why it matters

Health care and social assistance have accounted for most US job growth. They are mostly paid for by government, so in a downturn public budgets decide whether they keep hiring.

When it triggers

Triggers when private employers outside health care and social assistance have no more jobs than a year earlier while health care and social assistance are still adding jobs.

Where it fits

What happens to people who lose their jobs. The argument predicts: Employment among 25–54-year-olds falls while their unemployment rate stays level, and health care and social assistance keep hiring while private jobs outside them stop growing.

Other possible causes. Recoveries in which hiring lags, an ageing population, or government hiring and job cuts.

If our view is right, the number of private jobs added outside health care and social assistance over twelve months reaches zero or below by Sep 2027, the threshold at which its rule triggers. The date is a year after the start, the span the rule measures. The prediction is met only when the rule triggers, which also needs health care and social assistance to have added jobs over the same twelve months. Reaching it counts as evidence only if no US recession signal was on during the period it measures, or in the three months after, and the prices US businesses charge were not rising faster than in 95% of quarters from 1990 to 2019.

If it is at 846 thousand or above on two consecutive readings, that counts against our view. That level is the start, 192 thousand, raised by 654 thousand, the median size of its change over twelve months (the span its rule measures) from 1990 to 2019. The deadline refers to the period the data cover. If it passes without a reading of zero or below, that counts somewhat against our view.

When the rule was set

The rule was set on 8 Oct 2026, when it was not triggered.

How often it has been on

Applied to past data at the end of each month from Apr 1991, using only what had been published by then, it was on in 24% of months to 2019 and 24% since 2020. More than six months from any US recession as dated by the National Bureau of Economic Research, it triggered once, in Nov 2024. The rule was set with these years in view, so these figures cannot show how well it predicts.

Past triggers

On the data published at the time, it triggered in May 2001, Mar 2008, Apr 2020 and Nov 2024.
