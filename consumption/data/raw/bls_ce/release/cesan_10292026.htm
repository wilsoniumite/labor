Consumer Expenditures News Release

For release 10:00 a.m.  (ET) Thursday, October 29, 2026                     USDL-26-1619

Technical information:  (202) 691-6900  *  CEXInfo@bls.gov  *  www.bls.gov/cex
Media contact:          (202) 691-5902  *  PressOffice@bls.gov


				Consumer Expenditures--2025
					

Average annual expenditures for all consumer units in 2025 were $81,905, a 4.3-percent 
increase from 2024, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported today. (See table 1.) 
Average income before taxes was $110,847 in 2025, a 6.4-percent increase from 2024. During 
the same period, the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) rose 2.6 percent.

Focusing on statistically significant changes from 2024 to 2025 in the major components of 
total expenditures, average expenditures increased for food and housing. (See table 1.) The 
4.6-percent increase in the average annual expenditures on food is due in part to an 
11.7-percent increase in expenditures on meats, poultry, fish, and eggs. Average annual 
expenditures on housing increased 5.8 percent in 2025, mostly due to an 8.0-percent increase 
in expenditures on shelter.

On average, consumer units spent $27,790 on housing in 2025, which was 33.9 percent of total 
average annual expenditures. The next largest shares of total expenditures were transportation 
(16.5 percent), food (13.0 percent), and personal insurance and pensions (12.4 percent). 
(See table 2.)

In 2025, average annual expenditures ranged from $37,935 for consumer units in the lowest 
income quintile to $159,512 for consumer units in the highest income quintile, while average 
income before taxes ranged from $18,125 to $286,188, respectively, for these income quintiles. 
(See table 3.) For information about how income quintiles are constructed, see the technical 
notes section. 

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                Lapse in Federal Appropriations

Due to the 2025 lapse in federal appropriations, the Census Bureau could not collect the Consumer 
Expenditure Surveys (CE) for the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) in October and November 2025. BLS used 
a weight adjustment approach to account for these missing 2025 data. The Census Bureau resumed collection of 
CE data on December 5, 2025.

Additional information is available online at www.bls.gov/cex/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-ce.htm.

Additional Information

Data Products

In addition to expenditures, BLS also produces CE data on income, demographics, assets, and liabilities. Published 
tables with more expenditure detail than are presented here, as well as tables showing annual aggregate expenditures 
(total dollars spent in the economy) for various items, are available at www.bls.gov/cex/tables.htm. These tables 
provide 2025 CE data for all consumer units and by the following classifications: income before taxes; quintiles of 
income before taxes; deciles of income before taxes; region of residence; population size of area; type of area of 
consumer unit (e.g., urban or rural); composition of consumer unit (e.g., married couples, married couples with 
children, etc.); highest education level of any consumer unit member; housing tenure of consumer unit (e.g., owner, 
renter, etc.); number of earners in consumer unit; size of consumer unit; military status within consumer unit; age 
of reference person; generation of reference person; selected age ranges of reference person; Hispanic or Latino 
origin of reference person; occupation of reference person; and race of reference person. These tables include 
expenditure means, shares of total expenditures, standard errors, relative standard errors, and percent reporting. 
A table showing results for all consumer units including the most detailed breakdown of expenditures is available at 
www.bls.gov/cex/tables/top-line-means.htm. Annual historical published tables dating back to 1984 are also available 
at www.bls.gov/cex/tables/calendar-year/mean-item-share-average-standard-error.htm.

To aid in the interpretation of CE tables, the CE website includes the Tables Getting Started Guide, available at 
www.bls.gov/cex/tables-getting-started-guide.htm. The guide includes an overview of tables, concepts, and other 
information to help data users.

The CE database, which provides tools to access CE estimates, is available at www.bls.gov/cex/data.htm. The database 
includes estimates of annual means from 1984 forward. It also includes estimates of standard errors, relative 
standard errors, shares of total expenditures, and percent reporting from 2010-2025, and aggregate expenditures and 
their shares from 2011-2025. Detailed level estimates are also available from 2010-2025 for the classifications 
mentioned above and for all consumer units. Documentation on how to use the CE database is available at 
www.bls.gov/cex/labstat/ce-labstat-getting-started-guide.htm.

Additionally, CE public use microdata (PUMD) for 1980 through 2025 are available at www.bls.gov/cex/pumd_data.htm. 
The PUMD comprise the Interview Survey files, which include paradata (information about the data collection process), 
and the Diary Survey files. Documentation of the PUMD, its conventions, files, sample code, and methodology can be 
found at www.bls.gov/cex/pumd-getting-started-guide.htm.


New Product for the 2025 Data Release

The 2025 data release will include a table showing military status within a consumer unit. These data were previously 
available as a research product. The table includes three main categories: all consumer units, military consumer units, 
and non-military consumer units. Military consumer units comprise three groups: all veteran and/or active duty, veteran 
only, and active duty only. Military consumer units with both types of members (at least one veteran and at least one 
active duty member) are included in the first military consumer unit group; however, they are not listed as a separate 
group due to small sample size. In addition, data from this table are available in the CE database starting with the 2025 
data release. The CE population excludes, among others, those living on military bases.


Publications

BLS publishes additional information on the CE related to survey data, trends, analysis, and methods. These 
publications are available at www.bls.gov/cex/csxreport.htm. Additional CE program publications, documents, and 
presentations are available via the CE Library at www.bls.gov/cex/research_papers/research-paper-catalog.htm. 
Information on the methodology used to calculate and collect CE data is available at www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cex/home.htm. 

The 2025 Data Quality Profile, which reports quality metrics and indicators for the Interview and Diary Surveys 
regarding measurement, nonresponse, and processing error, will be available at www.bls.gov/cex/cecomparison.htm 
shortly after publication of this release.

The 2025 Annual Report, which includes more detailed information on spending patterns, is planned for publication 
in 2027. The 2024 Annual Report, published in August 2026, is available at 
www.bls.gov/opub/reports/consumer-expenditures/2024/home.htm.


Technical Notes

Terminology. For more information on how terms used in this release are defined (such as consumer units, reference 
person, owned dwellings, etc.), see the CE glossary at www.bls.gov/cex/csxgloss.htm.

Consumer Price Index Citation. The change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) cited in the 
text was calculated as the percentage change between the 2024 annual average CPI-U for all items (313.689) and the 
2025 annual average CPI-U for all items (321.943). For 2025, the annual average is calculated using 11 months of 
data since the October 2025 CPI-U is unavailable due to the lapse in federal appropriations. These data were 
obtained from the CPI databases, which can be found at data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SA0. BLS does not test this 
change for statistical significance.

Two-year Tables. The 2024-2025 tabular estimates ('Two-year means tables by geographic areas' and 'Two-year means 
cross-tabulated tables') will not be published with the 2025 annual release of CE data. BLS used the standard 
Balanced Repeated Replication (BRR) method for estimating standard errors prior to the 2025 annual release. BLS 
will calculate standard errors using Fay's method, a variant of BRR, with the 2025 annual release. This new method 
will produce more robust variance measures. Due to the difference in variance estimation methodology across the two 
years, BLS will not produce 2024-2025 two-year tabular estimates. For more information, see 
www.bls.gov/cex/ce-standarderrors.htm.

Significance Testing. Significance tests for differences in means shown in Table 1 were performed using t-tests. 
Differences denoted with an asterisk are statistically significant at the 95 percent confidence level due to their 
t-values being 2.02 or greater in absolute value, while differences without an asterisk are not statistically 
significant due to their t-values being less than 2.02 in absolute value. While the asterisks in Table 1 are shown 
next to percent changes, it is the differences in dollar amounts that were tested for statistical significance. The 
standard errors used in the t-tests were computed using Fay's method of BRR with 44 degrees of freedom.

Components of Income Before Taxes. Income before taxes in the CE includes the following components: wages and salaries; 
self-employment income; Social Security; private and government retirement; interest and dividends; rental and other 
property income; unemployment, workers' compensation, and veterans' benefits; public assistance, supplemental security 
income, and food stamps; regular contributions for support; and other income. Not included in these estimates are 
government subsidies for businesses, as these are out of scope for the CE.

Income Quintiles. Every year, BLS constructs income quintiles by sorting consumer units in the sample from lowest to 
highest income before taxes. Because there were approximately 138 million consumer units in the population in 2025, 
each quintile includes about 28 million consumer units. However, the counts are not identical across quintiles because 
the cumulative frequency count is performed within each quarter of the year, not for the year as a whole. In the 2025 
quintiles of income before taxes table, the lower income bounds were $32,623 for the second quintile, $60,056 for the 
third quintile, $99,007 for the fourth quintile, and $160,635 for the highest quintile.

Geographic Revisions. Every 10 years, BLS updates the geographic areas in the CE sample based on the results of the 
latest U.S. census. The most recent update occurred in 2025. This means some Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSAs) 
(or other geographic areas) that were in the sample in 2024 are no longer included, while other MSAs that were not 
in the sample in 2024 are now included. These updates to the sample affect the weighting of the data and the statistics 
derived from them, such as mean estimates and standard errors.
