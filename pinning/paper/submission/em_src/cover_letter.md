Dear Guest Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "Pinning the Wage to Scarcity and Technology: Replacement, exit, and the rents of non-produced inputs in a task economy" for the Economic Modelling special issue "Artificial Intelligence, Data Investment and the Digital Economy".

The paper asks what automation, including artificial intelligence, does to wages and to what wages can buy. We combine task assignment with a production network, so that the machines that replace labor are themselves produced with labor, machine services, and non-produced inputs such as land. This separates two automation channels: better machine capability at tasks, and less labor in producing machine services. Both can lower wages relative to rents on non-produced inputs. At an interior task margin, we derive bounds on wage purchasing power, with embodied rent costs as a ceiling, and we connect participation to the cost of the living basket and to household or public support. In U.S. data, wages deflated by durables and by shelter prices diverged by a factor of 4.8 over 1964–2024, and full capture of measured site rents would fund about 30 percent of a per-person subsistence bundle in 2025.

The paper speaks to the call's topics on AI and automation, employment, and factor income, and on generative AI and the labor market. AI can move both automation channels at once: models become effective substitutes at tasks, while software generation, chip design, and datacenter operation reduce the labor in the machine-service supply chain. The paper traces how such changes pass through to wages, rents, and participation, and why the scarce inputs of the AI buildout, such as sites, grid access, and mineral deposits, matter for what wages can buy. It also measures the U.S. quantities the argument turns on.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and both authors have approved its submission. A preprint is available on SSRN. The use of generative AI tools is declared in the manuscript, as the journal requires.
