# Final empirical figure captions — freeze candidate

Date: 2026-08-31

## Figure 3 — Real-wage fork
U.S. average hourly earnings of production and nonsupervisory employees, deflated separately by the durables CPI and shelter CPI and indexed to 1964. Retained from the manuscript's existing empirical design. Interpretation is descriptive: the figure documents a deflator-dependent wage fork rather than identifying its causal source.

## C1 — PCE product composition
Shares of U.S. personal consumption expenditures by broad product family, 1950–2025. Exact current-vintage NIPA partition of 100% of PCE.

## D-Q — Production labor content
Full-chain human-effort content of U.S. consumption, 1950–2025. The solid 1997–2023 segment is the BEA full-chain benchmark. Dashed outer segments and the shaded band are product-composition extensions and are not measured historical factor content.

## S1 — Owner-housing land/site stock
Land/site residual as a share of owner-occupied residential property value, 1950–2025, calculated as matched real-estate market value less matched current-cost residential structures. This is a stock decomposition, not annual rent flow.

## D-F — Labor-origin financing
Labor-origin financing of U.S. PCE. The solid 2004–2023 segment uses actual annual BEA source distributions under the same equivalized-DPI ranking as the joint PCE/DPI distribution. The shaded interval leaves personal-tax source incidence and within-decile spending-source allocation partially unidentified. The central line uses proportional personal-tax source allocation. Dashed outer segments are weaker long-run extensions.

## D-F/Q — Two labor linkages
Labor-origin financing versus full-chain labor content of consumed production. D-F and D-Q answer different accounting questions and are not additive. The figure is intended to show that financing can remain comparatively labor-linked even when the production chain contains substantially less labor.
